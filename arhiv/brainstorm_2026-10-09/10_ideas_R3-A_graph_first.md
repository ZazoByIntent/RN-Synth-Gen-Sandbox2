# R3-A Graph-first: ideas (brainstormer R3-A, 2026-10-09)

**Shared public structure (used by A1-A3; frozen by commit hash with the map before any u182 run, S1, F8).**
- Hierarchy H: a contraction hierarchy (CH; Geisberger et al. 2008) on the frozen OSM Beijing graph, built on free-flow time (OSM `maxspeed`, class defaults) with a deterministic node-ordering heuristic (edge difference + contracted neighbours, ties by node id). Node rank → 4 bands by public rank quantiles: B0 (bottom 80 %), B1 (15 %), B2 (4 %), B3 (top 1 %). An edge's band = the lower band of its two end nodes, so crossing a big road at a junction is not "using" it. Ablations with the same role: Gutman's reach (2004) banded in seconds (reads directly: a node of reach r is needed only by trips whose both ends are farther than r from it) and plain OSM classes.
- Domain reduction: 35,764 nodes and their edges become 4 bands; a route becomes its band sequence plus public free-flow times; the free-flow cost-to-go h_D(v) to each queried destination D comes from one reverse Dijkstra (P3). No node, edge, zone, corridor or place ever enters a report; every reported value is a function of one trip's band sequence, public times and the trip's own timestamps.
- Common rules: every train user sends exactly one report (P11), ⊥ is in every domain; the trip used is drawn uniformly among the user's eligible trips (eligibility = a public rule on one trip, including an artefact guard: at most 30 % of the route length farther than 50 m from any GPS point, so matcher gap-fills cannot dominate, X13; loops, i.e. straight O-D distance < 0.3 × route length, are ⊥ unless P10 splits sessions); full ε, no split; F7 gate (keep the prior unless a pre-registered test of the raw report histogram against the prior-predicted one rejects). Departure time and the base time model come from P3 (public diurnal profile in UTC+8, free-flow × public factor × AR(1) jitter + public turn delay) unless an idea says otherwise. Each idea is judged against its own prior arm and the X7 control arms (lifted `ldptrace`, `rn_ldp_synth`).
- Shared prerequisites: P0, P1, P3, P4, P5, P10, P11; P2 + P12 for evaluation; P6 for large n. New public build step: the CH and its bands (minutes on 35,764 nodes; cached by map hash).
- Dropped while ideating (not written up): landmark (ALT) distance coordinates as the report basis (a change of basis, C6); a trip-level speed ratio between bands (pure walk/vehicle mix, X10); unimodality of a route's band profile (K4's piece count plus unsplit-session loops, F1); random graph cuts with tomographic inversion (A.3's cordons in another form); a vote for one of several public backbones (C3, D.3).

## A1. Apex ceilings: how high up the public road hierarchy a trip climbs
1. **Pitch.** Each user says whether one of its trips reached the top band that the trip's own fastest path uses, or stayed one or more bands below it; the server learns a law over nested public sub-networks ("ceilings") and synthesizes from a mixture of Boltzmann routers confined to them, so the route law changes while every shortest-path computation stays public.
2. **Report.** On the device: draw one eligible trip uniformly (eligible: the free-flow fastest path between the trip's own endpoints has apex a* ≥ B1; route free-flow time ≤ 3 × fastest; common guard). Apex a(·) of a path = the highest band on which it runs ≥ 200 m contiguously (a* = the same function on the fastest path, from a CH query plus unpacking on the public map); deficit δ = a* − a(route). Value x ∈ {reached or above (δ ≤ 0), one band below (δ = 1), two or more below (δ ≥ 2), ⊥} → GRR over 4 (2 bits). The user's whole data becomes its personal law over the 3 deficit classes, of which one draw is sent. At ε 8 and at large n the joint (a*, δ-class) instead: 2 + 3 + 3 = 8 cells + ⊥, GRR over 9, which also carries the trip's scale in the hierarchy.
3. **Privacy.** Lift lemma X1: trip choice, CH query, apex and deficit are local preprocessing of the user's whole data into one value of a public 4- (or 9-) element domain, and only its GRR output leaves: P(y | D) ≤ max_x P(GRR(x) = y) ≤ e^ε · min_x P(GRR(x) = y) ≤ e^ε · P(y | D′) for any two users D, D′. One report, full ε, no split. Public and frozen: CH heuristic, band quantiles, 200 m rule, eligibility and guard, class edges, ⊥ semantics.
4. **Server.** Debias GRR, shares among eligible users s_c = ŝ_c / (1 − ŝ_⊥), F7 gate. Generative family: the ceiling-c router is the P3 Boltzmann walk with cost t(e) + κ·1[band(e) > c] (κ public, e.g. 300 s per edge above the ceiling; soft, so every O-D pair stays connected), c ∈ {B0, B1, B2, top}; the confusion A(δ | c, a*) is simulated once on public O-D pairs. At ε ≤ 2 one number: ρ = ceiling mass below "top" (lower ceilings in public fixed proportions), fitted by maximum likelihood on the GRR counts with the prior's a* law, shrunk to the prior's ρ₀ = 0. At ε 8 and large n the joint identifies the whole law π: in the hard-ceiling limit a = min(c, a*), so (a, 1[a = a*]) is a right-censored observation of the ceiling c with censoring "time" a*, and π is a Kaplan-Meier product over bands, valid for any trips-per-user law (one uniform trip, F2) and any O-D law if ceilings are independent of a*; without that assumption, one law per a* class. Synthesis: O, D, departure from P3 (or C2); c ~ π̂; route = ceiling-c walk (road-valid by construction; after the public step cap it finishes on the ceiling-c shortest path); segment times by P3 with public band factors (or A3's junction delays), i.e. time attached to hierarchy levels.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(O, D) + log Σ_c π̂_c · Π_k P_c(e_k | v_k, D) + public floor term. P_c reads the public per-ceiling cost-to-go h_{c,D}: 4 reverse Dijkstras per queried destination (≈ 160 ms with scipy `csgraph`), computed lazily for any destination on one code path for members and non-members, cached by map + config hash, shared by the 17 generators. The only private inputs are ≤ 3 weights snapped to a public grid (P4); π̂_c ≥ a public floor keeps every sequence finite.
6. **n = 91.** ε 0.5: 4-way GRR SD ≈ 0.33 per share → prior arm (the gate keeps the prior). ε 2: SD ≈ 0.074 per share, divided by (1 − d) with d = users without an eligible trip (guess 0.2-0.4) → ≈ 0.09-0.12; one number survives: the share of trips that stay below their fastest path's top band (what the prior walk gives at its public temperature must be simulated before any run; walks, bikes and campus trips plausibly put Geolife at 0.3-0.5, which would be 2-3 SD away from a small prior share). ε 8: GRR nearly truthful, SD ≈ √(π(1 − π)/91)/(1 − d) ≈ 0.06-0.08 per share: the split "one below" vs "two or more below" (2 numbers) and, through the joint, the a* law. Only at n = 10,000 (SD ≈ 0.008 per share at ε 2): one ceiling law per a* class without the censoring assumption, × 3 departure periods (≈ 24 cells by OLH), and per-ceiling band speed factors as a second question family.
7. **Closest work.** CH, highway hierarchies (Sanders & Schultes 2005) and reach (Gutman 2004) are non-private routing algorithmics; labelled and latent-class route choice (Ben-Akiva et al. 1984; Greene & Hensher 2003) mix route generators, non-private and not nested by network level; "sample one trip, then GRR" is the Acharya-Liu-Sun 2023 baseline; AHEAD, Yang 2020's trie, Cunningham 2021 and C8/A.4 report nodes or prefixes of a public hierarchy (frequency oracles), whereas A1 reports an ordinal max statistic relative to the trip's own fastest path and factorizes the likelihood over nested sub-networks. Vs C3/B.4 (label drawn from a posterior over K complete generators, confusion matrix under a trips-per-user law, F2): A1's label is a deterministic function of route geometry, its components are nested (censored identification) and only the route law moves; A1 could serve as C3's route layer. Vs C1/A.1 (user-averaged class metres centred on the fastest path, private cost tilts): ordinal, one trip, every cost-to-go public. Vs K4/R2-C.2 (via points, pieces) and C8 (corridors): no location. Vs K1 + K8: route space, no twins, no time. Vs C6, C9, C10, A.3: no basis, token, flow or cordon. LDP Kaplan-Meier with censored categorical reports: unchecked, search first.
8. **Risk.** X10: at n = 91 "stayed below" may largely be the walk/bike share that C3 and C1 already move; separation then rests on structure (route-only, censored identification, head-to-head with C3 on the same users, recovery simulation with ceilings independent of speed regimes). Walkers matched onto arterial carriageways count as "reached". CH bands depend on the ordering heuristic (ablation: reach bands, OSM classes); if OSM classes do as well, the hierarchy is not the contribution.

## A2. Regret ledger: in which band a route's main deviation is decided
1. **Pitch.** A route's excess free-flow time over its fastest path splits exactly into per-step regrets on the public cost-to-go field (a telescoping sum); each user reports in which band one trip's largest deliberate deviation was made, and the server fits band-specific sharpness of the P3 Boltzmann walk, whose cost-to-go stays entirely public.
2. **Report.** On the device, for one trip drawn uniformly among the eligible ones (fastest free-flow time h_D(O) ≥ 300 s; route free-flow time ≤ 3 × h_D(O); common guard; at least one deliberate deviation): the regret of step k over edge e = (v, w) is ρ_k = t_ff(e) + h_D(w) − h_D(v) ≥ 0, zero on any fastest continuation, and Σ_k ρ_k = T_ff(route) − h_D(O) exactly. A deliberate deviation is a step with ρ_k ≥ max(20 s, 5 % of h_D(O)) (public; GPS zigzags and matcher gap-fills carry almost no regret). Value x = band group of the largest-regret step ∈ {B0, B1-B3} or ⊥ (no eligible deviating trip) → GRR over 3. The user's whole data becomes its personal law over where its deviations happen. At ε 8 and large n: band group × progress third of that step (progress = 1 − h_D(v)/h_D(O)) = 6 cells + "no deviation" + ⊥, GRR over 8 (then trips without a deviation are eligible too).
3. **Privacy.** As A1 (X1): one GRR output over a public 3- (or 8-) element domain; h_D, bands, thresholds and eligibility public and frozen. Full ε, no split.
4. **Server.** Debias, F7 gate. Model: P(e | v) ∝ exp(−β_{b(e)}·ρ(e)) with β = β₀·e^λ on B0 edges and β₀ on upper edges; β₀ stays the prior's at ε ≤ 2 (set publicly from the near-fastest shares of Zhu & Levinson 2015), λ is estimated; class probabilities as functions of (β₀, λ) are simulated once on a public O-D sample over a public grid (e.g. 5 × 7 values), and maximum likelihood over the grid puts the estimate on the public grid by construction. Identification: given a deviation, its band depends on λ and on the routes' band composition, which the public walk itself simulates; β₀ is pinned by the "no deviation" share of the ε 8 item. Synthesis: O, D, departure from P3 (or C2); route = band-sharpness walk (road-valid, step cap); segment times by P3 (or A3). Reading: do people improvise on local streets and follow the main road (λ < 0), or the reverse.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(O, D) + Σ_k [−β_{b_k}·ρ_k − log Σ_{e′ ∈ out(v_k)} exp(−β_{b(e′)}·ρ(e′))] + public floor. It reads only the public h_D (the same cache as P3) and two numbers on a public grid; no private parameter ever enters a shortest-path computation (unlike C1's cost tilts, which need a private cost-to-go per destination and per generator, lesson 7). Cost ≈ route length × out-degree; finite on the strongly connected component, floor elsewhere.
6. **n = 91.** ε 0.5: prior arm. ε 2: 3-way GRR SD ≈ 0.073 per share, divided by (1 − d) with d ≈ 0.2-0.3 → ≈ 0.10 on the band split among deviating trips; λ moves only if the prior's split is off by ≥ 0.2 (plausible if Geolife's deviations are mostly campus and residential shortcuts, but unknown). ε 8: ≈ 0.07 on the split, the "no deviation" share fixes β₀ to ≈ ±0.06, the progress cells sit at ≈ ±0.05 each, so only a strong end-of-trip effect shows. Only at n = 10,000: sharpness by band × progress × period (≈ 6-12 numbers) and a regret-size class jointly.
7. **Closest work.** MaxEnt IRL (Ziebart et al. 2008) and recursive logit (Fosgerau et al. 2013) use the same per-step advantage with one temperature, non-private; Lima et al. 2016 (J. R. Soc. Interface) measure how drivers' routes depart from fastest paths, non-private; random regret minimisation (Chorus 2008) is a different "regret". No LDP work reports where along a route its Bellman residual sits (unchecked). Vs C1/A.1/E.3 (detour and class moments, private cost tilts): A2 tilts only the step sharpness and sends where the deviation happens, not how large it is. Vs K4/R2-C.2 (piece count, via points; Knapen 2016): no route segmentation, no location. Vs C7/C.4 (likelihood-gain bit) and D.3 (duel): no model comparison. Vs C9/E.2 (manoeuvre × class tokens): the value is the band of the route's largest regret, not a move's bigram. Vs K1 + K8 and K3: route space, not time.
8. **Risk.** Lesson 5: map-matching on dense local streets can create deliberate-looking deviations (parallel-street mismatches) and gap-fills hide real ones, so λ may describe the matcher (the guard and the 20 s threshold blunt this, they do not remove it); the novel number moves only at ε 8; at ε 2 the benchmark gain (length W1, MIA) may be small even when λ is estimated.

## A3. Junction ledger: seconds lost where a route changes hierarchy level
1. **Pitch.** Per-segment times get their stop-and-go part from the joints of the public hierarchy: each user reports the average time its trips lose at band-transition junctions, measured against each trip's own cruising speed so that walking, cycling and driving give comparable seconds, and the generator adds the estimated delay at every such junction of every synthetic route.
2. **Report.** Public junction set J = nodes where edges of different bands meet or two B1+ edges cross (a function of H only; OSM signal tags are left to lens R3-D). On the device, for every matched train trip with ≥ 3 junctions of J (all trips, not one: a scalar average has ≈ 2.7× lower variance at ε 2, X2): delay at junction j = time spent inside a public zone (40 m before and after j along the route, from the matched points) − zone length / the trip's own median speed outside all zones, clipped to [0, 120 s] (public cap, which bounds long stops of unsplit sessions, F1); trip value = mean delay per junction; user value x_u = mean over its trips, scaled to [−1, 1]. The user's whole data becomes this one number; users with no such trip send the public default (the prior's mean delay), as in SINTEZA §4. Sent by HM at full ε (d = 1); at ε 8 and large n, d = 2 with one publicly sampled coordinate per user (peak 7-9 h and 17-19 h vs other hours, or entering vs leaving an upper band).
3. **Privacy.** HM is ε-LDP for every input in [−1, 1], and x_u is a bounded function of the user's whole data (clip, then average), so the report is user-level ε-LDP; the coordinate at d = 2 is sampled publicly and answered at full ε (Wang et al. 2019), no split; output snapped to a public grid (P4).
4. **Server.** Mean delay D̂ = debiased HM mean, shrunk to the prior's public turn delay (the default-sending users also pull it toward the prior by their share; documented, not corrected); at ε 8, D̂_peak and D̂_off. Route law, O, D and departure stay P3's (or another module's). Segment times = free-flow × public factor × AR(1) jitter, plus a delay drawn from a public law with mean D̂ (e.g. a shifted exponential with a public share of zero-delay passages) at each junction of J that the synthetic route passes, plus the public turn delay elsewhere; per-edge entry times follow by accumulation (P1 payload).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Identical to the prior's route likelihood, because time never enters the edge likelihood (SINTEZA §4.7): the MIA is blind to A3 by construction (X11), a visibility weakness; P8 (a time-aware MIA) would be its test.
6. **n = 91.** ε 0.5: HM SD 0.43 of the half-range (60 s) ≈ ±26 s → nothing. ε 2: 0.11 of the half-range plus between-user spread ≈ ±7 s; if the prior's cited delay (5-10 s per junction) is off by 15-20 s (wide Beijing junctions with long signal cycles), that is 2-3 SD, i.e. one number. ε 8: ≈ ±3 s with a between-user spread of 0.4; at d = 2 ≈ ±4 s per coordinate, so a peak excess of ≥ 11 s shows (a P12 gain). Only at n = 10,000: delay by band pair × period (≈ 9-12 numbers, ≈ ±2 s each at ε 2) and the shape of the delay law (share of zero-delay passages). Unlike every ε 2 item of rounds 1-2 (X10), its value does not hinge on the walk/vehicle mix, because each trip is normalized by its own cruising speed (pedestrians and drivers both wait at the same signals; buses are the exception, see 8).
7. **Closest work.** Probe-based junction delay and queue estimation (e.g. Ban, Hao & Sun 2011, TR-C; non-private, per junction, cars only); BerlinMOD (stops by road class, non-private); Bian et al. 2024 (durations per mode, user-level central DP, no junctions). Vs K3/R2-A.2 (speed state at a random second; mode-dependent; stops of every kind mixed): A3 places the loss at public graph joints and removes the speed level, and with it most of the mode mix. Vs K1/R2-C.1 (rank of the total duration among twins) and C1/D.2 (speed ratio to free flow): a component of time that neither identifies. Vs K2 (prisms) and K8 (within-user contrasts): no prism, no pair. Vs C8/A.4 (a speed bit per hierarchy node, key-value): no node is reported, one user-level scalar.
8. **Risk.** Low novelty (a mean oracle on a known transport statistic + P3; an evaluator may score it 2); buses stopping near junctions inflate the delay; with points ≥ 5 s apart after cleaning, a fast vehicle has 1-2 points inside an 80 m zone, so per-junction values are noisy (the user average absorbs part of it); the duration W1 gain is small if the prior's speed level is the main error; MIA-blind.

## A4. Barrier sides: topological route classes on a covering graph
1. **Pitch.** Large faces of the public road graph (parks, lakes, palace grounds, rail yards without through roads) split route space into classes "passes this barrier on the left or on the right"; each user reports, for one trip whose straight O-D line crosses a barrier, whether it passed on the same side as its fastest path, and the generator walks on the double cover of the graph around that barrier, so a side preference enters an exact likelihood.
2. **Report.** Public barrier set: bounded faces of the planar embedding of the graph with area ≥ 1 km² inside the study area (≤ 20, frozen by hash), each with a public reference point and a public ray. For one eligible trip drawn uniformly (eligible: the straight O-D segment crosses a barrier; if several, the one with the longest chord, by public rule; common guard): side bit = parity of the route's crossings of that barrier's ray, compared with the fastest path's parity. Value ∈ {same side, other side, ⊥} → GRR over 3. The user's whole data becomes its personal law over the two sides.
3. **Privacy.** As A1 (X1): one GRR output over a public 3-element domain; barriers, rays and the selection rule public and frozen. Full ε.
4. **Server.** Share q̂ = P(other side | eligible), F7 gate. Model: with probability q the route ends in the opposite sheet of the double cover (base graph × crossing parity of the trip's barrier ray), otherwise in the fastest path's sheet; within a sheet, the P3 walk on the cover graph. Synthesis: O, D, departure from P3 (or C2); sheet ~ q̂; walk to (D, sheet) on the cover; its projection is a road-valid route of the base graph; times by P3.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** The barrier and the sheet of any edge sequence follow from its own O, D and ray crossings, so exactly one mixture term is nonzero: log P(O, D) + log P(sheet) + log P_walk(route | cover, D, sheet) + floor; trips without a barrier use the plain P3 walk. Public cost-to-go on the cover = 2 reverse Dijkstras on a graph of twice the size per queried (destination, barrier), cached by map + config hash, shared by the 17 generators.
6. **n = 91.** Few Geolife trips straddle a large face (short trips around Haidian; the candidates are the Yuanmingyuan, Summer Palace and Olympic parks and the central palace block), so d ≈ 0.7-0.9 and the side share has SD ≈ 0.07/(1 − d) ≈ 0.25-0.7 at ε 2 and ≈ 0.15-0.3 at ε 8: nothing survives at any ε. Only at n = 10,000 (recovery simulation; taxi-scale data such as T-Drive would be the natural target, outside S3): side preferences for the 5-10 most crossed barriers, ≈ ±0.02-0.05 each at ε 2.
7. **Closest work.** Homotopy classes and h-signatures in robot path planning (Bhattacharya, Kumar & Likhachev 2012, Autonomous Robots), non-private; screenline counts in transport planning; A.3 (cordon crossing counts by hour bin, dropped for overlapping ε-DP traffic measurement): A4 sends a topological class relative to the trip's own fastest path, not a count, and puts it into an exact likelihood on a covering graph. Vs K4 (via points), C6 (spectral field), C10 (flows): no location, basis or flow. No round-1 or round-2 candidate uses route topology.
8. **Risk.** Invisible at n = 91 (lesson 3: novelty that appears only at n ≥ 5,000 scored 1-2); faces depend on graph completeness (footpaths through a park missing from the graph create a false barrier, P9); the side taken may mostly follow from where D lies relative to the barrier's ends, which the P3 walk already reproduces, leaving little to correct.

## Self-ranking
1. A1 Apex ceilings (top pick): uses the lens's open angle (a routing hierarchy) both as the report alphabet and as the likelihood factorization; a 2-bit report whose one number moves at ε 2; censored identification for any trip count; route law visible to the MIA; every cache public.
2. A3 Junction ledger: low novelty, but its ε 2 number does not hinge on the walk/vehicle mix (X10), and it gives R1's per-segment times their stop-and-go part; MIA-blind.
3. A2 Regret ledger: the most novel statistic, but its new number moves only at ε 8 and may describe the matcher; A4 (barrier sides) is a large-n option only.
