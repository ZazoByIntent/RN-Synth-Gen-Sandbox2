# R3-C Utility-first (query-driven): four ideas

Lens: start from the planned metrics (P12 duration W1 within each departure period; length, duration, speed and departure W1 and 3 × 3 OD JSD of P2) and derive the per-user statistic that pins them down; the synthesis follows. Ids as in `00_baseline.md`. Noise from its §1 cheat sheet (n = 91; HM SD of the debiased mean in units of the half-range, d = 1: 0.43 / 0.11 / 0.017 at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8; d = 2: 0.61 / 0.15 / 0.023; d = 8: 1.21 / 0.30 / 0.047, up to 0.13 at ε 8 with between-user spread). Every idea: one report per train user at full ε (P11, ⊥ or a public null value without a matched trip), all constants frozen by hash before u182 (F8), P3 simulator as host, P4 kit, P6 recovery simulation.

## Idea 1. ESD: energy-score descent
1. **Name and pitch.** Energy-score descent: each phone sends the gradient of the benchmark's own kind of distance (the energy score, a proper scoring rule whose expectation is the energy distance, in 1-D the Cramér distance, between simulator and real trips) with respect to a few public simulator knobs at the public prior; the server takes one Gauss–Newton step.
2. **Report.** Public: P3 at θ₀ with d knobs from a frozen n·ε² table (d ≤ 2 at ε ≤ 2: log speed level, distance-decay; d ≤ 8 at ε 8: + peak factor, jitter scale, departure shift, route temperature, per-period decay); attribute map a(trip) = the P2/P12 attributes on public scales, clipped to public ranges (log duration, log length, log mean speed, departure cos/sin, period one-hot). Device, per own matched trip y with context c = (origin node, departure period): R = 16–32 simulator replicates X_r(θ) for c (fresh local randomness); per-trip gradient g(y) = ∂_θ ES_R(θ; y) at θ₀ with ES_R = mean_r ‖a(X_r) − a(y)‖ − ½·mean_{r≠r'} ‖a(X_r) − a(X_r')‖ (pathwise derivative for time knobs, baseline-subtracted score-function derivative for destination and route knobs). Average over all own trips: ḡ_u ∈ [−B, B]^d, B public. A public data-independent draw picks coordinate j; send HM(ḡ_u,j / B_j). Size: one real (plus the public j).
   Key identity: for a location knob on one attribute g(y) = 1 − 2·PIT(y) exactly (the CRPS gradient), so |g| ≤ 1 without any clip box; in general |g| ≤ the public Lipschitz constant of a(X_θ) in θ (pathwise) or attribute-box diameter × public score bound.
3. **Privacy.** ḡ_u is a (locally randomized) function of the user's whole data with a public range for any trip count; j is public; HM is ε-LDP on [−1, 1]; one report, the rest post-processing (lift lemma X1). Bounded by construction: no data-dependent clip range and no clipping bias. No ε split; users are split across coordinates only at ε 8.
4. **Server.** HM debias per coordinate (ĝ_j). Public Hessian H = ∂²_θ E_{Y~P_θ₀} ES(P_θ, Y) at θ₀ (null "real = prior"), by simulation once, cached by map + config hash. Gate, exact for any trips-per-user law: properness gives E g(y) = 0 for every trip under the null, so E ḡ_u = 0 for every m_u and for ⊥ users (HM(0)); the HM output is bounded, so a pre-registered Hoeffding-level z (d = 1) or Hotelling-type (d > 1) test keeps its level. If rejected: θ̂ = θ₀ − λ·H⁻¹ĝ (public damping λ, public box). Synthesis = P3 at θ̂: origin by road-length mass, destination by gravity with decay β̂, Boltzmann-walk route (road-valid), departure from the public profile (shifted at ε 8), segment time = free-flow × factor(θ̂) × jitter + turn delay (P1 payload).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** The simulator's exact route likelihood at θ̂: log P(o) + log P_β̂(d | o) + Σ log walk steps(θ̂) + public floor; time absent. It moves with the decay and route knobs, so the MIA sees a change (unlike K1 at n = 91, X11), but only through ≤ 8 population numbers. Reads only θ̂ and map/config caches.
6. **n = 91 vs 10,000.** Location knob on log duration (σ = prior spread): H = 1/(√π·σ), so SD(Δθ̂) = SD(ĝ)·√π·σ. ε 0.5: 0.43 → 0.76σ, prior kept unless off by ≥ 1.5σ. ε 2: 0.11 → 0.20σ (d = 2: 0.27σ each), a gain if the level is off by ≥ 0.4σ; the second number is the reach (decay). ε 8: d = 8 at 0.047–0.13 per coordinate → 0.08–0.23σ per knob if H is well conditioned (level and decay nearly orthogonal; level and peak factor are not). n = 10,000 (SD × 0.095): 10–25 knobs at ε 1–2, incl. per-period speed factors (P12 proper), per-period decay, road-class tastes. On Geolife: speed level and reach at ε 2; everything else only in P6.
7. **Closest work and difference.** LDP-SGD with PM/HM (Wang et al. 2019): ESD swaps the log-likelihood for a proper scoring rule of a likelihood-free simulator (times enter), its gradient is bounded by construction, and it takes one public-Hessian Newton step behind an exact gate. Scoring-rule and minimum-distance inference (Gneiting & Raftery 2007; Pacchiardi & Dutta 2022; Briol et al. 2019, robust MMD estimators; Bernton et al. 2019, Wasserstein estimators) is central or non-private. B.1 (→ C1) sends clipped recursive-logit score coordinates (routes only, needs an exact likelihood and a clip box); C1 matches moments by MaxEnt; ESD descends the benchmark distance itself. C7 is zero-order (likelihood gain at a server-set knob, surface fit); ESD is first-order, one step. K1: with one location knob, one trip and 3 bins, ESD's per-trip quantity is K1's PIT, so K1 *dur* is a special case; ESD averages all trips (X2: ≈ 2.7× lower variance for scalars), moves several knobs incl. route ones, keeps K1's exact null. "Simulate, perturb, match" matches a released summary; here the device computes the descent direction and nothing is matched. Not a vote (C3, C5), flow (C10), hierarchy (C8), contrast (K8), prism (K2), state clock (K3) or proxy answer (K7).
8. **Main risk.** At n = 91 and ε 2 it moves the same duration level as K1, C1 and C3 (lesson 2, X10); the distinct parts (several knobs, route knobs) show only at ε 8 and in P6. Device cost: R replicate routes per trip need a cost-to-go per sampled destination (≈ 40 ms each); a public replicate bank per origin zone (P3 cache shared by the 17 MIA generators) is needed or the budget breaks (lesson 7). The null Hessian is wrong when the prior is far off (damped step, undershoot). Attribute scales and ranges are design constants (F8). Unsplit sessions drag the speed level down (F1). Shares P1, P3, P4, P6, P10, P11, P12.

## Idea 2. ORC: opportunity-rank calibration of the destination law
1. **Name and pitch.** The metrics that read geography (length W1, 3 × 3 OD JSD and, through length, duration W1) are driven by how far people reach for a destination; in the radiation / intervening-opportunities family the destination law depends on the data only through the opportunity rank of each chosen destination (how much public opportunity lies closer by free-flow time), so each phone sends the mean PIT of its own opportunity ranks: one bounded number.
2. **Report.** Public: opportunity mass = road-length mass per node (OSM only, S1); origin mass m_o = road-length mass within a public radius r₀ of o; free-flow costs on the public graph. Device, per own trip (matched endpoints o, d): one forward Dijkstra from o (≈ 40 ms, scipy); s = mass of nodes strictly closer than d; randomized PIT under the prior u = 1 − (m_o / (m_o + s + V·v_d))^κ₀ with V ~ U(0, 1) local and v_d the mass of d (exactly uniform on the discrete graph under the prior). κ₀ = 1 is the parameter-free radiation law (Simini et al. 2012), so the prior needs no fitted constant. Average over own trips: ū ∈ [0, 1]; send HM(2ū − 1) at full ε; a user without a matched trip sends HM(ξ), ξ a public fair ±1 coin (null mean). At ε 8 or large n a public draw picks one of {pooled, AM peak, PM peak, off-peak} (users split, never ε).
3. **Privacy.** ū is a public (locally randomized) function of all the user's trips with range [0, 1] for any trip count; masses, r₀, κ₀ and the graph are public and frozen; HM at full ε; one report; lift lemma X1. The ⊥ coin is data-independent; everyone in the train split reports (P11).
4. **Server.** Debias Ū = (1 + mean HM)/2. Gate: every trip's u is uniform under the prior, so E ū = ½ for every user whatever m_u, ⊥ included; Hoeffding-level pre-registered test. If rejected, fit the tempered radiation law P(S > s | o) = (m_o/(m_o + s))^κ (κ = 1 radiation, close to Yang et al. 2014's extended radiation): E u = κ₀/(κ + κ₀), so κ̂ = κ₀·(1/Ū − 1) in closed form (an unknown ⊥ share only shrinks κ̂ toward κ₀, conservative). Synthesis: origin by road-length mass (prior), destination from o by the tempered law with κ̂ (exact, normalized over the sorted opportunity list of one forward Dijkstra), Boltzmann-walk route (road-valid), departure from the public profile, segment times by the P3 time model.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(o) + log P_κ̂(d | o) + Σ log walk steps + floor, with P_κ̂(d | o) = (m_o/(m_o + s_d))^κ̂ − (m_o/(m_o + s_d + v_d))^κ̂; one forward Dijkstra per queried origin, cached by node, the same code path for members and non-members. MIA-visible through one scalar; no user's destination enters.
6. **n = 91 vs 10,000.** SD(Ū) = ½·SD(HM mean): 0.215 / 0.055 / ≈ 0.02 (HM 0.009 plus between-user spread) at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8. SD(κ̂) ≈ SD(Ū)/Ū²: ε 2 ±0.22 at κ = 1; a truth with κ = 2 (half the radiation reach, Ū = 1/3, a 3 SD shift) or κ = 0.5 (Ū = 2/3) is detected and set to about ±25 %. ε 0.5: only an order-of-magnitude error moves anything (prior arm). ε 8: κ ±8 % pooled, or three period-specific κ_p at ≈ ±14 % each. n = 10,000: κ per period × origin density class (9–12 numbers) or a two-parameter law per period. The prior is plausibly wrong by ×2 (radiation was fitted to commuting flows; Geolife mixes walks and leisure), the regime in which ε 2 pays (lesson 11). The bounded u has ≈ 75 % of the efficiency of the unbounded sufficient statistic log(1 + s/m_o) at κ = 1, irrelevant while HM noise dominates (≈ 3× the data variance at ε 2).
7. **Closest work and difference.** Radiation model (Simini et al. 2012), intervening opportunities (Stouffer 1940, Schneider 1959), extended radiation (Yang et al. 2014), rank-distance (Noulas et al. 2012), TimeGeo's rank-based exploration (2016): all non-private; ORC adds the user-level LDP statistic, its exact null and the closed-form estimator. C2 and B.2 calibrate gravity from cost-band and zone shares of one sampled trip by min-KL Furness; ORC has no zones and no bands, an opportunity law instead of cost deterrence, one bounded scalar averaged over all trips and an exact null for any trips-per-user law (C2 calibrates where, ORC how far: complements). K2 ties destinations to a time budget (iso-time ring), ORC to intervening opportunities. K1 ranks durations among twins; ORC ranks the destination among public opportunities (the same exact-null idea on another model component, and the likelihood moves). Not a hierarchy (C8), flow (C10), corridor (K4), hub (K5) or signature (K6: radius of gyration is a user's spread, not the destination law given an origin).
8. **Main risk.** Unsplit sessions (F1): a round trip ends near its start (s ≈ 0, u ≈ 0) and drags κ̂ up, so P10 comes first. Road-length mass is a crude opportunity proxy (an OSM-amenity ablation needs the X14 export, borrowed from R3-D). The origin law stays prior, so the OD JSD gain is limited to the destination side. At ε 2 one number that may coincide with C2's band item and K2's reach (lesson 2): show head-to-head on the same users. Novelty may score 2–3 (published statistic + published generator; lesson 1). Shares P3, P4, P6, P10, P11 (P1 for time).

## Idea 3. UAD: utility-adjoint direction
1. **Name and pitch.** Instead of a randomly drawn coordinate of a statistic vector (C1) or a hand-picked question (round-1 n·ε² table, K1's frozen list), every phone sends one number: its own statistic vector projected on the single direction that a public pre-computation shows will most reduce the expected benchmark loss after the update; the direction comes from the metrics' sensitivity (their adjoint) and a public prior over how wrong the simulator may be.
2. **Report.** Public catalogue of K ≈ 10–20 per-user statistics s_k(D_u) ∈ [−1, 1], each a centred residual whose null mean is 0 for any trip count: mean duration PIT under twins or the period law (K1 type), mean opportunity-rank PIT (Idea 2), mean length PIT given origin, departure-hour PIT, mean of 1[zone(d) = z] − P_prior(z | o) for the 3 × 3 zones, mean clipped log speed ratio minus its prior mean. A public frozen vector w with ‖w‖₁ ≤ 1; the device computes r_u = wᵀs(D_u) ∈ [−1, 1] and sends HM(r_u) at full ε; ⊥ sends HM(0). Larger n·ε²: k directions w₁ … w_k, users split by a public draw, k and the w's from the same criterion.
3. **Privacy.** wᵀs is a public bounded function of all the user's data; w is computed before the freeze from OSM, cited constants and the metric definitions only (never Geolife, F8); HM at full ε; one report; X1.
4. **Server.** Offline and public: (a) a misspecification prior Σ₀ for μ = E_real s − E_θ₀ s, by simulating the statistic means under knob draws from cited plausible ranges (speed level, decay, peak factor, detour, departure shift); (b) the metric adjoint J, the Hessian of the expected benchmark loss (weighted P2 + P12 vector, squared Cramér as the W1 surrogate) in μ, by simulation; (c) after y = wᵀμ + noise (σ_n² = HM variance/n at the public n and ε) the expected loss reduction is wᵀΣ₀JΣ₀w / (wᵀΣ₀w + σ_n²); maximize over ‖w‖₁ ≤ 1 (a small sparse generalized eigenproblem). Small n·ε²: w ≈ the top eigenvector of Σ₀JΣ₀ (the "utility principal direction"); large n·ε²: the loss-weighted A-optimal split over k directions. Online: debias, gate (z-test of mean 0, exact for any trip count), Kalman update μ̂ = Σ₀w(wᵀΣ₀w + σ_n²)⁻¹ȳ, knobs θ̂ by the public linear response of E_θ s (ridge to θ₀); synthesis = P3 at θ̂ (road-valid, timed).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** The simulator's likelihood at θ̂; it moves only along knobs that w loads on: if w loads on reach or OD residuals, P(d | o) changes (MIA-visible); if only on time, the likelihood is the prior's (stated in the frozen design table, X11).
6. **n = 91 vs 10,000.** One HM number at ε 2, SD 0.11 along w. If the misfit is mostly one-dimensional in statistic space (one wrong knob moves several statistics together: reach shifts length PIT, duration PIT and OD residuals), the projection gathers all of it at the noise of one number, up to K× lower variance on the loss-relevant component than sampling one of K coordinates (C1's design pays √K in SD); a misfit orthogonal to w is not seen at all (Σ₀ decides). ε 0.5: nothing, as everywhere. ε 8: k = 3–4 directions. n = 10,000: k ≈ 10–20; the advantage over uniform coordinate sampling shrinks to a constant factor.
7. **Closest work and difference.** McKenna, Maity, Mazumdar, Miklau 2020 (workload-optimal LDP strategy matrices for linear counting queries, record-level; the unchecked neighbour of §9): UAD is user-level, targets a nonlinear metric workload through its adjoint, is Bayesian (prior Σ₀) and sends one scalar per user. HDMM and fitness-for-use (central), Bayesian optimal design (Chaloner & Verdinelli 1995), LDP PCA (Wang & Xu 2020) are other neighbours. C1 draws a coordinate uniformly and fits by MaxEnt; CALM splits users across marginals by a heuristic; UAD derives both the question and the allocation from an explicit expected-metric-loss criterion and updates by a Kalman step. Not a vote (C3), zero-order evaluation (C7), stated choice (K7) or contrast (K8); it can host the K1 *dur*, ORC and ESD statistics as catalogue members.
8. **Main risk.** Novelty: may be judged a CLOSE VARIANT of McKenna 2020 (one detail: user-level Bayesian projection), or a design rule rather than a mechanism. Σ₀ from cited ranges decides what can be learned, and a misfit orthogonal to w is invisible (a small public random-direction cohort as a check costs users). Linearization around the prior; the squared-Cramér surrogate of W1. Shares P2, P3, P4, P6, P11, P12 (the design needs the metric code before the freeze).

## Idea 4. MDR: metric-decision release with a certified P12 bound
1. **Name and pitch.** Treat the release as a decision under the benchmark's own loss: from one coarse duration rank per user under the prior's per-period duration law, release the per-period law that minimizes the posterior expected W1 (the pointwise posterior median of the CDF, the Bayes act for W1) and publish a test-set-free certified upper bound on P12.
2. **Report.** One own trip drawn uniformly (local, data-independent); its departure period p (3 public periods); u = F_prior,p(duration), the PIT under the prior simulator's period-marginal duration law (public, cached), so u carries length, speed and stops together, exactly what P12 reads. Since W1 = ∫|α − G(α)|·q′_prior(α) dα (G the CDF of u, q′_prior the prior's quantile density, largest in the long-duration tail), the class edges are set publicly to maximize the expected W1 reduction per GRR cell (for example {0–0.5, 0.5–0.85, 0.85–1} instead of terciles). ε ≤ 2: class of u (3) + ⊥, GRR over 4; ε 8: joint (p, class) 9 + ⊥, GRR over 10.
3. **Privacy.** A path to one value in a small public frozen domain, then GRR at full ε (lift lemma X1); uniform trip choice; frozen edges and periods; ⊥ for users without a matched trip.
4. **Server.** Debias; exact posterior over the class shares given the GRR counts (Dirichlet prior centred at the design shares, public concentration; the GRR likelihood is a known mixture; Gibbs data augmentation with b ≤ 10); ⊥ as in K1 (shares among eligible users). Release G_rel(α) = pointwise posterior median of G(α): W1 is pointwise L1 in CDF space with public weights q′, so this minimizes the posterior expected W1, and it is monotone automatically. Minimax variant: the centre of the simultaneous (1 − δ) band. Certificate: the posterior (1 − δ) quantile of W1(release, truth) per period, in seconds, beside the exact-null gate (uniform shares). Synthesis: a P3 trip with period p and duration T gets U = F_prior,p(T), U′ = G_rel⁻¹(U), T′ = F_prior,p⁻¹(U′); every segment time × T′/T (route unchanged, road-valid).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Time-only correction, so the route likelihood is the prior's: MIA-blind by construction at every ε (X11), as K1 *dur*. Said openly; the certificate, not the MIA, is its evidence.
6. **n = 91 vs 10,000.** ε 2: 3 shares ±0.073, so G at 0.5 and 0.85 ±0.07; a shift of the median PIT by ≥ 0.15 (≈ 0.4σ of log duration) is released; the 90 % certificate is about ±0.12 in PIT units, loose in the tail. ε 0.5: prior plus a near-vacuous certificate. ε 8: 9 cells ±0.033, so per-period G at two points ±0.1: P12 per period and a useful certificate. n = 10,000: 10 classes × 6 periods by OLH (≈ ±0.01 per cell), full per-period quantile functions and a tight certificate.
7. **Closest work and difference.** K1 (PIT under the prior, step function g, route-conditional twins, chi-square gate, plug-in shares): MDR conditions on the period-marginal law that P12 reads (not speed given route), sets class edges by the W1 weights, releases the Bayes or minimax act under W1 and adds the certificate. Boedihardjo, Strohmer & Vershynin 2022–24 (central-DP synthetic data with W1 accuracy guarantees); Waudby-Smith, Wu & Ramdas 2023 (LDP confidence sets by nonparametric randomized response); Cormode et al. 2019 (LDP range and CDF queries); Kuleshov et al. 2018 (recalibration, non-private). No moments or OD (C1, C2), no vote (C3, C5), no budget or state clock (K2, K3), no contrast (K8).
8. **Main risk.** Closest to K1 (novelty 2–3). Metric gaming: matching P12 by time scaling alone distorts speed W1 when the truth's long durations come from longer routes. MIA-blind. The certificate covers the user-weighted law of one sampled trip while P12 is trip-weighted (X4). Unsplit sessions (F1). Shares P1, P3, P4, P6, P10, P11, P12.

## Self-ranking
1. ESD: the lens's core (the user's own contribution to the metric is the report), bounded by construction, exact null for any trip count, moves route and time knobs; at ε 2 on Geolife it equals the duration level, its breadth shows at ε 8 and in P6.
2. ORC: the cleanest n = 91 story (one bounded scalar, closed-form PIT and estimator, exact null, MIA-visible, prior plausibly off by ×2), but novelty may score 2–3.
3. UAD as a design layer over ESD and ORC statistics (risk: CLOSE VARIANT of McKenna 2020); MDR last (closest to K1, MIA-blind; its certificate can be a side output of any idea).
