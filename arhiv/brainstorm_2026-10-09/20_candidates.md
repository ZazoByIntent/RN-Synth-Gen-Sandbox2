## Method note
- Every raw idea was tagged with its lens and with the excluded designs it touches (baseline §3, §4, §9). Ideas that repackage an excluded design were dropped; no dropped idea and no candidate revives C4–C10 or K2–K7, so no §3.2 condition is invoked anywhere below.
- Common frame of all six: every train user sends exactly one report at full ε (P11; ⊥ or a public null value without a matched trip); the report is GRR over a small public domain or HM on a publicly bounded user-level number, so the lift lemma X1 gives user-level ε-LDP with no composition; device randomness never depends on data; every class edge, threshold, layer and table is frozen by hash before the first u182 run (F8); the F7 gate keeps the prior unless a pre-registered test rejects; synthesis runs on P3 (Boltzmann walk with a step cap and a shortest-path finish, so routes are road-valid; departure from the public profile in UTC+8; per-segment times by P3's time model, written to the P1 payload).
- Twin-rank rule (applied per candidate): ranking one trip among simulated twins is K1's device. It counts as genuinely different from K1 only if (a) the ranked quantity belongs to a model component that K1 + K8 never rank (K1 ranks duration given the route, its large-n *len* ablation route length given O and D, K8 duration pairs and couplings) and (b) the result enters the released likelihood at n = 91. Tilting the likelihood alone is not new: K1's *len* ablation already adds log g(F_ℓ(ℓ(seq) | O, D)). A PIT that one public shortest-path computation gives exactly needs no twins but still shares K1's exact-null principle, so its novelty must come from elsewhere.
- Numbers were recomputed from the baseline cheat sheet at n = 91: kept when the one-line argument reproduces, corrected where it does not, otherwise marked "unverified". Every "the prior is off by ≥ 2 SD" statement stays unverified until the prior's own value is simulated on the public graph before the freeze.

## T1 Opportunity-rank destination law (core R3-C2; R3-D1, R3-B4)
- **Mechanism.** Destinations follow a public intervening-opportunities law: from origin o, the chance that a trip goes past the s nearest opportunities (by free-flow cost) is (m_o/(m_o + s))^κ, with κ₀ = 1 the parameter-free radiation law (Simini et al. 2012). Each phone places each trip's destination in its origin's opportunity order as a PIT under this law (uniform when the law is right), averages over its trips and sends the mean by HM. The server reads κ off the mean in closed form and draws synthetic destinations from the tempered law; origin, route and times come from P3. It learns how far people reach, never where they go.
- **Report.** Per matched trip (o, d = first and last node): one forward Dijkstra from o on public free-flow cost (≈ 40 ms, scipy); s = opportunity mass strictly closer than d; randomized PIT u = 1 − (m_o/(m_o + s + V·v_d))^κ₀, V ~ U(0, 1) device randomness, v_d the mass at d, m_o the mass within a public radius of o (exactly uniform on the discrete graph under the prior). Mass = road length per node (OSM only) or OSM places (buildings and tagged points snapped to nodes plus one pseudo-place per node, R3-D1). ū = mean u over eligible trips; send HM(2ū − 1) at full ε (one real); no eligible trip → HM(ξ), ξ a public fair ±1 coin. Before P10, trips with straight O–D distance < 200 m are ineligible and u is taken under the prior truncated to eligible destinations, which keeps it exactly uniform (X6). ε 8: one frozen choice between a public user split into AM-peak, PM-peak and off-peak κ_p, or R3-D1's decade of the place rank (GRR over 7) as a shape test.
- **Privacy.** When the user's whole data changes, ū can move anywhere in [0, 1]; HM is ε-LDP for every pair of inputs in [−1, 1]; masses, radius, cost, κ₀, eligibility rule and coin are public and frozen; one report, no composition (X1). The value is a reach quantile, never a place; under the prior its law is the same from every origin.
- **Server and synthesis.** Ū = (1 + mean HM)/2. Gate: every eligible u is uniform under the prior, so E ū = ½ for every user whatever its trip count, origin law or ⊥ status; a pre-registered Hoeffding-level test on the bounded outputs keeps its level. If rejected: κ̂ = κ₀(1/Ū − 1), since E u = κ₀/(κ + κ₀) (with truncation the same monotone map, computed once by public simulation); ⊥ coins only shrink κ̂ toward κ₀; snapped to a public grid. Rank-law variant (R3-D1): P(rank r) ∝ r^−α, α₀ = 0.84 (Noulas et al. 2012). Gravity variant (R3-B4): u = PIT of d's cost under P3's own gravity given o, a sum over the same Dijkstra order (no twins). Synthesis: departure from the public profile, o by road-length mass, d by the tempered law normalized over o's sorted opportunity list, P3 walk and segment times.
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(o) + log P_κ̂(d | o) + Σ log walk steps + public floor, with P_κ̂(d | o) = (m_o/(m_o + s_d))^κ̂ − (m_o/(m_o + s_d + v_d))^κ̂ renormalized over the finite list; one forward Dijkstra per queried origin, cached by node and map hash, shared by the 17 generators, one code path for members and non-members. One private scalar: the MIA sees a population change, nobody's destination.
- **n = 91 vs 10,000.** SD(Ū) = ½ × HM SD = 0.215 / 0.055 / ≈ 0.02 at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8 (at ε 8 mostly between-user spread). SD(κ̂) ≈ κ₀·SD(Ū)/Ū² = 0.22 at κ = 1, ε 2, so κ = 2 (Ū = 1/3) or κ = 0.5 (Ū = 2/3) is ≈ 3 SD away (verified). ε 0.5: prior arm. ε 8: κ ± 0.08 pooled or three κ_p at ≈ ± 0.14 (≈ 30 users each; verified). Decade item: α̂ ± 0.07 at ε 2 (recomputed from the GRR Fisher information; raw 0.065), 0.08–0.09 with a ⊥-plus-loop share of 0.2–0.3, ± 0.36 at ε 0.5. n = 10,000: κ per period × origin-density class (9–12 numbers) or a two-parameter law per period.
- **Merged.** R3-C2 ORC (core: report, closed-form estimator, gate); R3-D1 place rank (place measure, rank-law family, decade item); R3-B4 DRT (gravity variant; motivation that zone shares at ε 8 put rare cells into the likelihood; its 30 twin destinations are unnecessary).
- **Differences.** Round-1 §4: C2 inverts gravity from 3 × 3 zone shares and absolute cost bands of one sampled trip (min KL, Furness); T1 has no zone and no band, a relative rank with an exact null for any origin and trip-count law, all trips averaged, an opportunity law instead of cost deterrence (C2 says where, T1 how far; B.2's band item is the nearest excluded idea). C1's length scale is a route-choice moment, C3 a regime vote. K1 + K8: K1 ranks duration among twins of the route and leaves the route likelihood unchanged at n = 91; K8 contrasts a user's trips. C4 (anchors), C5 (library vote), C6, C8–C10, K2 (iso-time ring), K4 (via points), K5 (modal cell), K6 (radius of gyration, EPR), K3, K7: none ranks the destination in its origin's opportunity order. **Twin-rank check:** a rank is present but not among simulated twins: the PIT is exact from one public forward Dijkstra, the ranked component (destination given origin) is one K1 never ranks, and it enters P(d | o) at n = 91. It shares K1's exact-null principle, so novelty must come from the opportunity law under user-level LDP.
- **Prerequisites.** Shares P0, P1, P3, P4, P5, P6, P11, P12 (P2); P10 strongly advised. New: forward-Dijkstra opportunity cache per origin; for places the OSM semantic export (X14) and a frozen place rule.
- **Risks.** (1) Unsplit sessions: round trips end near their start (u ≈ 0) and pull κ̂ up; truncation keeps the null exact, not the estimand clean. (2) The two public priors disagree strongly (radiation with κ₀ = 1 has a density in opportunity units falling as s^−2, Noulas's rank law as r^−0.84), so the frozen family choice has a large effect; Lenormand et al. 2016 found gravity beats opportunity laws on commuting. (3) At ε 2 the reach may be the walk/vehicle mix again (short walks, X10): head-to-head with C2 on the same users. (4) Novelty 2–3 (published statistic and generator, lesson 1); the origin law stays prior, so Geolife's concentration in Haidian is not moved.
- **Novelty queries.** "radiation model" OR "intervening opportunities" "local differential privacy"; "rank-based" mobility "destination choice" "differential privacy"; "probability integral transform" "local differential privacy" "goodness-of-fit" OR "mean estimation".

## T2 Hierarchy-ceiling route law (core R3-A1; R3-A2 optional)
- **Mechanism.** A contraction hierarchy built once on the frozen OSM graph (free-flow time, deterministic node order) sorts nodes into four bands by rank (B0 bottom 80 %, B1 15 %, B2 4 %, B3 top 1 %). For one of its trips, each phone says whether the route climbed as high in this hierarchy as the trip's own fastest path does, or stayed one or two bands below. The server learns how often trips stay under a "ceiling" and generates routes from a mixture of public routers softly confined to nested sub-networks, so the route law changes while every shortest-path computation stays public.
- **Report.** Device: one eligible trip drawn uniformly (eligible: the free-flow fastest path between the trip's own endpoints has apex a* ≥ B1; route free-flow time ≤ 3 × fastest; guard: at most 30 % of the route length farther than 50 m from any GPS point, so gap fills cannot dominate; loops ⊥ before P10). Apex a(path) = the highest band on which the path runs ≥ 200 m contiguously (an edge's band is the lower band of its end nodes); deficit δ = a* − a(route). Value ∈ {δ ≤ 0, δ = 1, δ ≥ 2, ⊥}: GRR over 4 (2 bits) at full ε. ε 8 and large n: the joint (a*, δ class), 8 cells + ⊥, GRR over 9. Optional ε 8 family by a public user split (R3-A2): the band group {B0, B1–B3} of the route's largest-regret step (regret t_ff(e) + h_D(w) − h_D(v) ≥ 0, which sums exactly to the route's free-flow detour), "no deviation" or ⊥.
- **Privacy.** Trip choice, CH query, apex and deficit are local preprocessing of the user's whole data into one value of a public 4- or 9-element domain; when the data change, the value can become any other, and GRR bounds every output ratio by e^ε (X1). One report, full ε. Public and frozen: node-ordering heuristic, band quantiles, the 200 m rule, eligibility, guard, class edges.
- **Server and synthesis.** Debias; shares among eligible users s_c = ŝ_c/(1 − ŝ_⊥); F7 gate. Ceiling-c router = P3's Boltzmann walk with cost t(e) + Δ·1[band(e) > c] (Δ public, e.g. 300 s per edge above the ceiling; soft, so every O–D pair stays connected), c ∈ {B0, B1, B2, top}. ε ≤ 2: one number ρ = mass of ceilings below "top" (lower ceilings in public fixed proportions), maximum likelihood on the GRR counts with a confusion A(δ | c, a*) simulated once on public O–D pairs, shrunk to the prior's ρ₀ = 0. ε 8 and large n: in the hard-ceiling limit a = min(c, a*), so (a, 1[a = a*]) is a right-censored observation of c with censoring level a*, and the ceiling law π is a Kaplan–Meier product over bands; valid for any trips-per-user law (one uniform trip) if ceilings are independent of a*, otherwise one law per a* class. Option R3-A2: band-specific sharpness β_b = β₀·e^{λ·1[b = B0]}, λ fitted on a public grid from simulated class probabilities. Synthesis: O, D, departure from P3; c ~ π̂; route by the ceiling-c walk (road-valid; after the step cap the ceiling-c shortest path); per-segment times by P3 with public band factors.
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(O, D) + log Σ_c π̂_c Π_k P_c(e_k | v_k, D) + public floor. P_c reads the public per-ceiling cost-to-go: 4 reverse Dijkstras per queried destination (≈ 160 ms with scipy), computed lazily for any destination on one code path, cached by map + config hash, shared by the 17 generators. Private inputs: ≤ 3 weights (and λ) on a public grid; π̂_c ≥ a public floor keeps every sequence finite. The MIA sees the route law change.
- **n = 91 vs 10,000.** ε 0.5: 4-way GRR SD ≈ 0.33 per share (verified), prior arm. ε 2: SD ≈ 0.078 per share near 1/3 (recomputed, p − q = 0.615), ÷ (1 − d) for a share d of users without an eligible trip (guess 0.2–0.4) → 0.10–0.13; one number (the stayed-below share) moves if the prior's share, simulated at its public temperature before any run, is off by ≥ 0.25 (the raw claim that Geolife sits at 0.3–0.5 is unverified). ε 8: SD ≈ √(π(1 − π)/91)/(1 − d) ≈ 0.06–0.08: "one below" vs "two or more below" and the a* law through the joint; A2's band split ± 0.07 (unverified). n = 10,000 (± 0.008 per share at ε 2): one ceiling law per a* class without the independence assumption, × 3 periods (≈ 24 cells by OLH), per-ceiling band speed factors; A2's sharpness by band × progress × period.
- **Merged.** R3-A1 apex ceilings (core); R3-A2 regret ledger (optional ε 8 family; same bands, same public cost-to-go). R3-A4 (barrier sides) is dropped but may return here as a large-n route family.
- **Differences.** Round-1 §4: C1 sends user-averaged class metres and detours as moments and tilts costs privately (a private cost-to-go per destination and generator, lesson 7); T2 sends an ordinal one-trip class and every cost-to-go is public. C3 draws a label from a posterior over complete generators and needs the non-public trips-per-user law (F2); T2's label is a deterministic function of route geometry, its components are nested (censored identification) and only the route law moves, so it could serve as C3's route layer. C8 (A.4; AHEAD, Yang 2020) and C9 report nodes, prefixes or tokens of a public hierarchy (frequency oracles); T2 reports no node. K4 (via points, pieces), C6 (spectral), C10 (flows), A.3 (cordons): no location, basis, flow or cordon. K1 + K8: route space, no time. C4, C5, C7, K2, K3, K5–K7: none touches the route hierarchy. **Twin-rank check:** no twins and no rank: the class is computed deterministically against the trip's own fastest path from one public CH query, so this is not K1's pattern.
- **Prerequisites.** Shares P0, P1, P3, P4, P5, P6, P10, P11 (P2, P12 for evaluation) and P9 (footways and cycleways in the graph). New: the CH and its bands (minutes on 35,764 nodes, cached by map hash) and per-ceiling cost-to-go caches.
- **Risks.** (1) X10: at n = 91 "stayed below" may largely be the walk/bike share that C1 and C3 already move; separation must come from structure (route-only, censored identification, a recovery simulation with ceilings independent of speed regimes) and a head-to-head with C3. (2) Matcher artefacts (X13): gap fills follow shortest paths, which climb the hierarchy, so matched routes look higher than real ones; walkers matched onto arterial carriageways count as "reached". (3) Bands depend on the node-ordering heuristic; if plain OSM classes or Gutman's reach do as well (ablation), the hierarchy is not the contribution. (4) A2's number moves only at ε 8 and may describe the matcher (parallel-street mismatches).
- **Novelty queries.** "contraction hierarchy" OR "highway hierarchy" "differential privacy" route; "local differential privacy" "Kaplan-Meier" OR "right-censored" randomized response; "route choice" "road hierarchy" "latent class" OR "nested" synthetic trajectories privacy.

## T3 Within-trip segment-time contrasts on a public edge covariate (core R3-D3; R3-A3, R3-E1)
- **Mechanism.** Per-segment time (R1) gets a private correction that no planned module learns: how much slower people move on one public kind of segment than on otherwise similar segments of the same trip. Inside each trip the device compares residual log times (observed time over the prior's segment time) of "covariate-high" and "covariate-low" segments a few minutes apart, so the person's mode and speed level cancel, and sends one coarse class of that contrast. The covariate is one public edge property frozen before u182: street frontage lined by shops and markets (R3-D3) or the junction zones where hierarchy bands meet (R3-A3). The server applies the estimated factor to every such segment of every synthetic route and to the router's costs.
- **Report.** Public: covariate x(e) per edge (frontage = share of a 30 m buffer covered by commercial or retail land use or shop/amenity points, high ≥ 0.5, low ≤ 0.1; or junction zone = the 40 m before and after a node where bands meet, else mid-block); prior segment time τ₀(e, t) = P3 free-flow × period factor × cited signal and access adjustments, including the prior's junction delay. Device, over all matched trips: residual ρ(e) = log(t_obs(e)/τ₀(e)) from `matched_points` (entry and exit by interpolation; consecutive edges pooled to ≥ 100 m, R3-E1), only on edges with ≥ 1 matched point inside (no gap fills, X13), outside the first and last 200 m, without a dwell > 60 s (stops are not slowdowns, F1); K = 10 pairs drawn with device randomness (one high, one low edge of the same road-class group in the same trip, entered ≤ 3 min apart); c = median of ρ_high − ρ_low. Value ∈ {c < −0.1, |c| ≤ 0.1, c > 0.1, ⊥ (fewer than 3 distinct pairs)}: GRR over 4 (2 bits) at full ε. ε 8: 6 classes (cut-offs ± 0.1 and ± 0.3 nat), or a public user split between the two covariates. Numeric form (R3-A3): the user-mean junction delay against its own cruising speed, clipped to [0, 120 s], by HM.
- **Privacy.** GRR over a public 4- or 6-element domain (X1) or HM on a publicly bounded user-level mean: when the user's whole data change, the value can become any other in its public domain, and the randomizer bounds every output ratio by e^ε; one report, no split; covariate rule, τ₀, K, pairing rule and cut-offs public and frozen. Locating power 1 by construction (the same value can arise on any route); at ε 8 a near-truthful class reveals a trait ("shop fronts slow me down"), not a place.
- **Server and synthesis.** Debias; pre-registered sign test of P(slower) = P(faster): if the prior is right up to a multiplicative person-and-trip factor, each pair difference is symmetric, so the test is exact for every user, mode and trip count. If it rejects, γ̂ by an ordered probit with the public per-pair noise scale (from P3's jitter), snapped to {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5}. Synthesis: P3 demand, departure and router; τ(e, t) = τ₀(e, t)·exp(γ̂·x(e))·jitter, so times vary along a route with the covariate; the router's Boltzmann cost uses E_t τ, so routes avoid slow covariate segments when γ̂ > 0; per-edge entry times by accumulation (P1). Option (R3-E1, ε 8): four class-wise per-user median speed factors by HM (one publicly drawn coordinate) reset the base τ₀ per road-class group; their two-stage median (trip median, then user median) bounds the influence of long sessions and stops.
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** P3's likelihood with walk costs c_γ(e) = E_t τ(e, t) + public turn delay: log P(O, D) + Σ log Boltzmann steps + gap term; the cost-to-go per queried destination is cached for each of the 5 grid values (≈ 5 × 1,100 × 40 ms ≈ 220 s, public, shared by the 17 generators). Time stays outside the likelihood (SINTEZA §4.7), so the MIA sees γ̂ only through route choice; the numeric junction form alone leaves the likelihood at the prior (MIA-blind, X11).
- **n = 91 vs 10,000.** ε 2: GRR over 4 with p − q = 0.615; the balance P(slower) − P(faster) has SD ≈ 0.13 (recomputed: the two shares covary negatively, so the raw 0.105 was low), ≈ 0.17 with a ⊥ share of 0.3. The raw balance ≈ 0.42 for γ = 0.15 nat (per-pair noise 0.25 nat) is unverified; if right, 2.5–3 SD, while γ = 0.05 nat stays hidden. Numeric junction form: HM SD 0.11 × 60 s ≈ 6.6 s plus between-user spread ≈ ± 7 s (verified); a prior junction delay off by 15–20 s (wide signalised Beijing junctions) is 2–3 SD. ε 0.5: nothing. ε 8: two numbers (γ at peak and off-peak, or frontage and junctions); class-wise factors ± 0.12 nats each (verified: HM 0.05 plus spread 0.104 with 23 users per coordinate). n = 10,000: γ by road-class group × period × covariate (≈ 12 numbers), a public-structure speed field.
- **Merged.** R3-D3 frontage clock (core: paired design, sign classes, exact null); R3-A3 junction ledger (junction covariate and numeric form); R3-E1 stop-proof segment speed (per-edge extraction, dwell exclusion, ε 8 class-wise levels; its pooled ε 2 level is dropped as the X10 mode mix that K1 and C1 already move, with a prior-only likelihood).
- **Differences.** Round-1 §4: C1 sends the user's speed-ratio level (by road group × period in C.1), which at n = 91 measures the walk/vehicle mix (X10, F6); T3's contrast removes the level inside each pair. K1 + K8: K1 calibrates whole-trip duration and scales every segment by one ratio; K8 item a contrasts two different trips (peak vs off-peak) and the excluded item b a moment contrast across trips; T3 contrasts segments inside one trip by a public edge covariate. K3 samples a speed state (a level, stops included); C8/A.4 send a speed bit per hierarchy node (key–value); K2 uses time as a budget; C4–C7, C9, C10, K4–K7 contain no segment times. **Twin-rank check:** no twins and no rank against a simulator; the null is a within-trip symmetry, not a PIT, so this is not K1's pattern.
- **Prerequisites.** Shares P0, P1 (edge entry and exit times, stops), P3, P4, P5, P6, P11; P10 optional (the statistic is stop-proof). New: per-edge time extraction on the device from `matched_points`; the covariate layer (OSM semantic export X14 for frontage, or T2's CH bands for junctions); a segment-level utility metric (residual speed W1 by covariate class), because no P2 or P12 metric reads segment times.
- **Risks.** (1) Benchmark blindness (lesson 6): only a new segment-level metric sees the gain directly; P12 changes little and the MIA sees γ̂ only through router costs. (2) Per-edge times are noisy (points ≥ 5 s apart give a fast vehicle 1–2 points per 80 m zone; matcher snapping), and the ⊥ share may be large. (3) The symmetric null is exact only for a multiplicative time model; the prior's additive turn delays and buses stopping near junctions or shops break it, so the test can reject for the wrong reason. (4) Thin Beijing POI and land-use coverage (frontage); confounding with signals, crossings and parking (harmless for synthesis, not causal).
- **Novelty queries.** "local differential privacy" "travel time" "road segment" paired OR "within-trip"; "local differential privacy" "sign test" OR "paired differences" randomized response; "intersection delay" OR "street frontage" OR "land use" "segment travel time" "differential privacy".

## T4 Semantic trip-end relations (core R3-D2; R3-D4 optional)
- **Mechanism.** A public hierarchy of OSM enclosures (closed polygons of at least 1 ha: residential compounds, education and research campuses, work/commercial/industrial areas, parks; five frozen kinds; every node outside them is "street") lets each phone say how closely the two ends of one of its trips are related: inside one enclosure, in two different enclosures of the same kind, or across kinds. No place and no kind is named. The server turns the shares into two relation constants of the destination law, which produces campus-internal trips and kind-to-kind assortativity that no planned module can produce.
- **Report.** Device: one matched trip drawn uniformly; rel ∈ {internal (O, D and ≥ 80 % of the route length in one enclosure), same kind (two enclosures of one kind), cross (different kinds or any street end), return loop (O and D in one enclosure but the route leaves it: an unsplit session, F1), ⊥}: GRR over 5 at ε ≤ 2. ε 8: rel × kind of D (3 × 5 + loop + ⊥ = 17 values), allowed only if the taxonomy passes R3-D4's admissibility rule (below); sensitive kinds (health care, worship and the other GDPR Art. 9 categories) never form a category.
- **Privacy.** One value of a public 5- or 17-element domain, then GRR at full ε (X1); one report; polygons, kinds, thresholds and the admissibility rule frozen with the OSM export. Locating power (the largest factor by which a truthful report raises the probability that the user's home lies in any one 3 × 3 zone, computed from OSM and the public prior only): the relation fits thousands of enclosures across all nine zones, expected ≤ 2 (to be computed before the freeze). R3-D4's rule for the ε 8 kind: a taxonomy is admissible only if P(z | k)/P(z) ≤ 3 for every kind k and zone z; a failing kind (campuses cluster in Haidian) is merged, which prices utility against home-zone revelation in public.
- **Server and synthesis.** Debias, renormalize over eligible users (drop ⊥, report the loop share), F7 gate. Destination law = P3's gravity tilted by relation constants: P(D | O) ∝ θ_rel(O,D)·m(D)·f₀(c(O, D)), θ_cross = 1, so θ = 1 is exactly the prior; θ_internal and θ_same-kind solve two monotone equations matching the shares implied on a public seeded sample of 2,000 origins (the partial normalisers do not depend on θ, so each Newton step is closed form). ε 8 (R3-D4): kind-layer weights by iterative proportional fitting move trip ends toward the kinds the population uses, at the scale of OSM features. Synthesis: departure from the public profile, O from P3's origin law, D from the tilted law, P3 walk (internal trips use internal service and foot edges where present, P9), P3 segment times; loops only as an option.
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(O) + log θ_rel(O,D) + log m(D) + log f₀(c(O, D)) − log Σ_r θ_r Z_r(O) + walk + gap, with Z_r(O) the gravity mass of nodes in relation r to O: three public partial normalisers from one forward Dijkstra per queried origin, cached by map and hierarchy hash, shared by the 17 generators; two private scalars on a public grid; finite because every D has exactly one relation to O and P3's floor applies. The MIA sees P(D | O) change.
- **n = 91 vs 10,000.** ε 2: GRR over 5 (p − q = 0.561), each share ± 0.075 near 0.2 (verified), ÷ (1 − d) for a ⊥ share d. P3's gravity puts ≈ 1–3 % of trips inside one enclosure (unverified until simulated); under the null the internal share has SD ≈ 0.056, so a true share of 0.15 is ≈ 2.3 SD and 0.2 ≈ 3 SD (verified); the same-kind constant moves only if the prior is ≥ 0.15 off. ε 0.5: nothing (SD ≥ 0.28). ε 8: shares ± 0.03–0.045; the 17-value item gives kind-specific internal shares and the diagonal of a kind-to-kind matrix. ## T5 Metric-gradient report (core R3-C1 statistic; R3-C3 direction design)
- **Mechanism.** Every phone sends one number: the directional derivative, at the public simulator's parameters θ₀, of its own average energy score along one public direction w in knob space. The energy score is a proper scoring rule whose expectation is the energy distance between simulated and real trips (in one dimension the Cramér distance, a W1-like distance), so the report is the user's own contribution to how the benchmark distance would change if the simulator moved along w. The server takes one damped Newton step from θ₀ with a public Hessian. The direction w is either a publicly drawn knob coordinate (R3-C1's default) or the single direction that a public pre-computation shows will most reduce the expected benchmark loss (R3-C3's metric-adjoint design), so the question each user answers is derived from the benchmark's own metrics, the open angle of lens R3-C.
- **Report.** Public: P3 at θ₀ with d knobs from a frozen n·ε² table (d ≤ 2 at ε ≤ 2: log speed level, distance decay; d ≤ 8 at ε 8: plus peak factor, jitter scale, departure shift, route temperature); attribute map a(trip) = P2/P12 attributes on public scales, clipped to public ranges (log duration, log length, log mean speed, departure cos/sin, period). Device, per matched trip y with context (origin node, departure period): R = 16–32 simulator replicates for that context from a public replicate bank per origin zone; per-trip gradient g(y) = ∂_θ ES_R(θ; y) at θ₀ (pathwise for time knobs, a baseline-subtracted score-function estimate for destination and route knobs); average over own trips ḡ_u; project r_u = wᵀḡ_u and clip to a public bound; send HM(r_u) at full ε (one real). For a location knob on one attribute g(y) = 1 − 2·PIT(y) exactly, so |g| ≤ 1 with no clip box. Users without a matched trip send HM(0).
- **Privacy.** r_u is a locally randomized function of the user's whole data with a public range for any trip count; when the data change, r_u can move anywhere in that range and HM bounds every output ratio by e^ε; w, θ₀, the replicate bank and the clip bound are public and frozen (w computed before the freeze from OSM, cited knob ranges and the metric definitions only, F8); one report (X1). At ε 8 users are split across directions by a public draw, never ε.
- **Server and synthesis.** Debias the HM mean along w. Gate: properness gives E g(y) = 0 for every trip under the null "real = prior", so E r_u = 0 for every trip count and for users without trips; a pre-registered Hoeffding-level test on the bounded outputs keeps its level (exact only for unclipped gradients; score-function knobs need a clip box, which makes it approximate). If rejected: θ̂ = θ₀ − λ·H⁻¹ĝ along w (public damping λ, public box), H the public Hessian of the expected energy score at θ₀ by simulation, cached by map + config hash. R3-C3 option: w maximizes the expected loss reduction wᵀΣ₀JΣ₀w/(wᵀΣ₀w + σ_n²), with Σ₀ a public misspecification prior from cited knob ranges, J the Hessian of the weighted P2 + P12 loss (squared Cramér as the W1 surrogate) and σ_n² the HM variance at the public n and ε; the update is then a Kalman step. Synthesis = P3 at θ̂: origin by road-length mass, destination by gravity with the estimated decay, Boltzmann-walk route (road-valid), departure from the public profile, segment times from the time knobs (P1 payload).
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** The simulator's exact route likelihood at θ̂: log P(o) + log P_θ̂(d | o) + Σ log walk steps(θ̂) + floor; time absent. It moves only when w loads on decay or route knobs (then the MIA sees ≤ 8 population numbers); when w loads only on time knobs the likelihood is the prior's, which the frozen design table must state (X11). Reads only θ̂ and map/config caches.
- **n = 91 vs 10,000.** Location knob on log duration with prior spread σ: H = 1/(√π·σ) (verified for a Gaussian predictive), so SD(Δθ̂) = SD(ĝ)·√π·σ: ε 0.5 0.43 → 0.76σ (prior kept unless off by ≥ 1.5σ); ε 2 0.11 → 0.20σ (verified), a gain if the level is off by ≥ 0.4σ; d = 2 at 0.15 → 0.27σ each. ε 8: d = 8 at 0.047–0.13 per coordinate → 0.08–0.23σ per knob if H is well conditioned (level and peak factor are not). Designed direction: if the misfit is one-dimensional in statistic space, w gathers it at the noise of one number, up to K times lower variance than sampling one of K coordinates (unverified; it depends entirely on Σ₀). n = 10,000 (SD × 0.095): 10–25 knobs at ε 1–2, including per-period speed factors and decays and road-class tastes. On Geolife: speed level and reach at ε 2; the rest only in P6.
- **Merged.** R3-C1 ESD (statistic, bounded gradient, gate, Newton step); R3-C3 UAD (designed direction, Kalman update; its catalogue of centred residuals, e.g. T1's opportunity PIT, can enter as extra coordinates of ḡ).
- **Differences.** Round-1 §4: C1 sends one uniformly drawn coordinate of user-averaged centred moments and fits by MaxEnt moment matching; its raw source B.1 already sent user-averaged recursive-logit score coordinates by PM and took one Bayesian Newton step, so R3-C1 alone differs from B.1 by its loss (energy score instead of log-likelihood: bounded for location knobs, likelihood-free, time knobs included), one detail in lesson 1's sense; the candidate's claim rests on R3-C3's derivation of the question from the benchmark metrics. C7 (C.4) is zero-order (a likelihood-gain bit at a server-set knob, then a surface fit; CLOSE VARIANT of LDP Bayesian optimization); T5 is first-order at one public point. C3 and C5 are votes; C4, C6, C8–C10 unrelated. K1 + K8: for one location knob, one trip and three bins, T5's per-trip quantity is K1's PIT; T5 averages all trips (X2), sends a number by HM, moves several knobs including route ones, and takes a Newton step instead of a quantile map; K8 contrasts trips. K7 has the device fit its own model and answer a stated-choice scenario; T5 sends a derivative of the benchmark distance, never a self-fit. K2–K6: unrelated. **Twin-rank check:** present: the R replicates are twins of the trip's own context, and for a location knob the per-trip gradient is exactly 1 − 2 × K1's rank. It is genuinely different from K1 only in the multi-knob form (destination and route knobs, which enter the likelihood) and in the designed direction; at n = 91 and ε 2 it moves what K1 *dur* moves.
- **Prerequisites.** Shares P1, P2 (the metric code is needed before the freeze to compute J), P3, P4, P5, P6, P10, P11, P12. New: the public replicate bank per origin zone (cached with P3), the public Hessian and response surface, the design computation (Σ₀, J).
- **Risks.** (1) Novelty: R3-C1 alone is B.1 (→ C1) with another loss; R3-C3 alone may be a CLOSE VARIANT of McKenna et al. 2020 (workload-optimal LDP strategies for linear queries) or "a design rule, not a mechanism". (2) At n = 91 and ε 2 it moves the same duration level and reach as K1, C1 and C3 (lesson 2, X10). - **Novelty queries.** "local differential privacy" "scoring rule" OR "energy score" gradient calibration; "local differential privacy" workload optimal strategy "Bayesian experimental design" OR "nonlinear"; "simulation-based inference" OR "likelihood-free" "local differential privacy" one-step estimator.

## T6 Absolute-threshold duration CDF (core R3-E2; R3-C4 release rule and certificate)
- **Mechanism.** The thesis metric P12 (duration W1 within each departure period) is estimated directly. A public draw assigns each phone one departure period and one absolute duration threshold (for example 30 minutes), and the phone answers whether one of its trips in that period was shorter. The server fits the monotone duration CDF per period by isotonic regression under the known randomized-response noise (the current-status estimator) and quantile-matches the simulator's durations to it. Absolute thresholds have no prior pivot, so the answers cannot saturate when the prior's spread is wrong (K1's red-team finding, lesson 4). Optional (R3-C4): release the per-period law that minimizes the posterior expected W1 and publish a certified W1 bound.
- **Report.** Public draw (period p, threshold t_j): periods = the P12 departure periods (all day at ε ≤ 2 and n = 91); thresholds a frozen log-spaced grid of absolute constants (10, 30, 90 min at n = 91; J = 6 at large n), optionally placed by R3-C4's W1 weights computed from the public prior. Device: one matched trip departing in p, drawn uniformly with device randomness; x ∈ {≤ t_j, > t_j, ⊥ (no matched trip in p)}; GRR over 3 at full ε. At ε 8 the frozen n·ε² rule switches the same family to the clipped log duration of that trip by HM (a one-bit item cannot beat the Bernoulli floor at ε 8).
- **Privacy.** Period, threshold and item type are drawn publicly and independently of data; the trip choice uses device randomness (X1); GRR or HM at full ε on one value; one report, no split. R3-B drift, stated openly: at ε 8 the HM value is one trip's duration with per-user noise ≈ 0.16 of the half-range (≈ 0.24 nats for a ± 1.5-nat box; the raw "0.03 nats per user" is the SD of the mean), a weakly identifying per-user value.
- **Server and synthesis.** Per period, the debiased share of "≤" among eligible users at each threshold; pool-adjacent-violators across thresholds gives the monotone F̂(t_j | p) (current-status NPMLE, Groeneboom & Wellner 1992, here with known randomized-response noise). Gate (F7): deviance of the raw table against the table F_prior(t_j | p) predicts through the randomizer, critical value simulated in advance; exact under the null for any trip count because one trip is sampled. If rejected: quantile matching y = F̂⁻¹(F_prior(x | p)) with log-linear interpolation and the prior's tails outside the grid; all segment times of the route are scaled by y/x. R3-C4 option: release G_rel = the pointwise posterior median of the CDF (the Bayes act for W1, monotone automatically; Gibbs data augmentation over ≤ 10 GRR symbols) and the posterior (1 − δ) quantile of W1(release, truth) per period as a certificate. Origin, destination, route and departure come from P3.
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** P3's walk likelihood (SINTEZA §4.7); time is absent, so the MIA sees the prior at every ε (X11, stated); the certificate, not the MIA, is the evidence. Only a large-n *len* family at public length thresholds would move the likelihood, which is K1's ablation and not claimed.
- **n = 91 vs 10,000.** ε 2, all day, J = 3 (≈ 30 users per threshold): GRR over 3 with p − q = 0.68, F̂ at each threshold ± 0.13 (verified: √(0.25/30)/0.68); the gate rejects only if the prior's median duration is off by about a factor 2.5 (unverified), coarser than K1's ± 0.073 three-class shift, so the gain is robustness to a badly wrong prior, not resolution. ε 0.5: ≈ ± 0.5 per threshold (recomputed; the raw 0.75 is unverified), nothing. ε 8 (HM): a deconvolved CDF of 91 noisy one-trip durations, roughly ± 0.05–0.08 pointwise for one or two periods (unverified; the raw DKW band ± 0.14 assumes exact values and is optimistic). n = 10,000: 4 periods × 9 thresholds at ε 2, ± 0.044 per level (verified: √(0.25/278)/0.68), full per-period quantile functions and a tight certificate.
- **Merged.** R3-E2 current-status CDF (core); R3-C4 MDR (only the W1-weighted threshold placement, the Bayes or minimax release and the certificate; its own report, a PIT class under the prior's period-marginal law, is K1 *dur* with another pivot and is not used).
- **Differences.** Round-1 §4: C1 matches moments (including a speed ratio); C2 and C3 do not estimate the duration law directly; none is quantile-based. K1 + K8: K1 asks a relative rank among twins with prior-equal classes (an exact null that saturates when the prior's spread is wrong) and applies a density tilt; T6 asks an absolute threshold, estimates a monotone CDF and quantile-matches; K8 contrasts trips. K2 uses durations as a time budget, K3 samples speed states; C4–C10, K4–K7: unrelated. Published neighbours: the adaptive one-report quantile methods (Aamand 2025, Liu & Hu 2026, Penso 2025, Joseph 2019) choose thresholds from earlier cohorts; T6 is one non-adaptive round, and whether "random public threshold + RR + isotonic fit" is already their baseline or in Cormode et al. 2019 (LDP range and CDF queries) is the novelty checker's first query. **Twin-rank check:** no twins and no relative rank; the threshold is an absolute public constant, so this is not K1's pattern (R3-C4's own report was K1's pattern and is dropped).
- **Prerequisites.** Shares P1 (timed payload for synthesis), P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P11, P12; P10 decides which durations P12 compares. New: the threshold grid and its W1-weighted placement; the posterior sampler for the certificate.
- **Risks.** (1) Resolution below K1 at n = 91 and ε 2; it may never reject the prior on Geolife. (2) MIA-blind (lesson 6); the gain shows only in P12 and duration W1. (3) Novelty: the non-adaptive random-threshold baseline may be published. (4) Two code paths by ε (bit vs HM), and at ε 8 a near-raw one-trip duration per user. (5) Metric gaming: matching P12 by time scaling alone distorts speed W1 when long durations come from longer routes; durations keep stops (F1); the estimate is user-weighted while P12 is trip-weighted (X4, X9).
- **Novelty queries.** "local differential privacy" "current status" OR "isotonic regression" distribution function; "local differential privacy" "cumulative distribution function" "random threshold" one bit; "differentially private" synthetic data "Wasserstein" "accuracy guarantee" OR certified travel time.
