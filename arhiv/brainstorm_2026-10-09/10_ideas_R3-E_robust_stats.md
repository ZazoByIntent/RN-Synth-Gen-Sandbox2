# Round 3, brainstormer R3-E: robust statistics (wildcard)

Date 2026-10-09. Lens R3-E of `01_task.md` §4; `00_baseline.md` §9 (R3-E) and §10 read. Four ideas, all user-level pure ε-LDP with one report per user, all synthesising through the public simulator P3. They differ in where the robustness sits: idea 1 in the *statistic* (a per-user median over road segments), idea 2 in the *estimator* (one-round CDF from public random thresholds; combines with lens R3-C), idea 3 in the *server* (a manipulation-certified gate and a breakdown rule for the release; combines with lens R3-B), idea 4 in the *weights* (the trips-per-user law estimated inside the report; bounded Hájek weights). Taken items I differ from throughout: K1 + K8 and K1's sequential form, R2-D.1, adaptive one-report quantiles (Aamand 2025, Liu & Hu 2026, Penso 2025), the user-level templates (Acharya–Liu–Sun 2023, Kent 2024, Zhao 2024), C1's clipped boxes and X4's caps. Noise numbers use the baseline §1 cheat sheet at n = 91.

## Idea 1 — Stop-proof segment speed: the per-user median edge-time factor

1. **Pitch.** Each user reports one clipped number, the median over its traversed road segments of log(observed segment time / OSM free-flow time); the server turns it into per-road-class speed factors that set the per-segment times and re-rank routes, and the statistic is immune to stops inside unsplit sessions by construction (F1), with no stay split needed.
2. **Report.** On the device, for every matched trip and every edge e in `edge_seq`: traversal time t_obs(e) from `matched_points` (entry and exit by interpolation; consecutive edges pooled to ≥ 100 m by a public rule), r_e = log(t_obs(e) / t_ff(e)) with t_ff = length / (maxspeed or class default). Trip value = median over its edges; user value s_u = median over its trips (two-stage, so one long trip or one hour-long stop on one edge cannot move it; defined for any count m_u ≥ 1). Clip to the public box θ₀ ± a with a = 1.5 nats and θ₀ = log of a cited Beijing mean speed over a typical maxspeed (S1). At ε ≤ 2 one number (pooled); at ε 8 a vector of four class-wise medians (motorway–primary, secondary–tertiary, residential, foot and cycle paths if they are in the graph, P9) plus the pooled one, with a public draw picking one coordinate. HM at full ε. Domain: one real in [−1, 1] after the affine map, i.e. one HM output. Users without matched trips send HM(θ₀) (P11).
3. **Privacy.** s_u is a publicly bounded function of all the user's trips (box, pooling rule and class map are public and frozen by hash, F8); the coordinate is chosen by a public data-independent draw; HM at ε on that one value is ε-LDP; one report, so no composition; everything after is post-processing (the SINTEZA §4.6 template). No ε split.
4. **Server.** Debias, shrink toward θ₀ by a frozen factor, gate (F7: the report histogram against the one the prior predicts through HM). θ̂ pooled (ε 2) or per class (ε 8). At ε 8 the HM noise (0.03 nats) is far below the walk/vehicle gap (about 2 nats), so the server fits a two-component between-user mixture of the medians (walk-like, vehicle-like) with trimmed means per component. Synthesis (P3): origin, destination and departure from the public prior; route = Boltzmann walk toward the destination with the cost-to-go computed on the estimated times c(e) = t_ff(e) · exp(θ̂_class(e)); segment time = c(e) × public jitter + public turn delay; at ε 8 a per-trip component draw gives the bimodal duration law. No stop model unless P10 or a cited dwell constant is added.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** log P(O, D) from the prior gravity + Σ log P(step | cost-to-go under θ̂) + the public floor for gaps and unreachable destinations; time absent; θ̂ snapped to a 0.05-nat public grid before caching. At ε 2 the pooled factor cancels in relative step costs, so the likelihood equals the prior's (visibility weakness X11, stated plainly); at ε 8 the class-wise factors change which routes are cheap and the likelihood moves.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** ε 0.5: HM SD 0.43 × 1.5 = 0.65 nats, nothing (the gate keeps the prior). ε 2: pooled θ̂ ± 0.17 nats (0.11 × 1.5 HM noise plus a 0.5/√91 between-user spread); a plausible prior error — cited peak car speed about 22 km/h against a maxspeed of 55 (−0.9 nats) plus a walking share at −2.2 — exceeds 1 nat, i.e. more than 5 SD. ε 8: four class factors ± 0.12 nats each (HM 0.05 plus spread 0.105 with 23 users per coordinate) and a mixture weight ± 0.052. Only at n = 10,000: 4 classes × 3 departure periods = 12 factors ± 0.06 nats at ε 2 (HM, d = 12) and the mixture by period.
7. **Closest work and differences.** Rameshwar et al. 2024 (user-level central DP mean bus speeds with unequal samples per user: central, a mean, no road graph, no synthesis); Bian et al. 2024 (per-mode durations and distances, central, clipping); floating-car speed estimation uses segment medians, non-privately. Versus round 1 §4: C1 sends a clipped per-trip *mean* speed ratio and moment-matches taste parameters; here the statistic is a per-edge *median*, stop-immune, and it feeds segment times and route costs directly. Versus K1 + K8: no twins, no rank, no calibration test as the core; the per-user median's null is not count-free (F2) and is not claimed to be. Versus K3: a clock-sampled speed state is weighted by time spent, so stops dominate it; here stops are a minority of edges. Versus A.4/C8: no hierarchy oracle, no speed bit per node. Shares P1 (edge entry times), P3, P4, P11.
8. **Risk.** Short-edge GPS noise and matcher snapping bias the per-edge ratios systematically, and a median removes random but not systematic error; if the benchmark's reference durations keep stops, a stop-free synthesizer loses duration W1 even when its speeds are right (needs P10 or a public dwell constant); at ε 2 the gain is the X10 mode-mix level again and the likelihood is prior-only.

## Idea 2 — Current-status duration CDF: one bit at a public random threshold, isotonic fit, quantile matching (R3-E with R3-C)

1. **Pitch.** The thesis metric P12 (duration W1 within each departure period) is estimated directly: each user answers whether one of its trips in a publicly drawn period is shorter than a publicly drawn duration threshold; the server fits the monotone CDF by isotonic regression (the current-status estimator) and quantile-matches the simulator's durations to it, with no prior pivot and therefore no saturation (lesson 4).
2. **Report.** A public draw gives the user a pair (period p, threshold t_j): periods = the P12 departure periods (all day only at ε ≤ 2 and n = 91); thresholds a frozen log-spaced grid 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160 min (J = 6; at n = 91 J = 3: 10, 30, 90 min), absolute public constants that encode nothing about Geolife (F8). The device picks one matched trip departing in p uniformly (local randomness) and answers x ∈ {≤ t_j, > t_j, ⊥} (⊥ = no matched trip in p); GRR over 3 at full ε. At ε 8 the frozen n·ε² rule switches the same family to the clipped log-duration of that trip by HM, because a one-bit item cannot beat the Bernoulli floor at ε 8 (cheat sheet). Variant: the user's *median* duration instead of one sampled trip (bounded influence of long sessions; the location null is exact for any trip count under a symmetric log law, the spread is not).
3. **Privacy.** Period, threshold and item type are drawn publicly and data-independently; the trip choice uses local randomness that never leaves the device (lift lemma X1); the answer is GRR (or HM) at full ε on one value in a public domain; one report, no split.
4. **Server.** Per period: the debiased share of "≤" among eligible users (divide by 1 − ŝ_⊥) at each threshold; pool-adjacent-violators across thresholds gives the monotone F̂(t_j | p), the nonparametric maximum-likelihood estimate for current-status data (Groeneboom and Wellner 1992), here with known randomized-response noise. Gate (F7): deviance of the raw report table against the table that the prior's F_prior(t_j | p) predicts through the randomizer, critical value simulated in advance; exact under the null for any trip count because one trip is sampled. If rejected: quantile matching — a simulated duration x in period p becomes y = F̂⁻¹(F_prior(x | p)) with log-linear interpolation between grid points and the prior's tails outside the grid; all segment times of that route are scaled by y/x. Origin, destination, route and departure come from P3.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** The prior walk likelihood of SINTEZA §4.7; time absent, so the MIA sees the prior at every ε (X11 weakness, stated); at n = 10,000 a `len` family at public random length thresholds adds log g(F_ℓ(ℓ(seq) | O, D)) as in K1's large-n ablation, and only then does the likelihood move.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** ε 0.5: GRR-3 has p − q = 0.18, per-level SD about 0.75, nothing. ε 2, all day, J = 3 (about 30 users per level): F̂ at each level ± 0.13 after pooling; the gate rejects only if the prior's median duration is off by roughly a factor 2.5 — coarser than K1's ± 0.073 three-class shift, said honestly; the gain is robustness to a badly wrong prior, not resolution. ε 8 (HM values, noise 0.03 nats per user): the empirical CDF of 91 near-exact one-trip durations, pointwise ± 0.05 all day or ± 0.075 for two periods, DKW 95 % band ± 0.14 — a full duration law per period, the first direct P12 estimate. Only at n = 10,000: 4 periods × 9 thresholds at ε 2 with ± 0.044 per level, and the median variant's between-user spread.
7. **Closest work and differences.** Aamand et al. 2025, Liu and Hu 2026, Penso et al. 2025 and Joseph et al. 2019 (all taken) are adaptive or sequential: thresholds are chosen from earlier cohorts; here one non-adaptive round with random public thresholds and the isotonic estimator — whether Aamand's non-adaptive baseline is exactly "random threshold + RR" must be the novelty checker's first query. K1 asks a *relative* rank among twins with prior-equal classes (an exactly uniform null that saturates when the prior's spread is wrong) and applies a density tilt g(F_prior); here an *absolute* threshold, a monotone CDF estimate and monotone quantile matching. C1/A.1 (moment matching), C2/B.2 (minimum KL), CALM, LoPub and Calibrate are not quantile-based; K2's prisms use durations as a budget, not as the estimand. Shares P1, P3, P4, P11, P12.
8. **Risk.** Users are spread thin over thresholds, so at n = 91 and ε 2 the design sits below K1 in resolution and may never reject the prior on Geolife; two code paths (bit versus HM) by ε; a one-trip HM value at ε 8 is nearly the raw duration of one trip (weakly identifying — an R3-B drift to call out); durations keep stops (F1), which matches today's reference but not the reference after P10.

## Idea 3 — Certified gate: manipulation-robust aggregation and a breakdown rule for the release (R3-E with R3-B)

1. **Pitch.** At n = 91 a handful of fake users can flip the "keep the prior / recalibrate" decision of any gated mechanism; the server therefore estimates by median-of-means over public user groups, raises the gate's critical value by the worst-case shift that B fake reports can cause, and releases a category or parameter only if its mass cannot have come from B or fewer users — a certificate that no small group steers the released model, with the LDP guarantee untouched.
2. **Report.** Nothing new leaves the device: the host report is idea 1's HM coordinate (numbers) or any ≤ 9-way GRR item (idea 2 or idea 4). Counted as a mechanism because the estimator and the release rule change what `fit` outputs and what the likelihood contains.
3. **Privacy.** Unchanged: the per-user randomizer is the host's ε-LDP primitive; grouping, trimming, floors and the raised critical value are post-processing on reports. The public random partition of the train users into G groups is seeded from the frozen config before the first report (as K1's cohort partition, X1 conditions). Robustness is a property of the estimator, never a privacy claim.
4. **Server.** (a) Influence arithmetic: for GRR, B fake reports move any debiased share by at most B/(n(p − q)), which equals about 2B/√n standard deviations whatever ε is (ε cancels): at n = 91 five fakes are about 1 SD and ten about 2 SD, enough to flip a 5 % gate; for HM at ε 2 one fake moves the mean by up to C·a/n ≈ 0.036 nats (C = 2.16, a = 1.5). (b) Median-of-means: estimate per group, release the median across groups (coordinate-wise for share vectors, then renormalize); tolerates ⌊(G − 1)/2⌋ fakes in any pattern. (c) Certified gate: the pre-registered test keeps the prior unless its statistic exceeds the simulated critical value plus the maximal B-fake shift, in both directions (fakes forcing a recalibration and fakes suppressing one). (d) Breakdown floor: a category or parameter enters the released likelihood or the synthesis only if its estimate exceeds B/(n(p − q)) + 2 SD; otherwise it is merged into the prior's mass. Synthesis as the host (P3 with the robust parameters).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** The host's (idea 1: class-wise costs; idea 4: a regime mixture), with the breakdown floor guaranteeing that every parameter or category in the likelihood is supported by more than B users — the explicit form of the R3-B rule "every released category carries prior mass from many users", which the baseline §9 marks as untested.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** Price of (b): the median of G roughly normal group means costs √(π/2) ≈ 1.25 in SD (G = 7 tolerates 3 fakes, G = 11 five); a GRR-3 share becomes ± 0.091 instead of ± 0.073 at ε 2, the equivalent of losing 33 of 91 users. Price of (c): a power loss equal to a 1-SD smaller effect for B = 5 and no variance cost — the recommended form at n = 91. Floor (d) at ε 8 with B = 5: 0.055 + 0.105 = 0.16, so a 9-cell item keeps only cells above 16 %. At n = 10,000: G = 101 tolerates 50 fakes at the same 1.25 price and the floor is 0.015. Nothing of this is visible on Geolife; the evidence is a poisoning arm in the recovery simulation (S2): b ∈ {0, 2, 5, 10} fake users with both attacker goals, measuring gate flips and parameter error against the unprotected estimator.
7. **Closest work and differences.** Cao, Jia and Gong 2021 (poisoning attacks on kRR/OUE/OLH; defenses by normalization and fake-user detection from output patterns) and Cheu, Smith and Ullman 2021 (manipulation is inherent, shifts of order B/(n·ε)): I neither detect fakes nor prevent manipulation but certify the decision and the released model; Yin et al. 2018 (Byzantine-robust coordinate-wise median and trimmed mean for distributed gradients: no randomizer, no debiasing, no gate); Dwork and Lei 2009 use robust statistics to lower sensitivity in central DP, the opposite direction; LDPGuard (Huang et al. 2024) is detection. Versus round 1 §4 and K1 + K8: both gates are unprotected, and K1's "the test rejects" decision is exactly what B fakes flip. Not C7 (no optimization loop), not R2-D.3 (no validator cohort), not D.3's duel. Shares P3, P4, P6.
8. **Risk.** Judged an estimator add-on rather than a mechanism, since the benchmark has no poisoner; the 1.25 variance price of median-of-means at n = 91 may exceed its benefit, which is why the certified gate is the primary form; a reviewer may cite Cheu–Smith–Ullman and call certification trivial; the breakdown floor coarsens ε 8 releases.

## Idea 4 — Count law inside the report: a (count class × content class) item with bounded Hájek weights

1. **Pitch.** The trips-per-user law is not public (X5, F2), yet every trip-weighted estimand and C3's confusion matrix need it; here each user's single 9-way item carries its matched-trip count class together with its content class, so the server estimates the count law, the user-weighted and the capped trip-weighted content shares and their dependence, with no census cohort and an explicit cap-bias versus noise trade-off.
2. **Report.** κ_u ∈ {1–3, 4–15, ≥ 16} matched trips (geometric edges 4^k, fixed by rule before any look at Geolife, F8) × c_u ∈ 3 content classes: the band of the user's median edge-time factor of idea 1 (walk-like < −1.5 nats, slow vehicle −1.5 to −0.5, near free-flow > −0.5; bounds from cited walking and Beijing speeds); users without matched trips send the public ⊥ cell (κ = 0, a 10-way domain). GRR over 9 (or 10) at full ε; one report.
3. **Privacy.** The pair (κ_u, c_u) is a deterministic, publicly defined function of the user's whole data into a public finite domain; GRR at ε on it is ε-LDP; one report, no split. The count class is part of the protected value, not metadata, and F3 holds because every train user reports.
4. **Server.** Debias the 9 cells; the marginals give the count law π̂_κ and the user-weighted content shares; the Hájek estimate of the trip-weighted share uses bounded weights w_κ = min(representative count of κ, M)/M with the public cap M = 16 (w = 2/16, 8/16, 1), normalized over users — the cap bounds any heavy user's influence (the Kish n_eff reasoning of X4) at a bias that the public prior's count law quantifies; the joint tells whether frequent travellers travel differently. The count law feeds the confusion-matrix deconvolution of C3-type votes (F2's fix) and the effective n of every gate. Synthesis (P3): regime shares = trip-weighted content shares; each synthetic trip draws a regime, then a speed factor per road class from that regime's public constants (as idea 1 at ε 8), a route by Boltzmann walk on the regime's costs, departure from the public profile, segment times = cost × jitter.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** log Σ_k π̂_k P_k(seq) over the 3 regimes, with P_k the prior walk under regime k's costs (the mixture form of SINTEZA §4.7 for C3); weights snapped to a public 0.01 grid; finite via the floor.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** 9-way GRR at ε 2: p − q = 0.415, joint cells ± 0.079 near 1/9 (noise level, the dependence is invisible), marginals ± 0.12 near 1/3 — worse than a direct 3-way item (± 0.073) and than a two-cohort split (± 0.103), the honest price of asking the joint. ε 8: marginals ± 0.05, joint cells ± 0.033, so a dependence of 0.07 or more between count class and content shows. ε 0.5: nothing. Only at n = 10,000: cells ± 0.0075 at ε 2, a full 9-cell joint and a count law by period.
7. **Closest work and differences.** R2-D.2 Census-first (a separate cohort of about 20 users reports a count bit; dropped as a prerequisite): here no cohort and no √c penalty, the count rides inside the content item; F2 suggested "a count class in the same item" without an estimator — the bounded Hájek weights and the dependence are the addition; Acharya–Liu–Sun 2023 and Kent 2024 assume a known, equal m per user; Rameshwar 2024 and Bian 2024 handle unequal counts under central DP by clipping; PCKV pads and samples with a public cap. Versus round 1 §4: C3 draws a label from a posterior over complete generators and needs a public count law for its confusion matrix; here the label is a deterministic robust band and the count law arrives with it. Versus K1 + K8: ranks are count-free by design and never estimate the law. Shares P1, P3, P4, P11.
8. **Risk.** The matched-trip count measures logging behaviour and the roughly 10 % match rate, not travel (lesson 5), so "frequent travellers" is a pipeline class; at ε 2 the joint is pure noise and the marginals pay 1.6×; the count classes are hard to freeze without the suspicion that "9 trips per user" informed them (F8); the mixture likelihood is C3's form, so the evaluator may file the idea as C3 plus F2.

## Self-ranking

1. Idea 1 (stop-proof segment speed): the only one whose statistic is new on both the time side (the per-segment time factor is the report) and the route side (class-wise costs at ε 8), with a plausible > 5 SD prior error at ε 2; main risk: the benchmark reference keeps stops.
2. Idea 3 (certified gate): the cheapest to add to any gated design and the only one with a structural claim no earlier round made (no B or fewer users steer the release); but invisible on Geolife and an estimator by nature.
3. Ideas 2 (current-status CDF) and 4 (count law in the report): resolution below K1 at ε 2 and n = 91; their value lies at ε 8 and n = 10,000 (idea 2 as the first direct P12 estimate, idea 4 as F2's fix with an estimator).
