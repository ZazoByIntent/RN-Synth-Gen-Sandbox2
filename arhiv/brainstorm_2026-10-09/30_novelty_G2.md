### Closest works
1. Roth & Avella-Medina, "Differential Privacy with Dependent Data", arXiv 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18583 (baseline). Does: in §6 "Extensions to Local Differential Privacy" each user, under user-level LDP, projects its own per-user OLS coefficient (Cor. 6.10, longitudinal linear regression) or its user mean (Cor. 6.8) onto an interval located by a randomized-response histogram (ε/2) and adds Laplace noise (ε/2); the server averages. Difference: a numeric report with an ε split and a data-located interval; a per-user regression over all records, with no within-trip pairing and no exact null; generic statistics, no road graph, no synthesis. T3 sends one coarse sign class of a within-trip matched-pair median by GRR at full ε over a frozen domain. Note: the baseline row says "no within-user contrast", but a per-user OLS slope on a covariate is a within-user effect, so this is T3's nearest statistical twin.
2. Sopa, Avella-Medina & Rush, "Differentially Private Inference for Longitudinal Linear Regression", arXiv 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10626 (baseline). Does: user-level DP by aggregating per-user ("local") regressions, with finite-sample and asymptotic inference, trusted curator. Difference: central and numeric; same estimand family (an average within-user effect), no local randomizer. User-level LDP regression also exists in Zhao et al. 2024 (baseline) and Ma, Jia & Yang, "Better Locally Private Sparse Estimation Given Multiple Samples Per User", ICML 2024, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ma24c.html (no within-unit contrast).
3. Kalinin & Steinberger, "Efficient Estimation of a Gaussian Mean with Local Differential Privacy", arXiv 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04840. Does: the sign mechanism (randomized response on the sign of X_i minus a centre), efficient for ε ≤ 1, two stages with a user split to locate the centre. Difference: i.i.d. scalars and an adaptive centre; T3 centres at the exact null 0 (no locating stage), adds a "near zero" class and ⊥, and recovers γ by an ordered probit with a public noise scale, the same threshold-inversion idea as Joseph et al. 2019 (baseline).
4. Ding, Nori, Li & Allen, "Comparing Population Means under Local Differential Privacy: with Significance and Power", AAAI 2018, https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11301 (baseline); Ohnishi & Awan, "Locally Private Causal Inference for Randomized Experiments", JMLR 26 (2025), https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v26/23-1401.html. Do: LDP tests and estimators for contrasts between groups or randomized arms. Difference: between-unit contrasts with an assignment; T3's contrast lies inside one trip, and its "treatment" is a public edge covariate.
5. Rameshwar et al., arXiv 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15906 (baseline): user-level central-DP mean bus speeds per cell and time window. Brown, Ohrimenko & Tamassia, "Haze: Privacy-Preserving Real-Time Traffic Statistics", ACM SIGSPATIAL 2013, https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3515: drivers' per-segment speed and alert reports aggregated by threshold cryptography, jury voting and DP. Difference: speed levels per place, central or cryptographic aggregation, no contrast, no synthesis.
6. Central-DP paired tests (baseline): the Awan & Slavković sign test (journal version "Differentially Private Inference for Binomial Data", https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00459) and Couch et al., "A Differentially Private Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test", https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01635. Difference: trusted curator; no LDP version of a paired sign test was found.

Background, not a threat: slower speeds along commercial frontage ("side friction") are an established non-private finding, e.g. "Critical Analysis of Road Side Friction on an Urban Arterial Road" (ETASR), https://etasr.com/index.php/ETASR/article/view/5603, and a GPS speed study of Chennai arterials, https://trid.trb.org/View/777779 (year not verified). The estimand is not new; only its private collection is.
Not found: any LDP or federated work that reports a within-trip or within-unit paired contrast; any DP work on segment times that uses an edge-covariate contrast. Private travel-time work uses geo-indistinguishability, cryptography or federated learning (Liu, Wang & Xu, IEEE TMC 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00310; https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13305; GOF-TTE, https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00838, with an unspecified DP layer).

### Verdict
**NOVEL COMBINATION, medium confidence.** The parts exist separately: user-level LDP averaging of per-user within-user effects (1, 2), sign or threshold randomized response with Gaussian inversion (3), LDP contrasts between groups (4), private segment speeds (5), private sign tests (6). Not found: a within-trip matched-pair contrast on a public edge covariate, sent as one GRR class with an exact symmetric null and used to recalibrate a road-network synthesizer's segment times and router costs.
- Sub-form: the numeric junction form (a publicly bounded user-mean junction delay by HM) alone is a **CLOSE VARIANT** of Roth & Avella-Medina's user-level LDP mean (Cor. 6.8); only the mechanism (HM instead of Laplace) and the public instead of data-located interval differ. To make it novel: send the junction delay as a within-trip contrast (junction zone against mid-block of the same trip) in the core's sign classes with its exact null, i.e. fold it into the core instead of keeping a level.
- Evaluator caveat: a strict reader who takes "a per-user within-user effect released under user-level LDP and averaged" as the core report would call the categorical core a CLOSE VARIANT of Cor. 6.10 (noise type plus the pairing choice). The defence: a per-user OLS cancels neither each trip's speed level nor the mode mix inside a user, gives no exact null, and does not feed a synthesizer.

Not found: any LDP or DP work whose report is a relation between the two ends of a trip (same enclosure, same kind, cross) that names neither end. This agrees with round 1, where D.4's relation-only reports were found nowhere.

### Verdict
**NOVEL COMBINATION, medium confidence.** The ε ≤ 2 relation-only report was not found in any published work, but it is GRR on a derived attribute (a standard primitive), and the server side is a standard gravity calibration with relation dummies matched to observed shares. The novelty lies in the choice of statistic and its coupling to an exact-likelihood destination law, not in a new primitive; NOVEL is not warranted because the estimator is standard practice. Confidence stays medium because three planned queries could not run.
- Sub-form: the ε 8 item (relation × kind of D, 17 values) and R3-D4's kind of a trip end are **CLOSE VARIANTS**: the kind of one trip end by GRR is round-1 B.2's trip-end zone item (C2) with a semantic alphabet, close to the per-location semantic LDP of Cunningham et al. 2021 and PLTS 2024. To make them novel: keep the kind only inside a relation that names no single end (for example "same kind as the origin", or only the diagonal of the kind-pair table), or drop the item.
- Evaluator caveat: the principle "relations, not places" is round-1 D.4 (C4). T4's difference rests on the relation type (enclosure identity and kind equality of one trip's two ends) and on needing no anchors and no linked days, which the evaluator must judge against `01_task.md` §3.
