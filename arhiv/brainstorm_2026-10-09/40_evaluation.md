### 2.1 T1 Opportunity-rank destination law
- (1) No input breaks the bound: ū ∈ [0, 1] for any data, HM is ε-LDP on [−1, 1], V and the ⊥ coin are device randomness with a public law, and the truncation to eligible destinations is computed from public data only. **Minor T1-a:** a destination unreachable from o on the public graph must map to a public default (u drawn uniformly), never to NaN. **Serious T1-b (hyperparameter that is effectively fitted):** under κ₀ = 1 the median trip passes exactly m_o of opportunity mass, so the radius behind m_o sets the prior's median reach, a quantity the designers have seen in the S4 length metrics; the family choice (radiation, Noulas rank law, gravity variant) has an equally large effect. Freeze the radius from a cited Beijing travel survey, or use the scale-free rank law, and verify α₀ = 0.84 in Noulas et al. (G1 found it only in lecture slides).
- (2) Recomputed: SD(Ū) = ½·√(1.04 + 0.04)/√91 = 0.055 and SD(κ̂) = κ₀·0.055/Ū² = 0.22 at κ = 1 reproduce. **Serious T1-c (⊥ attenuation, Y2):** users without an eligible trip send a coin with the null's mean, so the observed Ū − ½ is (1 − d) times the eligible one, and HM outputs cannot reveal d. At d = 0.3 a true κ = 2 gives observed Ū = 0.383, κ̂ = 1.61 (bias −0.39, SD 0.37), 2.1 SD from the null instead of 3.0. **Serious T1-d (gate power, Y1):** the pre-registered Hoeffding test must use the HM output range ± 2.16, so it rejects only if |Ū − ½| ≥ 0.31, i.e. κ ≥ 4.2 or κ ≤ 0.24. A CLT test or a sign-flip test (each output is symmetric about 0 under the null when a user's trips are independent) restores the claimed power, but its level is then asymptotic or conditional on independent trips. A fix for both: one uniformly drawn trip's PIT tercile plus ⊥ by GRR over 4. The balance P(u > ⅔) − P(u ≤ ⅓) moves by −0.64 per unit of κ at κ = 1 and has SD 0.17 among eligible users at d = 0.3, so SD(κ̂) ≈ 0.27, unbiased, with an exact multinomial gate; but that is K1's report form on a new component (the twin-rank rule's (a) and (b) still pass). **Serious T1-e (estimand):** one exponent fitted to one moment cannot fix both the scale and the tail; with a mis-set radius κ̂ absorbs the scale and the synthetic length law can still miss. The recovery simulation (data from the same family) cannot show this; only Geolife's length W1 can. **Serious T1-f (sessions, F1):** before P10, loops that end 200 m–1 km from their start give u ≈ 0 and pull κ̂ up.
- (3) Nothing leaks beyond κ̂: P(o) and the opportunity lists are public, caches are keyed by the queried origin on one code path. Minor: when the gate does not reject, the likelihood is the prior's and the MIA is blind at that ε; report gate outcomes per ε.
- (4) No gaming: one scalar in P(d | o) is a real, small change.
- (5) Reviewer: "a user-level LDP mean (HM on a bounded average) of a randomized PIT under a published one-parameter law (Yang et al. 2014); on Geolife the number is the walk/vehicle mix (X10)." The defence must be the origin-pivotal estimator (E u = κ₀/(κ + κ₀) from every origin, so neither the origin law nor the trip count is needed) and a head-to-head with C2's band item on the same users.
- Requirement §3: passes narrowly. It shares K1's exact-null PIT principle (R2-C.1), C1/D.2's user-averaged HM report, and C2/B.2's target (the band item is the nearest excluded idea); new are the opportunity order, the law and the origin-pivotal estimator. ### 2.7 Cross-cutting findings Y1–Y8
- **Y1 Hoeffding gates on HM outputs are about three times weaker than the SD-based claims (T1, T5).** HM's output range at ε 2 is ± 2.16 against an SD of 1.02, so a level-0.05 Hoeffding test needs a mean shift of 0.62 where a CLT test needs 0.21. Exactness costs power; a sign-flip test is exact only if a user's trips are independent. GRR gates have no such problem (T3's conditional binomial is exact and powerful).
- **Y2 ⊥ inside HM reports attenuates silently (T1, T5):** the estimate shrinks by the unknown (1 − d); GRR reports with an explicit ⊥ class do not.
- **Y3 Pivotal versus non-pivotal.** T1 (null and estimator hold from every origin), T3 (null holds inside each trip, under Y5's model) and T6 (direct estimand) need no population law. T2 (O–D law) and T4 (origin law) need one at ε ≤ 2: lesson 8's class of flaw.

## 4. Part D: verdict

**1. T1, best built as K-α (27.0; T1 alone 26.5).** In plain words: each phone looks at where its trips ended, measures how many "opportunities" (road length or mapped places) lay closer to the start than the actual destination, turns this into a number between 0 and 1 that would be spread evenly if the public travel law were right, averages it over its trips and sends that one number with noise. The server learns one thing, whether people travel farther or less far than the public law says, and adjusts the destination model by one number.
- At ε 2 it moves that one number, and only if the public law is off by about a factor of two in its exponent; at ε 0.5 the output is the public model.
- As written, users with no usable trip silently pull the estimate toward the public model, and the chosen exact test is about three times too cautious. Both are fixable, but the cleanest fix makes the report look like round 2's rank report.
- The law hides a scale (how far an origin's "neighbourhood" reaches) that decides how wrong the public model is. It must come from a published Beijing survey and be frozen before the 182-user run, never from what you have seen in Geolife.
- On Geolife the number will probably mostly reflect how many trips are walks; show it head-to-head with round 1's C2 band question on the same users.
- Novelty is narrow; the paper's claim must be the estimator that works from every origin without knowing where users live.
- **Open decision:** keep the average over all trips (more distinct from round 2, but biased toward the public model by an unknown share) or report one trip's third (unbiased, with an exact test, but round 2's report form).

**2. T3 (26.5).** In plain words: inside each trip the phone compares how much slower than the public model it moved on segments lined with shops (or near junctions) with similar plain segments a few minutes apart, so the person's own speed and travel mode cancel out, and sends one of three classes (slower, same, faster) with randomized response.
- It is the only candidate that corrects per-segment times (requirement R1) and the only one that does not end up measuring the walk/car mix, but only for slowdowns that act as a percentage. Waiting at signals adds fixed seconds, so the junction version brings the walk/car mix back.
- The exact test needs enough pairs inside trips; with patchy shop and land-use mapping in Beijing, half of the users may have none, which lowers the ε 2 signal to about 2 SD.
- None of the planned measurements sees the effect: you would need a new segment-level metric written down before any result, and the membership attack sees it only weakly through route choice.
- Its report and estimator are round 2's K8 design moved inside one trip; the paper must say so.
- **Open decision:** shop frontage (cancels the mode, but sparse) or signalised junctions (plentiful, but mode-dependent), decided by a one-hour simulation on the public graph of pair counts and of the test's behaviour when the public model is right.

**3. T2 (26.0).** In plain words: a routing hierarchy computed from the OSM graph sorts nodes into four levels; for one trip, the phone says whether its route climbed as high as the trip's own fastest path would, or stayed one or two levels below. The server learns how often trips stay below their "ceiling" and generates routes from a mix of public routers confined to lower levels.
- The most original object of the round; visible to the membership attack; every route computation stays public.
- At ε ≤ 2 both its estimate and its test lean on the real pattern of where trips start and end, which is not public: the same kind of flaw that hurt round 1's C3.
- Map-matching gap fills and walkers (who cannot use expressways) distort the measured share in opposite directions; the number will mostly be the walk share.
- If plain OSM road classes work as well as the hierarchy, it is round 1's C1 statistic in a coarser form.
- **Open decision:** run the public ablation (hierarchy levels versus OSM road classes) and the public model's own "stayed below" share before writing any code, and continue only if the hierarchy matters.

Not recommended: T4 (borderline signal, confounded by where Geolife users live; its relation question survives inside K-α); T5 and T6 (fail the novelty gate: a published one-step estimator and a published CDF estimator).

### 6.2 T2 Hierarchy-ceiling route law
