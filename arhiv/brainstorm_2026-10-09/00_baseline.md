## 1. Gap and constraints (both plans' §1)
- **Gap, round 1.** No published mechanism synthesizes whole road-graph routes with a departure time and per-segment times such that the release is pure LDP at user level. Randomization on the device; shuffler or secure aggregation only as optional extensions; one report per collection period (a later submission is a new user); incremental server updates allowed; free architecture; time mandatory; road-valid by construction (every next edge exists in the public graph).
- **Gap, round 2 (relative to the round-1 design).** (1) No misspecification test that stays exact whatever a user's trip count: C1 sends clipped averages in hand-set ranges and C3 needs a trips-per-user law that no source gives (F2), whereas a rank among twins of the trip's own context is exactly uniform under a correct prior for every user. (2) No within-person effects (how much slower the same person travels at peak): pooled estimates confound them with who travels when; per-trajectory LDP cannot produce them by construction; user-level LDP can, because both trips enter one report.
- **Must not repeat:** LDPTrace, PrivTrace, AdaTrace, DPT, DP-Star, RetraSyn, Cunningham et al., TrajLDP-style collection, DP-FedAvg-style models, non-private generators (TS-TrajGen, Diff-RNTraj, DiffTraj, ControlTraj). A combination of known pieces counts only if it solves a problem no piece solves alone.
- **Few users.** `fit` sees the train split only: u182 ≈ 91 users × ≈ 9 matched trips (trips that passed map matching) ≈ 809 trips; u50 ≈ 25, u20 ≈ 10. Error under pure LDP falls as 1/(ε√n), so per-edge histograms and transitions are out.
- **MIA** calls only `fit` and `sequence_log_prob(edge_seq)`, which must be finite for every sequence; never `generate`, never times. One arm builds 17 generators (target + 16 shadows of ≈ 18 users plus candidates), so public computation is cached; one attack call has 300 s at u20/u50 and 1200 s at u182.
- **Missing in the repo:** timed payload (P1) and synthetic utility metrics in the orchestrator (P2). Synthetic `user_id`s are not needed because trips are unlinked.
- **ε grid {0.5, 2, 8} is per user.** Existing arms protect one trajectory: 9 trips at per-trip ε 0.5–2 are user-level 4.5–18, 95 trips at 0.5 ≈ 47. **Tools:** no deep generators (PyTorch, GPU); `rn_ldp_synth` (per-trip reports, no time) is not a template.
- **Sessions, not trips** (verified in code, RANGI §1): one trajectory per `.plt`; cleaning never cuts at stops, so durations hold stops and recording gaps and a home–shop–home session is a loop. How often is unmeasured and must not be measured on Geolife before the prior is frozen; P10 adds a public split.
- **Only matched train trips reach `fit`** (verified in `experiments/orchestrator.py`), so today a user without a matched trip sends nothing (F3); P11 fixes it. R1–R3 and S1–S5 in RANGI §1 match `01_task.md` §1–§2; SINTEZA §1 states the same gap without these labels.
- **Noise cheat sheet, n = 91** (SD of the debiased estimate; exact formulas of X3 and F4, which supersede the round-1 cheat sheet):
  - Categorical (GRR): SD = √(f(1 − f)/n)/(p − q), f = q + π(p − q). k shares near 1/k at ε 2: 0.073 (k = 3), 0.074 (4), 0.076 (6), 0.079 (9); at ε 1: 0.136 (3), 0.206 (9); one of 9 shares near 0.5: 0.11; at ε 0.5: 0.28–0.41 per share. GRR beats OLH while k < 3e^ε + 2 (10.2 at ε 1, 24 at ε 2); OLH at ε 2: 0.089 (empty cell) to 0.118 (share 0.35). Binary RR near ½: 0.214 / 0.069 / 0.052 at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8.
  - Numeric (HM, d publicly sampled coordinates, fraction of the half-range) at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8: d = 1 0.43 / 0.11 / 0.017; d = 2 0.61 / 0.15 / 0.023; d = 8 1.21 / 0.30 / 0.047 (a between-user spread of 0.4 lifts the ε 8 values to 0.045–0.13). A one-bit item at ε 8 cannot beat the Bernoulli variance (up to 0.105), so numeric items at ε 8 use HM or PM.
  - Rules: a gain needs a prior error of ≈ 2 SD; ε 0.5 shows nothing; ε 2 moves one question family (≤ 2 numbers or one ≤ 9-way item); ε 8 a small model (≈ 8 numbers); n = 10,000 buys 10–25 numbers at ε 1–2, not "100+". c user groups multiply every SD by √c; splitting ε never pays (variance ∝ 1/ε²); a coupling sent as two bits at ε 1 behaves like one bit at ε ≈ 0.43, so couplings go into one categorical item; a ⊥ share d multiplies SD by 1/(1 − d). Membership under user-level ε-LDP: TPR ≤ e^ε·FPR (FPR 0.01 → 0.017 at ε 0.5, 0.074 at ε 2, vacuous at ε 8).

## 2. The two chosen designs
**Round 1 (SINTEZA §4): one mechanism, three modules, one public simulator.**
- **Public simulator P3**, the shared prerequisite of both plans. Router: Boltzmann walk toward the destination (each out-edge with probability falling exponentially in its added cost; cost-to-go from one reverse Dijkstra; after a public step cap the walk finishes on the shortest path, so routes are road-valid). Demand: origin by public road-length mass, destination by gravity, departure from a public prior in Beijing time UTC+8 (uniform unless a cited diurnal profile). Edge time = free-flow time (OSM `maxspeed` or class default) × public factor × jitter (AR(1) or log-normal) + public turn delay. Exact likelihood with a public floor per step (unreachable destinations, edge gaps, step cap); cache keyed by map + config hash, shared by the 17 generators (≈ 1,100 destinations per arm). ε = 0 gives the prior arm, exact statistics the oracle arm.
- **Report = (module id, answer).** A public data-independent draw picks module and question; the user answers at full ε; every enrolled user sends exactly one fixed-size report (public default without matched trips); a frozen rule in n·ε² sets users per module and the number of questions (small n·ε²: all users in one module). Code split `encode_user(views, public, rng) -> Report` on the device, `server_fit(reports, public) -> params` never sees views (P4). Each module ships as its own arm, then the composite.
- **Users are split, not ε:** three modules at ε/3 cost ≈ 3× the variance of a user split at ε ≤ 2 and 7.5× (numbers) to 85× (3-way label) at ε 8; three mechanisms on the same users would cost 3ε by basic composition. One ε per user, a three-line proof.
- **C3 regime vote (built first).** The device scores all its trips under K public complete generators (K = 3 at n = 91: walk/cycle, urban motor, fast motor; they differ in routes, speed, departure profile, distance decay) and draws one label from its posterior (local randomness, free); GRR at full ε (OLH if K > 3e^ε + 2). Server: debias, shrink to uniform, EM removal of a confusion matrix simulated on the public graph under a public trips-per-user law (F2: no such law is public). n = 91: 3 shares ±0.136 (ε 1) / ±0.073 (ε 2) before deconvolution (F4); gain = shape of duration and speed (walk vs vehicle); geography stays prior. Large n: 24–48 classes plus per-regime corrections.
- **C2 OD shares by gravity inversion (second).** The device holds shares of its trip ends over Z public zones (3 × 3 at n = 91, 36–144 at n ≥ 5,000) and of its trips over 6 free-flow cost bands, 5 periods, 3 speed regimes; it answers one item about one locally sampled trip end or trip (zone by OLH with probability 0.4, where F5 prefers GRR for 3 × 3; band, period or regime by GRR, 0.2 each). Server: T = argmin KL(T‖Q) + ½‖AT − t̂‖² weighted by V⁻¹ (Q a gravity prior on a 144-zone free-flow skim, A maps T to margins), three-way Furness. n = 91, ε 2: 3 × 3 shares ±0.079 when all users answer (F4). Cordons dropped (they overlap published ε-DP point-to-point traffic measurement). Unchecked risk: Geolife trip ends may not be concentrated relative to road-length mass.
- **C1 behavioural moments and router (third).** Boltzmann-walk variant first (exact RL needs 17 private sparse factorizations, risking 300 s). The device averages clipped per-trip statistics (road-class shares, turns/km, detour or length, speed ratio to free flow, departure harmonics), mostly as differences to the same-endpoint fastest path, into x_u ∈ [−1, 1]^d (d = 3–8) and sends one publicly drawn coordinate by PM/HM/Duchi (categories by GRR). Server: debias, project, shrink to θ₀, moment-match a handful of parameters. n = 91: ε 2 moves 2 numbers (speed ratio to OSM, length scale), ε 8 ≈ 8; route tastes stay prior and partly describe the map matcher. Large n: 10–25 numbers at ε 1–2. Novelty hinges on the unread GeoPM-DMEIRL.
- **Privacy (§4.6).** (1) Module and question by a public, data-independent draw; (2) the answer is a standard ε-LDP primitive (GRR, OLH, PM, HM, Duchi) on a publicly bounded function of all the user's trips, or of one trip chosen with data-independent randomness; (3) one report, so no composition; the rest is post-processing. Subtle: nothing "public" (boxes, reference speeds, zones, catalogue, question-count rule) may come from training data or from the number of users with matched trips; undefined statistic → public default; outputs snapped to a public grid; C3 sends only a label; adaptation only across disjoint cohorts; the time model is in the proof but untested by the MIA.
- **`sequence_log_prob` (§4.7).** Time does not enter. One regime: log P(O, D) + Σ log walk-step probabilities + public gap term (P(O, D) from the prior or C2, steps from the prior or C1); C3: log Σ_k π̂_k·P_k(seq); how C1's corrections apply to C3's regimes is open. Reads only parameters and map/config-keyed caches; cost-to-go computed lazily for any queried destination on one code path for members and non-members; finite via the floor. Options (§4.8): adaptive allocation at large n, shuffling outside the core, "spectral modes vs zones" ablation if the C6 check passes, BPR congestion for C1 at n ≈ 10,000, the C9 alphabet as a C1 question family.

**Round 2 (RANGI §5): rank calibration across and within users (K1 + K8).**
- **Host and structure.** The P3 simulator (a prerequisite, not a round-1 module); question families inside one report and one proof; a frozen n·ε² table splits users, never ε. K1 comes from R2-C.1 and R2-D.1, K8 from R2-C.3, R2-A.4 and R2-E.4.
- **Twins and the one coarse rank.** A public draw picks one question from a frozen list; every train user sends exactly one report; ⊥ is part of every domain; twins never leave the device. *dur* (K1): one matched trip uniformly; the public simulator draws 29–50 twins with travel times for the same edge sequence and departure; rank = number of faster twins (random tie-break); sent as one of 3 classes of equal prior probability (optionally a "much slower" class) or ⊥, by GRR at full ε. At n = 91 all users get *dur* at ε ≤ 2 (at ε 0.5 the gate keeps the prior), about half at ε 8. Only at ε 8 or large n: *dep* (exact PIT of the departure hour under the public diurnal profile, no twins), the 9-cell joint dep × dur, K8 item *a* (one public-rule pair: a peak trip at 7–9 h or 17–19 h and an off-peak trip in 6–22 h, free-flow times within × 1.5; each duration ranked among 29 twins shifted by the user's own mean log-speed ratio; class of d = rank_peak − rank_off in {d < −10, −10 ≤ d ≤ 10, d > 10, ⊥}, GRR over 4) and K8 item *c* (sign of the within-user Spearman correlation of two trip properties, ≥ 3 trips, threshold ±0.3; HM at ε 8). At ε 8 a rank u ∈ [0, 1] may go by HM. Excluded by the author: K8 (b), a moment contrast, and (d), the quadrant; the sequential form (median bit, then a re-centring cohort) is a CLOSE VARIANT of Joseph et al. 2019 and only an ablation.
- **Pre-registered test and recalibration.** Debias; shares among eligible users ŝ_c/(1 − ŝ_⊥). Gate: a corrected chi-square test against uniform with a critical value simulated in advance; the prior stays unless the test rejects (⊥ is outside the null, so the gate stays exact). Quantile map for *dur*: new duration density = prior × g(F_prior(x | context)), g a step function from the class shares, exactly normalized because E_prior[g(U)] = 1; 3 classes give a shift and a spread of log-duration, not bimodality; the tail shape stays prior. Pair: sign balance is an exact test of the prior's peak factor; an ordered probit with a public jitter scale gives the peak log-speed error μ̂. Sign: a Gaussian-copula parameter shrunk to 0.
- **Synthesis.** Origin, destination and route stay the prior's (P3); departure from the public profile (*dep* corrects it only at ε 8 or large n); segment times = free-flow × factor × jitter, the total quantile-mapped from the duration ranks and every segment scaled by the same ratio; at large n also exp(μ̂·1[peak]) and copula-drawn (period, distance class) pairs.
- **Privacy (§5.5, lift lemma X1).** Whatever the device computes (choice of trip or pair, twins, rank, class) is only a path to one value x in a small public domain, and only the GRR output leaves: P(y | D) ≤ max_x P(GRR(x) = y) ≤ e^ε·min_x P(GRR(x) = y) ≤ e^ε·P(y | D'). Conditions: domains, classes, twin count, pair rule, n·ε² table, critical value and session-split rule public and frozen by commit hash before the first u182 run (F8); the server must know the selection law; n = the train-split count (F3); the sequential form needs a public random cohort partition fixed before the first report. The red team found no attack on the core.
- **`sequence_log_prob`.** As SINTEZA §4.7, time absent; at n = 91 only *dur* is asked, so the likelihood equals the prior's and the MIA is blind by construction; K8 items do not change it; only a large-n *len* ablation adds log g(F_ℓ(ℓ(seq) | O, D)), twins seeded by hash(O, D, map, config), ≈ 40 ms per queried destination, caches shared by the 17 generators.
- **What Geolife can show.** At ε 2 only the duration level, the same walk/vehicle mix that C1 and C3 move (to be shown head-to-head with C1 on the same users); the exact null under any trips-per-user law and the within-person effect appear only in the recovery simulation.

## 6. When an unchosen candidate may return (both plans' §3.2, condensed; cite the condition by name)
- **Cond-C4:** only once linked synthetic users are evaluated (a user-level coherence metric exists). Relation-only reports (e.g. a home–work distance class, no coordinates) are new but invisible in a benchmark of unlinked trips; anchors are undefined at ≈ 9 trips; taxis have none.
- **Cond-C5:** only as a cheap honest successor of v1, or at n ≈ 10,000 (64–256 archetypes). At n ≈ 91 it reweights 4–8 coarse trip types (length, daypart), no geography, and C3's uniform-weight arm already is "public prior + one vote".
- **Cond-C6:** one hour of computing low Laplacian eigenvectors of the public graph. If they cover the city: a "spectral modes vs zones" ablation in C2 (one session) or a replacement of C2's zones. If they sit on fringes: it is Duchi's known estimator.
- **Cond-C7:** only for non-linear knobs (major-road weight, peak hour) at n ≈ 5,000 (6–8 knobs); at n ≈ 91 it fits 1–2 knobs, often on the design boundary, and is less efficient than C1's moment matching.
- **Cond-C8:** only with T-Drive (corridors above 2–5 % of traffic with their speeds). At n ≈ 91 there is one batch, so the descent never runs; a resampled pool has no finite likelihood; corridors need a new OSM name/ref export.
- **Cond-C9:** at n ≈ 10,000 frequent bigrams become measurable; until then the alphabet is only a possible C1 question family; without a destination pull the walks wander.
- **Cond-C10:** per-arc SD 0.4 at ε 2 against flows of 0.1–0.5, so it equals the prior below ε ≈ 4 at every Geolife rung; at most a T-Drive arm (3 × 3 zones at SD 0.06–0.09, n ≈ 5,000). Keep only the flow-conserving walk that needs no length model.
- **R2-Cond-C (K6 + K5, 62/95, ≈ 7–8 sessions):** (1) linked output and user-level metrics (Cond-C4; at u182 they rest on ≈ 36 test users, useless at u20/u50); (2) a one-hour public simulation before any code shows that the return constant ρ moves length W1 and 3 × 3 OD JSD at all; (3) the trips-per-user law is asked inside the report, never fitted on Geolife (S1). Risk: the main cell reads as a home report and at ε 8 releases cells near homes.
- **R2-Cond-A′ (K4, 56/95, ≈ 3–4 sessions):** only if a prior from cited constants can reasonably be expected to miss the multi-piece share by ≥ 0.15 (the least ε 2 corrects); or if sessions are modelled instead of split (combination A); or as a router option for the round-1 design, which hardly creates loops; or through the window profile (probability that a random window of a route is near-shortest, by window length), the one estimand of this family without precedent.
- **R2-Cond-T (K2 + K3, 54/95):** within scoring noise of A′; preferred only if time itself must be what users report (both items measure the mode mix at n = 91, X10); the key evidence would be the ablation "isochrone ring vs gravity"; K3 is better presented as a time-and-stops module.
- **R2-Cond-K7 (rejected):** only as indirect inference under LDP with designed multi-family scenarios at n ≳ 1,000, if simulation shows one designed answer recovers more parameters than a privately sent coordinate.
- **K1 or K8 alone** are parts of the chosen design: K1 alone estimates what C1 does; K8 alone has no generator and is invisible on the benchmark.

## 7. Findings F1–F9 of round 2 about the round-1 design (RANGI §10; not applied in SINTEZA, the author decides; for the rank plan F1 and F3 became P10 and P11, and F7 is its core)
- **F1 Sessions, not trips** (verified): stops and recording gaps inflate C1's speed ratio and departure moments; a round-trip session puts the destination at the origin, which gravity (C2) and the Boltzmann walk almost never produce; C3 can label a car session with a long stop as walk or cycle. Fix: a public stay split (shared prerequisite) or session modelling; P1 must record stops (edge entry and exit times).
- **F2 The trips-per-user law is not public** (set by cleaning and the ≈ 10 % match rate), yet C3's confusion matrix is simulated under it. Fix: draw C3's label from ONE uniformly sampled trip (public matrix by construction), or a count class in the same item, or ranks (K1), or a sensitivity analysis. C1's averages and C2's sampled trip end are unbiased for user-weighted estimands without it.
- **F3 Who reports must not depend on data** (verified for the harness): only users with a matched trip reach `fit`, and n in the n·ε² rule counts them; fix: all train users report a public default, n from the train split, a P4 test (now P11).
- **F4 Noise numbers** in SINTEZA §4.3–§5.1 hold only for vanishing shares; the exact values near 1/k (§1 cheat sheet) are 15–40 % larger; no module verdict flips. **F5** For 3 × 3 zones GRR beats OLH at ε ≥ 1; OLH only from 36 zones on.
- **F6 (conjecture)** C1 will duplicate C3 on Geolife: at n = 91 the speed ratio and the regime vote measure the same mode mix; report the C1-only arm at ε 2 as a check, not as a separate gain.
- **F7 A gate per module:** compare the raw report histogram with the one the prior predicts through the known randomizer (exact under the null, no extra users) and keep the prior unless a pre-registered test rejects.
- **F8 Freeze design constants too:** class edges and eligibility rules built around ≈ 9 matched trips per user use a Geolife number.

Only map-matched train trips arrive (X6, F3), so every user-level descriptor sees a ≈ 10 % subsample of the user's trajectories; passing clean views to the device is privacy-neutral but a harness change.
- **Generator ABC:** `fit(train: Sequence[TrajectoryView]) -> None` (train split only); `generate(n, seed) -> Sequence[SyntheticTrajectory]`; `sequence_log_prob(edge_seq) -> float`, finite for every edge sequence. `@register("generator", "<name>")`; constructor arguments filled by name: `network` = map, `seed` = cfg.seed + offset (0 target, 1000 + k shadow k); YAML parameters pass through; list values become separate arms. `generate` and `sequence_log_prob` must describe the same edge model (X12).
- **Output:** `SyntheticTrajectory` = `syn_id`, `generator_id`, `params_hash`, untyped `payload`, `trained_on_split`, `map_id`; no `user_id`; no generator emits timestamps yet (S5).
- **Map:** `RoadNetwork` = networkx `MultiDiGraph` + node/edge tables (`length_m`, `highway`, `oneway`, `maxspeed`; maxspeed coverage undocumented, class defaults fill gaps), EPSG:32650; Beijing ≈ 30 × 33 km, 35,764 nodes (edge count undocumented). Road names/refs, land use, POIs and opening hours are not in the tables (a new export from the same OSM snapshot, X14); whether footways and cycleways are in the graph is unchecked (P9). Reverse Dijkstra per destination: networkx ≈ 0.4 s, scipy `csgraph` ≈ 40 ms.
- **Public prior (S1):** OSM plus cited published constants only (diurnal profiles, route-choice coefficients, mode speeds, motif shares, capacities, route-structure shares); frozen by commit hash before any 182-user run, design constants included (F8), because the designers have seen Geolife S4 numbers. Anything tuned on Geolife is an unprotected release.
- **Geolife v1.3:** 182 users, one trajectory per `.plt`, GMT timestamps (Beijing = UTC+8), transport-mode labels unused. Cleaning drops points implying > 200 km/h, keeps points ≥ 5 s apart, needs ≥ 20 points and ≥ 500 m, never splits at stops. 17,313 clean trajectories (≈ 95 per user); 1,770 pass match score 0.3; generators train on ≈ 809. Split once by user, seed 42: train 0.5 / test 0.2 / shadow 0.2 / attack 0.1. Per-user trip counts and time spans are undocumented and their law is not public (X5). Porto (LDPTrace `.dat`): 367,008 one-trajectory users, no map, no real timestamps.
- **MIA (LiRA-lite), synthetic only:** candidate = one trajectory; members = train trajectories, non-members = test trajectories of other users; 16 shadows each fit a random half of the shadow split plus candidates; score = likelihood ratio of the candidate's `sequence_log_prob` under shadows with vs without it; AUC and TPR at FPR 0.001/0.01/0.1. Members/non-members 90/15 (u20), 130/33 (u50), 809/296 (u182); FPR 0.001 is NaN below 1,000 non-members. Whether candidates enter shadows under their own `user_id` is unverified (P0). The proposed pass mark TPR ≤ 0.02 at FPR 0.01 is guaranteed by user-level ε only for ε ≤ ln 2.
- **Reidentification, perturbation mechanisms only** (by decision never on synthetic data, round-1 briefing): knowing k = 3/5/10 evenly spaced points of a raw trajectory, the attacker names the user of the nearest other release under DTW, plain or length-normalised (`dtw_norm`), space only; galleries `rematched` (releases that re-match to the map; heavy noise empties it) and `release` (all released points), kept apart by the `gallery` column of `results.csv`; top-1/top-k accuracy and linkage rate; raw top-1 at u20 0.28/0.38/0.49; proposed pass mark top-1 < 5 % at k ≤ 10. Two attacker artefacts are scheduled fixes (the `release` gallery also changes the probe source; `dtw_norm` at k = 3 favours sparse traces).
- **Reference numbers:** non-private `markov` ceiling u182 AUC 0.776, TPR@0.01 0.027; u50 AUC 0.542. `rn_ldp_synth` u182 AUC 0.513 / 0.517 / 0.472 at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8. u182 membership run 101–161 s per seed; per arm at u20 `rn_ldp_synth` 20 s, `ldptrace` 10 s, `privtrace` 8 s.
- **Utility:** today only paired raw-vs-release metrics for perturbation (`cell_js_divergence` on 20 × 20 cells; length, duration and speed W1). Synthetic metrics arrive with P2 and P12; gain = share of the prior-to-oracle gap recovered; metrics where even the oracle does not beat the prior are dropped. No segment-usage, regime-mix, purpose or user-coherence metric exists or is planned.
- **Large-n evidence (S2, P6):** parameter-recovery simulation with known parameters, n = 10, 25, 91, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000, ε ∈ {0.5, 2, 8}, ≥ 20 seeds per cell, the same `encode_user`/`server_fit` code; per-parameter error, interval coverage, parameters leaving the prior, gap share on the utility metrics; one misspecified run. True parameters may come from an oracle fit on Geolife train users plus public offsets, never into prior or release.
- **Existing mechanisms (ε units differ):** `geo_indistinguishability` (ε per 100 m, per point), `point_ldp` (per point, RR over 20 × 20 cells), `ldptrace` (per trajectory, 12 × 12 cells), `privtrace` (central, per trajectory, adaptive 6 × 6), `rn_ldp_synth` (per trajectory: start zone, end zone, transition count ≤ 24, one zone transition; ε shares 0.15/0.15/0.2/0.5; 12 × 12 zones; no time), `markov` (none). None protects whole users. Round-2 control arms: `ldptrace` and `rn_ldp_synth` lifted to user level by one uniformly sampled trip at full ε (X7); every new mechanism must beat them and its own prior arm.
- **Engineering:** Python 3.11+, numpy with seeded `np.random.Generator` only, Parquet + DuckDB, PyYAML, ruff, mypy; every new dependency needs a one-line justification; no PyTorch/GPU. Tests offline in seconds on committed fixtures (20 synthetic Geolife-format trajectories from 5 users, 183-node / 388-edge map).
- **Privacy kit (P4):** `encode_user`/`server_fit` split; tests (1) parameters unchanged when views change but reports are fixed, (2) randomizer audit (empirical log-ratio ≤ ε), (3) canary user with an extreme trip, (4) `sequence_log_prob` reads only parameters and map/config-keyed caches; a deliberately leaky arm as positive control. Device randomness must not depend on data; pre-noise values never reach logs, `run.json` or caches; floating-point outputs are snapped to a public grid.

## 9. Direction map for the round-3 lenses (baseline's reading; "open" = not covered in rounds 1–2, novelty unchecked)

  - Not again: a frequency oracle on public-hierarchy nodes (C8, AHEAD, Yang 2020), a zone-transition or flow oracle (LDPTrace, C10, lifted `rn_ldp_synth`), a change of basis (C6, Chedemail 2022), a token alphabet (C9, Cunningham 2021), cordons (A.3), via/corridor structure (K4).
- **R3-B Attack-first: round-1 lens D already designed backwards from these attacks.**
  - Touched: lens D §0 (`10_ideas_D_adversary.md`): the MIA bound TPR ≤ e^ε·FPR makes the empirical MIA sit at chance; reidentification never runs on synthetic data and its synthetic analogue, distance to the closest record, obeys the same bound; blind spots (time invisible, `generate` never called, hence a proposed generate/likelihood consistency test); metadata leaks (participation, trip counts, floating point). ## 10. Lessons from the round-1 and round-2 evaluations: what sank candidates
1. Novelty is a gate: a CLOSE VARIANT (published work differs in one named detail) cannot be chosen. One detail each: noise moved onto the device, one round instead of many, a new alphabet (C5, C7, C9, C10, C3's duel); a sequential re-centring protocol (K1's sequential form); "report the mode, not a sample" (K5); a threshold query on a per-user estimate (K7). Round-2 rule: a frequency oracle on a published statistic + a published generator scores novelty 2 (K2, K4); an estimation step with its own identification argument 3 (K1, K6); a new estimand 4 (K8).
2. Convergence is a warning: C1 was the five-way obvious construction and still hinges on one unread paper; in round 2 the ε 2 item of K1 (*dur*), K2 (u), K3 (states), K7, C1 (speed ratio) and C3 (regime) is the same quantity, Geolife's walk/vehicle mix and speed level (X10). Utility at n = 91 cannot separate them; separation must come from structure (exact null, within-person estimand, joint items) and the recovery simulation.
3. Noise at n = 91 decides gain: novelty that appears only at n ≥ 5,000 scored 1–2 (C8's descent, C10's flows, C9's bigrams); authors who used n ≈ 180 were √2 optimistic and vanishing-share formulas 15–40 % optimistic (X3, F4); at ε 0.5 every candidate is its prior arm.
4. Saturation: a single-trip rank or bit against a narrow car-speed prior puts walks, buses and stop-laden sessions in the top class, and every remedy (a mode-mixture prior, a re-centring cohort) re-runs a round-1 module (K1's red team).
5. Pipeline artefacts masquerade as behaviour: route tastes partly describe the matcher, which fills gaps with shortest paths (X13); unsplit sessions put stops into durations and loops into routes (F1); K4's route-structure law "describes the pipeline, not behaviour".
6. Benchmark blindness: the MIA reads only the edge likelihood and P2 has single-trip marginals, so time-only, coupling-only and copy-layer gains are invisible (K8 alone did not survive: "nothing shows on Geolife"); privacy does not rank candidates (all at chance; TPR ≤ e^ε·FPR). Evidence = gain over the candidate's own prior arm on time-aware metrics, structural privacy tests, the recovery simulation.
7. Fit penalties: no natural finite likelihood (C8's pool resampling, E.4's surrogate, K6's Monte-Carlo likelihood); `generate` and likelihood describing different objects (X12, K7's released trips); private factorizations × 17 against the 300 s budget (exact RL).
8. Non-public inputs: any constant, zone, band, catalogue member, decay, class edge or eligibility rule chosen after seeing Geolife is an unprotected release (freeze by hash); non-public population laws sink estimators (the trips-per-user law behind C3's confusion matrix, K5's habit correction, K6's ρ, K7's response surface; X5, F2).
9. Membership leaks enter through the one non-public factor of a public likelihood: train-derived within-zone node mass (C2), familiarity costs or a cost-to-go cache filled only for training destinations (C1), a train-fitted catalogue member (C3), a data-derived floor (C6); at ε 8, habit items in the likelihood release single users (K5's hub = a home cell, K4's corridor).
10. Fragile primitives and splits cost points: PCKV's budget split (C8), exact ℓ2-ball sampling at dimension 69–2,000 (C10), fringe-localized eigenvectors (C6); ε splits across modules or bits never pay; who reports must not depend on data (F3).
11. Winning profile: a small public question family that moves 1–2 numbers or one ≤ 9-way categorical at ε 2 where the public prior is plausibly wrong, plus a structural claim that holds for any trip count and shows in the recovery simulation, presented head-to-head with the round-1 module that targets the same quantity on the same users.
