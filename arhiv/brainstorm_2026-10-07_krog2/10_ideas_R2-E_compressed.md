# R2-E ideas: local synthesis with a compressed description (brainstormer R2-E, 2026-10-07)

Raw English material; nothing here is a decision. Excluded on purpose: a label from a posterior over
public complete generators (C3), the nearest item of a public trip library (C5 / D.1 / C.3), program
slots or prefixes (E.4 -> C4), a scalar reward for a server-assigned knob (C.4 -> C7) and moment
coordinates of the user's own trips (C1). Two observations shaped the ideas. (i) At n = 91 every
R2-E code is worth at most one <= 9-way item or one number at eps 2 (baseline §1), so a code is only
as good as the question it answers and the decoder behind it, never its size in bits. (ii) The lens's
open angles (baseline §8) are: a local model answering public questions, a tilted sample of a public
simulator, and descriptors that exist only at user level. One idea each, plus one small one.
Repo fact checked: `fit` receives only map-matched trajectories (`orchestrator.py:1503`), about 9 per
user; descriptors below are computed on those unless P-level work passes clean views too.

## Idea 1 — Proxy respondent: the device answers one public stated-choice scenario with a model fitted to its own trips
1. **Pitch.** Transport science learns tastes from stated-preference surveys with designed choice sets;
   here the *device* is the respondent: it fits the public simulator's parameters to the user's trips,
   answers one publicly drawn hypothetical scenario ("for this public origin, destination and departure,
   which of these K alternatives would you take?"), and sends the answer through randomized response.
2. **Report.** Public per-user seed -> scenario s (a public OD pair, departure window and K <= 4
   alternatives generated by the public simulator; families: K routes differing in major-road share vs
   length, K duration bins for a public OD at a public departure, K destination zones for a public origin,
   K departure bins). Device: theta_u = maximum likelihood of the simulator's conditional parts (speed
   ratio to free flow by period, route coefficients, destination decay, departure profile) on its matched
   trips, clipped to a public box; users with < 2 trips use the public theta_0 (public default). Answer =
   the alternative theta_u prefers (or a draw from its local choice probabilities, free randomness); GRR
   over K at full eps. Payload: log2 K <= 2 bits (the scenario is reconstructible from the public seed).
3. **Privacy.** The scenario draw is public and data-independent; the answer is a function of all the
   user's trips into {1..K}; GRR satisfies P(r | a) / P(r | a') <= e^eps for *any* two answers, hence for
   any two users; one report, no composition; the server's fit is post-processing. No eps split: with
   two scenario families the public rule in n·eps² splits users, not eps.
4. **Server.** Target: 1–3 continuous simulator parameters theta (speed ratio, length scale or decay,
   major-road bonus). For each scenario the response surface P(answer = j | s, theta) is simulated on the
   public graph with the same local-fit code and a public trips-per-user law (so the local fit's sampling
   noise and clipping are inside the model). Estimation: misclassification-corrected discrete-choice MLE,
   L(theta) = sum_i log sum_j GRR(r_i | j) P(j | s_i, theta), with the GRR matrix exactly known; the
   scenario design is D-optimal for theta under the public prior and frozen by hash before any run.
   Synthesis: the public simulator at theta-hat (Boltzmann router toward gravity destinations, public
   departure prior, free-flow time × fitted speed ratio × jitter) -> road-valid routes with departure and
   per-segment times. If alternatives are complete timed public trips, every report also decodes to one
   road-valid trip (direct pooling at eps 8).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Simulator likelihood at theta-hat as in plan §4.7: log P(O, D) + sum of log
   step probabilities + public floor; time marginalised out; caches keyed by map, config and theta-hat.
6. **n = 91.** A designed scenario moves one alternative's share by about 0.25 between theta_0 and a
   plausible theta_1. GRR K = 4: SD 0.31 per share at eps 0.5 (nothing); 0.050 at eps 2 plus sampling
   0.045 -> 0.067 total, so 0.25 is 3.7 SD: one parameter solidly, two marginally (√2 from the user
   split: 0.095 -> 2.6 SD); eps 8: GRR noise 0.002, sampling 0.045 -> 3–4 scenario families with split
   users (lens-A budget 210 answers). Local fits on about 9 trips add noise but no bias (it is in the
   response surface). n = 10,000: SD 0.005 at eps 2 -> 10–25 parameters, including route tastes.
7. **Closest work.** PATE (Papernot et al. 2017) and LDP knowledge distillation (arXiv 2202.02971;
   FedMD-NFDP arXiv 2009.05537): local models label *unlabelled public samples*, many labels per client,
   a neural student. Zhou & Tan 2021 (= C7 neighbourhood): the server assigns a knob, the user returns a
   scalar reward. Warner 1965 RR on survey answers; stated-choice design (Louviere, Hensher & Swait 2000);
   misclassified discrete-response MLE (Hausman, Abrevaya & Scott-Morton 1998) with a *known* matrix.
   Precise difference: the query is a designed scenario of a mechanistic simulator, one answer per user,
   the estimand is a structural parameter recovered through the known RR channel. Versus §4: C3 asks
   "which of K models am I" (hypothesis selection on the user), C1 reports moments of the trips the user
   happened to make; here the alternatives are *actions* and the information per answer is set by design,
   not by where the user travelled. Not C5 (no library), not C7 (no knob, no reward), not E.4.
8. **Main risk.** A novelty reader may file it as "LDP distillation with a different query"; route-choice
   scenarios inherit the map matcher's shortest-path bias (lesson 4), so at n = 91 use speed, duration,
   departure and destination scenarios; the design must be fixed from the public prior before seeing data.

## Idea 2 — Mobility signature: the device compresses its month into classic individual-mobility descriptors that calibrate an exploration-and-return generator (R2-E × R2-B)
1. **Pitch.** Mobility science describes a person by a handful of numbers (number of distinct places S,
   radius of gyration r_g, returner-vs-explorer ratio); the device computes them from all its trips and
   releases one binned descriptor; the server fits the exploration–preferential-return (EPR) model's
   constants and generates whole synthetic users as visit sequences on the OSM graph.
2. **Report.** Device: trip ends snapped to a public 500 m grid; S = distinct cells (capped at 2m, m =
   trips); r_g in km; k-radius ratio r_g^(2) / r_g (share of spread explained by the two most visited
   cells). Public draw picks one descriptor (all users the same one when n·eps² is small); 4 public bins
   (S: <= 4, 5–7, 8–11, >= 12; r_g: < 2, 2–5, 5–10, > 10 km; ratio: 4 quartile bins); users with < 2 trips
   send the public default bin. GRR over 4 at full eps; 2 bits.
3. **Privacy.** Each descriptor is a bounded deterministic function of the user's whole trip set with a
   public binning and a public default; the descriptor choice is a public draw; GRR at full eps bounds
   the ratio for any two users; one report. No eps split (user split if several descriptors).
4. **Server.** Debias shares, shrink to the public prediction, then simulated method of moments on the
   public graph: EPR with Song et al. 2010 constants (rho = 0.6, gamma = 0.21; P_new = rho·S^-gamma),
   exploration destinations by a d-EPR gravity law (Pappalardo & Simini 2018) over OSM building or
   road-length mass (S1), returns proportional to visit counts; a public trips-per-user law gives m.
   S bins pin rho (and gamma at eps 8), r_g bins pin the gravity decay, the k-radius ratio pins the
   returner share. Synthesis per synthetic user: m trips, origin by public mass, each move explore-or-
   return, departure by the public diurnal profile or EPR's waiting-time law (beta = 0.8, cut-off 17 h),
   route by the public Boltzmann router, segment times free-flow × public factor × jitter; trips are
   emitted unlinked (the benchmark needs no user ids), linked output is one flag.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** The EPR population's long-run OD distribution over a public 12 × 12 zone grid
   is simulated once per theta-hat and cached (keyed by map, config, theta-hat); P(seq) = log P-hat(zone_O,
   zone_D) + log public within-zone node masses + sum of log router steps + public floor. Only public
   quantities and theta-hat enter, one code path for members and non-members, finite everywhere.
6. **n = 91.** EPR with public constants predicts S ≈ 8.4 of 18 trip ends; rho = 0.3 would give S ≈ 4.7,
   a bin-share shift of about 0.4. GRR K = 4 all users: SD 0.31 at eps 0.5 (nothing); 0.050 + sampling
   0.045 at eps 2 -> 0.4 is 6 SD: rho at fixed public gamma, i.e. "how concentrated are trip ends" — the
   very quantity C2 lists as its unchecked risk; eps 8: three descriptors with split users, SD ≈ 0.08
   each (sampling-dominated) -> rho, gamma and the decay. n = 10,000: joint (S, r_g) 16-cell OLH, EPR
   constants per regime, m law estimated instead of assumed.
7. **Closest work.** Song et al. 2010 (EPR), Pappalardo & Simini 2018 (d-EPR), Pappalardo et al. 2015
   (returners and explorers), TimeGeo 2016: all non-private. DP-WHERE 2013: central DP on a simulator's
   driving distributions. No LDP calibration of EPR found (search 2026-10-07). Versus §4: no catalogue
   (C3), no moments of trips (C1), no zone shares (C2) — geography stays public, but its *concentration*
   is measured. Versus C4 (Cond-C4): no home/work anchors or schedules; S, r_g and the k-radius ratio are
   defined for any user with >= 2 trips, taxis included, and the output changes trip-level marginals
   (length W1, 3 × 3 OD JSD) that the unlinked benchmark does measure, so Cond-C4 is not needed.
8. **Main risk.** S on about 9 matched trips is heavily truncated, so the response surface leans on the
   *public* trips-per-user law (lesson 8); if the real m law differs, rho is biased. Partial cure: a tiny
   m question (R2-A) or clean views in `fit`. Also the EPR constants were measured on call records, not
   GPS, so the "prior badly wrong" premise is plausible but unverified.

## Idea 3 — Tilted-prior sampling: one timed, road-valid public trip per user, reweighted by at most e^(eps/2) toward the user (wildcard)
1. **Pitch.** Instead of a code the user releases a *trip*: the public simulator proposes M complete timed
   trips from a public seed and the device picks one with probability proportional to exp((eps/2)·u),
   where u in [0, 1] is how well the candidate fits the user's locally fitted model; the server pools the
   trips and inverts the exactly known tilt.
2. **Report.** Public per-user seed -> M public candidates (O, D, departure, route, segment times) from the
   public simulator; M set by a public rate–distortion rule (M ≈ 8–16 at eps 8; at eps <= 2 M is
   irrelevant, see 6). Device: theta_u as in Idea 1, u(x_j) = clipped, normalised log-likelihood ratio of
   candidate j under theta_u versus theta_0 (or 1 − clipped public distance to the user's trips); draw j*
   proportional to exp((eps/2)·u(x_j)); send j* (log2 M = 3–8 bits). No trips: u constant -> uniform.
3. **Privacy.** Exponential mechanism over a finite public set with utility range 1: for any two users,
   P(j | u) / P(j | u') <= e^(eps/2) · Z(u') / Z(u) <= e^eps (McSherry & Talwar 2007); the candidate set and
   seed are data-independent; one report, no split. The released trip is road-valid and timed because the
   public simulator made it; the server adds nothing private.
4. **Server.** (a) Simulated method of moments through the exact channel: for trial theta, simulate users
   (public trips-per-user law), run the identical device code, match the released trips' feature means and
   histograms (log length, departure, speed ratio, 3 × 3 zone shares) to the observed ones. First order,
   E[f(x_j*)] ≈ E_pub[f] + (eps/2)·Cov_pub(f, u_u), so with u linear in standardised features the
   population mean is recovered linearly. (b) At eps 8 the 91 released trips are also usable directly as a
   synthetic sample (each is "a public trip that fits me", chosen about 40 % of the time at M = 16).
   Generation: simulator at theta-hat; optionally mixed with the released trips at eps 8.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Simulator at theta-hat (as Idea 1); released trips never enter the likelihood.
6. **n = 91.** Tilt factor e^(eps/2) = 1.28 / 2.7 / 55 at eps 0.5 / 2 / 8. With the bounded utility only the
   *direction* of the user's deviation is encoded, scaled by 1/√d for d features: relative SD of a
   population direction ≈ (4/eps)·√d/√n = 0.84 (eps 0.5), 0.21 (eps 2, d = 1; 0.42 at d = 4), about 0.05
   at eps 8 (first-order; use (a) there). Honest reading: at eps 2 it carries about half of one HM number
   (0.11), but the "question" is chosen by the data (the tilt concentrates on whatever the user deviates
   in most), and at eps 8 it yields a timed synthetic sample with no decoder. Selection sharpness:
   P(best of M) ≈ e^4 / (e^4 + (M−1)·e^1.6) = 42 % at M = 16, 4 % at M = 256 — hence the M rule.
   n = 10,000: direction and magnitude of 5–10 deviations at eps 2.
7. **Closest work.** DPMM 2022 and Takagi et al. 2019: per-trajectory exponential mechanisms whose utility
   is distance to the *true* object over a uniform base. Private Evolution / Sim-PE / PrE-Text and C5
   (CLOSE VARIANT): hard nearest-sample votes over a shared library, central or GRR noise, iterated.
   Duchi et al. 2018 sample a tilted vector on a sphere. Posterior-sampling privacy (Dimitrakakis et al.
   2014; Wang, Fienberg & Smola 2015): one sample from a tempered posterior is DP — this is its local
   form with the public simulator as base measure. BLS "weighted selection" synthetic data: central.
   Differences: user-level utility from a locally fitted model, per-user fresh public candidate sets (no
   codebook), soft tilt instead of argmax + GRR, and a tilt-inversion estimator for simulator parameters.
   Not C3 (no label over designed generators), not C5 by the released object and estimator — but this is
   the line a reviewer will test; Cond-C5 is not claimed.
8. **Main risk.** CLOSE VARIANT verdict ("C5 with graded utility"); at eps <= 2 the per-user release is
   nearly a prior draw and the population gain is half an HM number; utility design must be frozen.

## Idea 4 — Within-user coupling code: a one-bit description of the dependence between a user's trip attributes (small)
1. **Pitch.** A per-trip mechanism can never release how a user's attributes co-vary *across* their trips
   (long trips in the morning? fast on long trips?); a user-level mechanism can, and one bit about it
   calibrates a coupling parameter the simulator otherwise samples independently.
2. **Report.** Public draw picks one attribute pair from a public list: (trip length, peak-period
   departure), (trip length, mean speed), (detour ratio, trip length). Device (>= 3 trips): within-user
   Spearman correlation clipped to [−1, 1]; Duchi one bit, or GRR over {negative, none, positive} with
   thresholds ±0.3; < 3 trips: public default "none". 1–2 bits.
3. **Privacy.** Bounded statistic of the whole trip set, public pair choice and default, Duchi/GRR at
   full eps, one report.
4. **Server.** Debiased mean coupling -> a Gaussian-copula parameter between the simulator's departure
   period and distance class (or speed regime); marginals stay prior (or come from C1/C2 when combined).
   Synthesis: draw (period, distance class) jointly, destination by gravity within the class, route and
   segment times public.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Unchanged from the prior: marginalising the period restores P(O, D), so the
   edge likelihood is exactly the public one (the MIA is blind by construction).
6. **n = 91.** Duchi SD 0.43 / 0.14 / 0.017 at eps 0.5 / 2 / 8: a mean coupling of 0.3 against a prior
   of 0 is 2 SD at eps 2 (borderline); the 3-way share form (SD 0.05) detects "60 % of users positive"
   versus 33 % at 5 SD. n = 10,000: full 3 × 3 copula among period, distance and regime, per regime.
7. **Closest work.** LoPub 2018 (LDP correlations among a record's attributes, EM on joint reports) — here
   the record is a user and the correlation is across their trips, a second-order quantity that no
   per-trajectory LDP release (LDPTrace, L-SRR, Cunningham) can produce; Gaussian-copula population
   synthesis is non-private. Versus §4: C1 averages per-trip statistics; this one is not an average, needs
   >= 3 trips and targets a joint, not a marginal. Lies in R2-A's open "departure × distance coupling".
8. **Main risk.** No current P2 metric sees it (a joint departure × length metric is needed, cf.
   Cond-C4); the primitive is standard, so a reviewer may call it a C1 question family rather than a
   mechanism. Kept because it is the cleanest argument for *why* user-level LDP is worth its cost.

## Self-ranking
1. Idea 1 (proxy respondent): clearest distance from C3/C5/E.4, fits the winning profile (1–2 numbers at eps 2) and gives a public, pre-registrable experimental design.
2. Idea 2 (mobility signature): a new object for LDP that directly measures C2's unchecked assumption; held back by the public trips-per-user law and the coherence-blind benchmark.
3. Idea 3 (tilted-prior sampling) is the true wildcard but risks a CLOSE VARIANT verdict and is weak at eps 2; Idea 4 (coupling code) is sound but small, a question family unless a joint metric exists.
