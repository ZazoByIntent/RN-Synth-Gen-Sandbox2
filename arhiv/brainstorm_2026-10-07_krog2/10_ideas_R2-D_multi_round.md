# R2-D ideas: interactive multi-round protocols over disjoint cohorts (brainstormer R2-D, 2026-10-07)

Inputs: `01_task.md`, `00_baseline.md`. Three web queries for prior art (adaptive LDP location estimation; LDP conformal calibration); results are cited in §7 of idea 1. No repo file was read.

## 0. Where rounds pay at n = 91 (the lens question, answered first)

Facts used. Binary randomised response, debiased share near p = ½: SD = (e^ε + 1) / (2 (e^ε − 1) √n). At n = 91: 0.214 / 0.069 / 0.052 for ε 0.5 / 2 / 8. A half cohort (45 users) multiplies by 1.42 (0.30 / 0.098 / 0.074), a third (30) by 1.74, 20 users by 2.13. A location (median) follows from a share through the density of the user statistic at the pivot, Δθ = Δp / f(pivot); for a spread s, f = 0.40/s at the median, 0.24/s one spread away, 0.05/s two spreads away (the bit saturates and only says "far, this way"). The non-adaptive numeric alternative (PM/HM, d = 1) at a public half-range B costs 0.43 B / 0.11 B / 0.017 B (cheat sheet) plus the between-user sampling spread s/√91 = 0.105 s, which no protocol beats.

Where adaptivity pays back more than the √c cost:
- (a) Pivot re-centring pays only when the first pivot (the public prior) is more than about one spread off: then a non-adaptive bit saturates and a numeric report costs 0.11 B ≥ 0.3 s. If the prior is within one spread, one round with all users equals two rounds (± 0.07 s vs ± 0.07 s at ε 2). If it is two spreads off, two rounds give ± 0.25 s at ε 2 where one round gives "≥ 1.3 s, direction only", a factor of at least four. The second cohort must therefore be conditional: a public saturation test on cohort 1's debiased share (outside [0.25, 0.75], 2.5 SD at ε 2) decides whether cohort 2 re-asks the same question against the corrected model or moves to the next one. This is the "sequential design with exactly known LDP noise" that the baseline lists as open.
- (b) Structural choices pay without bound: when cohort 1's answer selects the model's support (motor versus pedestrian OSM layer for the route model, P9; the regime set; which question family is worth asking at all), a wrong non-adaptive choice cannot be repaired by any later estimate, and the cost is only √2 on the parametric cohort. The chosen design's rule "all users in one module at small n·ε²" forgoes this.
- (c) Allocation among d ≥ 3 candidate numbers of which one matters given the regime mix: the adaptive protocol gets the vote and the one number at √2 each instead of √(d + 1) for everything; gain √((d + 1)/2), i.e. 1.4 at d = 3. This is plan §4.8's logic brought down to n = 91; modest.
- (d) Questions that are undefined before a population estimate exists: a coupling relative to population medians (idea 4), validation of a privately fitted model (idea 3), nuisance parameters of the pipeline (idea 2). Single-round protocols have no competitor here; the only question is whether a cohort can afford the answer.

What never pays at n = 91 (same arithmetic): refinement of a categorical domain (AHEAD/C8-type zoom: the refined cells see n/(2·share) users, ± 0.3 per cell); regime-conditional second questions ("answer only if you are in regime k, else a default": SD × 1/π_k on top of √2, 0.20 against 0.16 for one GRR over 2K labels in a single round at ε 2); Neyman allocation between two questions (< 10 %); any ε split across rounds (variance ∝ 1/ε², always worse than a user split); three cohorts for one parameter (after a re-centred bit the pivot error is ≈ 0.3 s, below one spread, so a third bit cannot improve it).

How many rounds n = 91 affords. ε 0.5: one round, one bit against the public pivot (share ± 0.21 flags a prior ≥ 1.5 spreads off with ≈ 70 % power, direction only; a half cohort at ± 0.30 cannot trigger anything, so no second round). ε 2: two rounds, the second only if the first saturates. ε 8: zero re-centring rounds (PM/HM is near exact and the sampling spread dominates; one round with d ≤ 8 ranks reported as numbers), but one structural cohort of type (b) still pays. Larger n: at n = 300 one parameter per cohort of 100 (SD 0.066 at ε 2) gives 3 rounds; at n = 10,000, 8–10 cohorts of ≈ 1,000 users (SD 0.021) in a fixed public order from coarse to fine. Rounds then buy ordering (a spread or coupling question is meaningless before the level is right), not variance.

Two standing conditions. Cohorts are a public random partition of enrolled users fixed before any report (R3; a linked device answering twice pays 2ε, the lens-D warning). Every adaptive choice is a public function of earlier cohorts' reports, which also turns design constants that the chosen design must freeze by commit hash (graph layer, regime set, band edges) into measured, protected quantities instead of possible prior contamination (lesson 7).

## Idea 1 — Rank-and-correct: model-conditional rank bits with a saturation-triggered second cohort

1. **Pitch.** Each user reports one bit: whether one of their own trips ranks above the median of what the current public simulator predicts for that very trip's origin, destination and departure. The server moves one simulator parameter until the simulated share of such bits matches the debiased share, and a second, disjoint cohort repeats the question against the corrected simulator only if the first cohort's share is saturated.
2. **Report.** The device draws one matched trip uniformly (data-independent local randomness; a user without matched trips draws the bit from Bernoulli(½), the calibrated default). It runs the public simulator (P3: Boltzmann walk, free-flow × factor × jitter, OD prior) at the current public parameters θ_t on the trip's own (O, D, departure), 50 draws, and computes the rank u = F̂_θ(x | O, D, dep) of the scheduled statistic x. Menu, one statistic per cohort in a public order: trip duration (→ speed level λ; only when the user cannot normalise it locally by regime), log length under the prior's length law (→ length scale), radial distance of the trip end under the OD prior's radial CDF (→ centrality exponent of the gravity prior, C2's unchecked risk), the actual route's log-likelihood among the simulated routes for the same OD (→ route temperature T). The bit b = 1[u > ½] goes through binary randomised response at full ε. Size: 1 bit. The question (statistic, θ_t, q = ½) is a public message to the cohort.
3. **Privacy.** b is a function of the user's whole data with a binary domain; binary RR with flip probability 1/(1 + e^ε) is ε-LDP for any two user datasets; statistic, θ_t and q depend only on public data and on the reports of earlier, disjoint cohorts (sequentially interactive LDP: every user reports once, so ε per user with no split); the cohort partition is public and fixed before any report; the rest is post-processing. The default for users without trips pulls the share toward ½, i.e. toward "no change" (conservative; idea 2 removes it).
4. **Server.** Debias p̂ = (p̂_obs − (1 − π)) / (2π − 1) with π = e^ε / (1 + e^ε). Simulated method of moments on one parameter: for a public grid of θ, simulate trips from the simulator plus a cited between-user heterogeneity law (± 0.3 nats on the level), compute s(θ) = P(b = 1) (monotone), take the posterior under the exact binomial likelihood, shrink to θ_t when the posterior is wide. Public saturation rule: if the 90 % interval of p̂ lies outside [0.25, 0.75], the next cohort re-asks the same statistic at θ_{t+1}; otherwise it gets the next statistic in the public order, or there is no next cohort (n = 91). Round 1 may instead be C3's regime vote; the ranks are then computed under the user's own MAP regime generator (local, free), the mode mixture is factored out of the level, and the same public rule may switch the walk regime to the pedestrian OSM layer before any route rank is computed. Synthesis: destination from the OD prior with the fitted centrality exponent, route by the Boltzmann walk at T̂ (road-valid by construction), departure from the public diurnal profile, segment time = free-flow × e^λ̂ × jitter + turn delay (timed payload needs P1, S5).
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** As plan §4.7: log P(O, D) under the centrality-corrected gravity prior + Σ log Boltzmann step probabilities at T̂ + the public gap floor; time marginalised out; reads only parameters and the map/config-keyed cost-to-go cache, computed lazily on one code path for members and non-members; finite.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** Share SD 0.214 / 0.069 / 0.052 (all users) and 0.30 / 0.098 / 0.074 (half cohort) at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8. Speed level, single-trip rank spread ≈ 0.42 nats (jitter 0.3 ⊕ heterogeneity 0.3): ε 2 gives ± 0.07 nats in one round if the public pivot is within one spread and ± 0.10 after two rounds if it was not (one round alone: "≥ 1.3 nats, direction only"); a PM report of the clipped log speed ratio at a mixed-population half-range of 1.35 nats costs ± 0.15, but C1 can shrink that range locally by regime to ≈ 0.55 (± 0.06), so the gain on the speed level is real only without regimes. Length scale (trip spread ≈ 1.0 nat, public half-range 2.3 nats, not shrinkable locally): ± 0.17 one round, ± 0.25 two rounds, against PM ± 0.25. Centrality: the share inside the prior's median radius at ± 0.07 detects 70 % against 50 % at 2.9 SD. Departure shift: no gain (the 24-hour circle is an intrinsic range; 4-way daypart GRR does better). ε 0.5: one round, ± 0.23 nats on the level when the pivot is near, otherwise a shrunken one-sided step. ε 8: the bit (± 0.055 nats) is no better than HM (± 0.045 including sampling spread), so the protocol collapses to one round of HM on up to 8 ranks reported as numbers in [0, 1] (no public range is needed either). n = 10,000: cohorts of ≈ 1,000 (SD 0.021 at ε 2), 8–10 rounds in the public order, then two-quantile rounds (q = 0.1 and 0.9) for the jitter and the between-user spread, which only make sense after the level is right.
7. **Closest work.** Joseph, Kulkarni, Mao, Wu, NeurIPS 2019, "Locally Private Gaussian Estimation" (arXiv 1811.08382): adaptive two-round LDP mean estimation, a coarse first round then a re-centred second, tight for sequentially interactive protocols. Difference: my statistic is a rank under a public road-network simulator run on the user's own trip, the parameter is a simulator setting, the second round is conditional on saturation, and the schedule orders several parameters. Penso et al., COPA 2025 (PMLR 266; arXiv 2505.15721), "Privacy-Preserving Conformal Prediction under LDP": users compute a conformity score with the model and privatise it by randomised response or a binary-search response to estimate a quantile; same primitive, different purpose (a prediction-set threshold, not moving a generative model), no re-centring, no simulator. Xiong, Ju, Zhang 2023 (simulation-based inference from DP releases) is central DP. Against §4: C1, E.3 and D.2 report clipped raw moments at hand-set public ranges by PM and never re-centre; against D.3 (duel between two models, i.e. LDP hypothesis selection, CLOSE VARIANT verdict): a within-model calibration bit that moves a continuous parameter; against §4.8 (cohorts to modules at large n): the adaptive object is the pivot, at n = 91, with a stopping rule; against C7: no response surface, one monotone share per parameter.
8. **Risk.** The share-to-parameter map assumes the simulator's conditional shape (jitter, heterogeneity law); a wrong shape biases the step size, not the direction, and at n = 91 the second cohort does not re-check. A bimodal population under one regime moves λ to a compromise, so the level question must run under C3's locally chosen regimes. The route-temperature rank is contaminated by the map matcher's shortest-path bias (lesson 4). The novelty check may read it as "Joseph et al. with a simulator"; the defence is the model-conditional rank, the saturation rule and the ordered menu, none of which exists there.

## Idea 2 — Census-first: a small cohort measures the pipeline's nuisance parameters

1. **Pitch.** About 20 users report one bit about how much usable data they have. The share of enrolled users with no matched trip and the trips-per-user law are artefacts of cleaning and map matching (≈ 10 % of clean trajectories pass; per-user counts undocumented), not facts about Beijing, so no OSM constant can supply them; yet C3's confusion matrix, the default-report dilution of every share and the trip- versus user-weighting all assume them silently.
2. **Report.** b = 1[m_u ≥ M] with public M (M = 1: "any matched trip"), binary RR at full ε; or a 3-way band {0, 1–4, ≥ 5} by GRR (k = 3). 1 bit (or log₂ 3).
3. **Privacy.** m_u is a function of the user's data on a bounded domain; a standard primitive at full ε; the census cohort is a public random subset fixed before any report; later cohorts' debiasing uses the census estimate as post-processing; the main cohort never sends anything about m_u.
4. **Server.** ρ̂₀ = share with no matched trip and a one-parameter law for m_u given m_u ≥ 1 (geometric or log-series fitted from the ≥ M share). Uses: (i) un-dilute every later share, p_real = (p̂ − ρ̂₀ · default) / (1 − ρ̂₀); (ii) simulate C3's confusion matrix and idea 1's s(θ) under the measured law instead of a "public" one; (iii) set the cap M of the lens-A ratio estimator when a trip-weighted reference is wanted. It synthesises nothing itself; routes and times come from the module it serves.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Unchanged; the census adds no likelihood parameter.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** A 20-user census at ε 2 gives the "any trip" share ± 0.15 (ε 8: ± 0.11), which separates ρ₀ = 0.1 from 0.4 at 2 SD, while the main cohort of 71 pays SD × 1.13. It pays when the dilution bias ρ₀ × (correction) exceeds the main estimate's SD: at ε 8 (main SD ≈ 5 %) for any ρ₀ ≥ 0.1, at ε 2 only for ρ₀ ≥ 0.3. At ε 0.5 the census is useless (± 0.46): skip it and accept the conservative dilution. n = 10,000: 300 users give ρ₀ ± 0.04 and a 5-band law at negligible cost.
7. **Closest work.** Wang, Li, Jha, IEEE S&P 2018 (LDP frequent itemset mining, SVIM: one user group reports its itemset length so the server can choose the padding length) and padding-and-sampling (LDPMiner 2016, PCKV 2020); Rameshwar et al. 2024 (unequal samples per user, central DP). Difference: the census calibrates a simulator-based deconvolution and un-dilutes default reports instead of fixing a padding length; §4 treats the trips-per-user law as public and the default share as unknowable, and this makes both measured and protected. Honest status: an interactive prerequisite for C3 and idea 1, not a mechanism.
8. **Risk.** Crude (± 0.15); the law's shape beyond one parameter is assumed; if almost every enrolled user has a matched trip the cohort was wasted (SD × 1.13 for nothing); the decision to run it must be a public rule in n·ε² fixed before data.

## Idea 3 — Do-no-harm validator: the last cohort sets the mixture weight between prior and fit

1. **Pitch.** The last cohort fits nothing. Each validator answers one bit comparing the public prior with the privately fitted model on a utility scalar for one of their own trips ("which of the two predicts my duration better"), and a public rule turns the debiased share into the mixture weight of the release, so the released generator is never worse than the prior by more than a quantified margin, also under misspecification, where analytic shrinkage (which knows only the noise variance, not the model error) gives no protection.
2. **Report.** One sampled trip; the device computes |log dur − log dur_prior(O, D, dep)| and |log dur − log dur_fit(O, D, dep)| from the two public simulators (medians of 50 draws) and sends b = 1[fit closer] by binary RR at full ε; 1 bit. Variants: the Scheffé bit on log-likelihoods; for routes, which simulator's route has the larger edge overlap with the actual one.
3. **Privacy.** As idea 1: a binary function of the user's data, binary RR at ε, both models fixed from earlier disjoint cohorts and public data, one report.
4. **Server.** p̂ with its known SD; w = clip((p̂ − ½) / (p_max − ½), 0, 1), where p_max is the share the fitted model would win if it were exactly right (simulated); release the mixture w·P̂ + (1 − w)·P₀ over whole generators (each synthetic trip draws its component, then route, departure and segment times from it). At ε 0.5 a one-sided test (p̂ − 1.64 SD > ½) gates any w > 0.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** log(w·P̂(seq) + (1 − w)·P₀(seq)), the mixture form §4.7 already uses for C3; finite.
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** With 30 validators at ε 2 the share SD is 0.12: only a fit that is closer for ≥ 75 % of users is certified (2 SD), and the fitting cohorts shrink from 91 to 61 (SD × 1.22). ε 8: SD 0.09 (≥ 68 % certified). ε 0.5: SD 0.37, nothing is certified and the rule releases the prior, which is what the evidence supports at ε 0.5 anyway. n = 10,000: 500 validators, SD 0.03, certify 6 % improvements and can rank several fitted modules with a K-way GRR "which of K models predicts my trip best".
7. **Closest work.** Gopi et al. 2020 (LDP hypothesis selection by Scheffé comparisons, interactive); D.3's duel incumbent versus challenger (CLOSE VARIANT verdict in round 1); central-DP sample-splitting selection (Liu and Talwar 2019; Bun, Kamath, Steinke, Wu 2019). Difference: one hypothesis is fitted privately on earlier cohorts, the comparison is on a utility scalar rather than a likelihood, and the output is a mixture weight with a do-no-harm guarantee rather than a winner. Honest verdict: probably CLOSE VARIANT as a mechanism; its value is as a protocol step for S2's misspecified run and for the composite release.
8. **Risk.** At n = 91 it certifies only large gains, so at ε 2 it mostly releases the prior and erases a module's small true gains; the duration bit ignores geography (a fit can win on time and lose on OD); novelty.

## Idea 4 — Medians-then-quadrant: a coupling needs a centred model first

1. **Pitch.** Couplings between two trip quantities (departure period × duration ratio; length × speed level) are invisible to every single-round LDP protocol at n = 91: a two-dimensional histogram is unaffordable and the public prior's medians may be far off. Once an earlier cohort has centred the simulator (idea 1), a later cohort reports the quadrant of one trip relative to the model's conditional medians as one 4-way label, and the concordant share is Blomqvist's medial correlation, one parameter of a Gaussian copula that couples two marginals inside the simulator.
2. **Report.** One sampled trip; the device computes two model-conditional ranks (u₁, u₂) under the corrected simulator; label ℓ = (1[u₁ > ½], 1[u₂ > ½]) ∈ {0, 1}²; GRR over k = 4 at full ε; 2 bits.
3. **Privacy.** 4-way GRR at ε on a function of the user's data; medians and simulator from earlier disjoint cohorts; one report. Two separate bits at ε/2 would be far worse: the concordance of two ε = 1 bits behaves like one bit at ε ≈ 0.43. Rule of thumb: couplings need one categorical report, levels need cohorts.
4. **Server.** Debias the four cells; β̂ = (p₀₀ + p₁₁) − (p₀₁ + p₁₀); ρ̂ = sin(πβ̂/2); shrink to ρ = 0 by the known variance; the simulator draws the two quantities from a Gaussian copula with ρ̂ over its public marginals (slower when departing in the peak; higher speed level on longer trips), snapped to a public grid.
5. **`sequence_log_prob`.** Unchanged; the couplings involve time or length, not the edge sequence (§4.7).
6. **Estimable at n = 91.** ε 2 with 45 users: cell SD ≈ 0.07, β ± 0.20, ρ ± 0.3, so only |ρ| ≥ 0.6 is detected; ε 8: ρ ± 0.25–0.3 (sampling-dominated); ε 0.5: nothing. Three cohorts (level, re-centre, quadrant) are unaffordable, so at n = 91 the quadrant must use the public prior's medians unless cohort 1 did not saturate. n = 1,000: ρ ± 0.07 at ε 2. Benchmark blindness: P2's metrics are marginals; a coupling shows only in a joint metric (duration W1 within departure period, or a 2-D JSD), which does not exist; between-user couplings are blocked by Cond-C4. A large-n design.
7. **Closest work.** CALM (Zhang et al. 2018) and LoPub (Ren et al. 2018) estimate k-way marginals of categorical attributes under LDP with public bins; Blomqvist's β (1950) is classical; the medians come from idea 1 / Joseph et al. 2019. Difference: bins placed adaptively at model-conditional medians, so the 2 × 2 table has one free parameter and maximal sensitivity, and the estimand feeds a copula in a road-network simulator; no round-1 idea estimates any coupling (R2-A's open "joint departure × duration × distance coupling").
8. **Risk.** Gaussian-copula assumption; misplaced medians (0.6 spreads off) cut sensitivity to 70 % and bias β; invisible to the current metrics; at n = 91 it competes with idea 1 for users and loses.

## Self-ranking
1. Rank-and-correct: the one R2-D idea that pays at n = 91 (at ε ≈ 1–3, conditional on saturation) and scales to many rounds; novelty hinges on the model-conditional rank and the saturation rule against Joseph et al. 2019 and Penso et al. 2025.
2. Census-first: cheap, fixes a bias the chosen design knowingly accepts; a prerequisite, not a mechanism.
3. Do-no-harm validator, then Medians-then-quadrant: both need rounds by construction, but one is a probable close variant and the other is invisible to the current metrics.
