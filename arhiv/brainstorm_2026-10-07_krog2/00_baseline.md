## 1. Gap and constraints (plan §1)
- **Gap.** No published mechanism synthesizes whole road-graph routes with departure time and per-segment times such that the release is pure LDP at user level. Required: randomization on the device; no shuffler or secure aggregation in the core (optional extensions only); one report per collection period (e.g. a month), a later submission is a new user; the server may update incrementally; free architecture; time mandatory; road-valid by construction (every next edge exists in the public graph).
- **Must not repeat:** LDPTrace, PrivTrace, AdaTrace, DPT, DP-Star, RetraSyn, Cunningham et al., TrajLDP-style collection, DP-FedAvg-style models, non-private generators (TS-TrajGen, Diff-RNTraj, DiffTraj, ControlTraj). A combination of known pieces counts only if it solves a problem no piece solves alone.
- **Few users.** `fit` sees only the train split: u182 ≈ 91 users × ≈ 9 matched trips (trips that passed map matching) ≈ 809 trips; possibly fewer users, since only users with ≥ 1 matched trip reach `fit`. u50 ≈ 25, u20 ≈ 10. Error under pure LDP falls as 1/(ε√n), so per-edge histograms and transitions are out.
- **MIA** calls only `fit` and `sequence_log_prob(edge_seq)`, finite for every sequence; never `generate`, never times. One arm builds 17 generators (target + 16 shadows of ≈ 18 users plus candidate trips), so public computation must be cached; one attack call has 300 s at u20/u50, 1200 s at u182.
- **Missing in the repo:** timed payload (P1; `SyntheticTrajectory` carries edges or cells only) and synthetic utility metrics in the orchestrator (P2; cell JSD and length error live only in `rnldp_eval`). Synthetic `user_id`s are not needed because trips are unlinked.
- **ε grid {0.5, 2, 8} is per user.** Existing arms protect one trajectory: 9 trips at per-trip ε 0.5–2 = user-level 4.5–18; 95 trips at 0.5 ≈ 47.
- **Tools:** no deep generators (PyTorch, GPU); `rn_ldp_synth` (per-trip reports, no time) is not a template.
- **Noise cheat sheet** (round-1 evaluator, pushback-corrected; n = 91; SD of the debiased estimate):
  - One number all users answer (Duchi): 0.23 (ε 1), 0.14 (ε 2) of the half-range. HM with d publicly sampled questions, ε 0.5 / 2 / 8: d=1 0.43 / 0.11 / 0.017; d=2 0.61 / 0.15 / 0.023; d=4 0.86 / 0.21 / 0.033; d=8 1.21 / 0.30 / 0.047 (a between-user spread of 0.4 half-range lifts the ε 8 values to 0.045–0.13).
  - One categorical question all users answer (GRR): 3 shares ±0.12 (ε 1) / ±0.048 (ε 2); 9 shares ±0.19 / ±0.062. At ε 8 sampling noise √(p(1−p)/91) ≈ 0.05 dominates.
  - Formulas: GRR Var = (e^ε + k − 2)/(n(e^ε − 1)²), best for k < 3e^ε + 2; OUE/OLH Var = 4e^ε/(n(e^ε − 1)²) for any k. Splitting users into c groups multiplies every SD by √c. Lens-A answer budget (A §0) at n ≈ 90: ≈ 5 / 19 / 52 / 104 / 210 error-free yes/no answers at ε 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8, ≈ 10 answers per parameter.
  - A gain needs a prior error ≈ 2 SD: ε 0.5 shows nothing; ε 2 supports one question family (d ≤ 2 numbers or one ≤ 9-way item); ε 8 a small model (d ≤ 8). n = 5,000 buys ≈ 10–25 numbers at ε 1–2, not "100+".
  - Membership bound under user-level ε-LDP: TPR ≤ e^ε·FPR (FPR 0.01 → 0.017 at ε 0.5, 0.074 at ε 2, vacuous at ε 8).

## 2. The chosen design (plan §4): one mechanism, three modules, one public simulator
- **Public simulator (P3).** Router = Boltzmann walk toward the destination: at each node an out-edge with probability falling exponentially in its added cost; cost-to-go from one reverse Dijkstra from the destination; after a public step cap the walk finishes on the shortest path, so routes are road-valid. Demand: origin by public road-length mass, destination by gravity; departure from a public prior in Beijing time UTC+8 (uniform unless a cited diurnal profile); edge time = free-flow time (OSM `maxspeed` or class default) × public factor × jitter (AR(1) or log-normal) + public turn delay. Exact likelihood with a public floor per step (unreachable destinations, edge gaps, step cap). Cache keyed by map + config hash, shared by the 17 generators (≈ 1,100 destinations per arm). C3's catalogue members are settings of it, C2 gives it an OD matrix, C1 fits its route and time parameters.
- **Report = (module id, answer).** A public draw independent of the data picks module and question; the user answers at full ε. Every enrolled user sends exactly one fixed-size report, also without matched trips (public default). A public rule in n·ε² sets users per module and the number of questions; small n·ε² puts all users in one module. Code split: `encode_user(views, public, rng) -> Report` on the device, `server_fit(reports, public) -> params` never sees views (P4). Each module ships as its own arm, then the composite.
- **Users are split, not ε.** Answering three modules at ε/3 costs ≈ 3× the variance of a user split at ε ≤ 2, and 7.5× (numbers) to 85× (3-way label) at ε 8; three mechanisms on the same users would cost 3ε by basic composition. Result: one 3-line proof, one ε per user.
- **C3 regime vote (built first).** Device scores all its trips under K public complete generators (K = 3 at n ≈ 91: walk/cycle, urban motor, fast motor; they differ in routes, speed, departure profile, distance decay) and draws one label from its posterior (local randomness, free); GRR at full ε (OLH if K > 3e^ε + 2). Server: debias, shrink to uniform, EM removal of a confusion matrix simulated on the public graph under a public trips-per-user law. n ≈ 91: 3 shares ±0.12 (ε 1) / ±0.05 (ε 2) before deconvolution; gain = shape of duration and speed (walk vs vehicle bimodality); geography stays prior. Large n: 24–48 classes + per-regime corrections (label + 1 bit in one GRR over 2K values).
- **C2 OD shares by gravity inversion (second).** Device: shares of its trip ends over Z public zones (3 × 3 at n ≈ 91, 36–144 at n ≥ 5,000) and of its trips over 6 free-flow cost bands, 5 periods, 3 speed regimes (Z + 14 numbers). One question about one locally sampled trip end or trip: zone by OLH (prob. 0.4), band, period or regime by GRR (0.2 each). Server: T = argmin KL(T‖Q) + ½‖AT − t̂‖² weighted by V⁻¹ (Q gravity prior on a 144-zone free-flow skim; A maps T to margins), three-way Furness. n ≈ 91, ε 2: 3 × 3 shares ±0.06 (all answer) / ±0.10 (40 %); ε 1: ±0.19. No cordons. Unchecked risk: Geolife trip ends may not be concentrated relative to road-length mass.
- **C1 behavioural moments + router (third).** Boltzmann-walk variant first (exact recursive logit needs 17 private sparse factorizations, risking 300 s). Device: per matched trip a few clipped statistics (road-class shares, turns/km, detour or length, speed ratio to free flow, departure harmonics), mostly as differences to the same-endpoint fastest path; average x_u ∈ [−1, 1]^d, d = 3–8; one publicly drawn coordinate by PM/HM/Duchi (categories by GRR). Server: debias → project → shrink to θ₀ → moment-match a handful of parameters. n ≈ 91: ε 2 moves 2 numbers (speed ratio to OSM, length scale), ε 8 ≈ 8; route tastes stay prior at ε ≤ 2 and partly describe the map matcher. Large n: 10–25 numbers at ε 1–2. Novelty hinges on the unread GeoPM-DMEIRL.
- **Privacy argument (§4.6).** (1) Module and question by a public, data-independent draw; (2) the answer is a standard ε-LDP primitive (GRR, OLH, PM, HM, Duchi) on a publicly bounded function of all the user's trips, or of one trip chosen with data-independent randomness; (3) one report, so no composition; the rest is post-processing. Subtle: nothing "public" (boxes, reference speeds, zones, catalogue, question-count rule) may come from training data or from the number of users with matched trips; undefined statistic → public default; outputs snapped to a public grid; C3 sends only a label; adaptation only across disjoint cohorts; the time model is in the proof but untested by the MIA.
- **`sequence_log_prob` (§4.7).** Time does not enter. One regime: log P(O, D) + Σ log step probabilities + public gap term (P(O, D) from prior or C2; steps from prior or C1). C3: log Σ_k π̂_k · P_k(seq); how C1 corrections apply to C3 regimes is open. Reads only parameters and map/config-keyed caches; cost-to-go computed lazily for any queried destination on one code path for members and non-members; finite via the public floor.
- **Optional extensions (§4.8):** adaptive allocation at large n (later disjoint cohorts to the module with the most uncertain parameters, public rule); shuffle amplification outside the core; "spectral modes vs zones" C2 ablation if the C6 check passes; BPR congestion equilibrium for C1 at n ≈ 10,000 (needs published capacities); C9 alphabet as a C1 question family at T-Drive size.

## 6. When an unchosen candidate may return (plan §3.2, condensed; cite the condition by name)
- **Cond-C4:** only once linked synthetic users are evaluated (a user-level coherence metric exists). Relation-only reports (e.g. home–work distance class, no coordinates) are new but invisible in a benchmark of unlinked trips; anchors are undefined at ≈ 9 trips; taxis have none.
- **Cond-C5:** only as a cheap honest successor of v1, or at n ≈ 10,000 (64–256 archetypes). At n ≈ 91 it reweights 4–8 coarse trip types (length, daypart), no geography, and C3's uniform-weight arm already is "public prior + one vote".
- **Cond-C6:** one hour of computing low Laplacian eigenvectors of the public graph. If they cover the city: a "spectral modes vs zones" ablation in C2 (one session) or a replacement of C2's zones. If they sit on fringes: it is Duchi's known estimator.
- **Cond-C7:** only for non-linear knobs (major-road weight, peak hour) at n ≈ 5,000 (6–8 knobs). At n ≈ 91 it fits 1–2 knobs, often on the design boundary, and is less efficient than C1's moment matching for exponential-family parameters.
- **Cond-C8:** only with T-Drive (corridors above 2–5 % of traffic with their speeds). At n ≈ 91 there is one batch, so the descent never runs; a resampled pool has no finite likelihood; corridors need a new OSM name/ref export.
- **Cond-C9:** at n ≈ 10,000 frequent bigrams become measurable; until then the alphabet is only a possible C1 question family; without a destination pull the walks wander.
- **Cond-C10:** per-arc SD 0.4 at ε 2 against flows of 0.1–0.5, so it equals the prior below ε ≈ 4 at every Geolife rung; at most a T-Drive arm (3 × 3 zones at SD 0.06–0.09, n ≈ 5,000). Keep only the flow-conserving walk that needs no length model.
- **Baseline's note:** under round-2 settlements S2/S3 (Geolife + a recovery simulation, no T-Drive), Cond-C8 and Cond-C10 cannot be met with real data, and the n ≥ 5,000 conditions (C5, C7, C9) can be shown only in the recovery simulation.

## 7. Repo facts a mechanism designer needs (round-1 briefing)
- **Input to `fit`:** `TrajectoryView` objects wrapping `CleanTrajectory` (`traj_id`, `user_id`, `points` = (lat, lon, t unix s), `duration_s`, `length_m`, `mean_speed`, `split`) and `MatchedTrajectory` (`edge_seq` int edge ids, `matched_points` = (x, y, t, offset_m) in UTM metres, `match_score`); accessors `as_gps()`, `as_segments()`, `user_id`, `split`. Raw timestamps are therefore available "on the device". Round 1 simulated devices by grouping views by `user_id`, each with an RNG spawned from `seed`.
- **Generator ABC:** `fit(train: Sequence[TrajectoryView]) -> None` (train split only); `generate(n, seed) -> Sequence[SyntheticTrajectory]`; `sequence_log_prob(edge_seq) -> float`, finite for every edge sequence. `@register("generator", "<name>")`; constructor args filled by name: `network` = map, `seed` = cfg.seed + offset (0 target, 1000 + k shadow k); YAML parameters pass through; list values become separate arms.
- **Output:** `SyntheticTrajectory` = `syn_id`, `generator_id`, `params_hash`, untyped `payload`, `trained_on_split`, `map_id`; no `user_id`; no generator emits timestamps yet (S5; P1 adds edge ids + departure + per-edge entry times, UTC+8).
- **Map:** `RoadNetwork` = networkx `MultiDiGraph` + node/edge tables (`length_m`, `highway`, `oneway`, `maxspeed`; maxspeed coverage undocumented → class defaults), EPSG:32650; Beijing ≈ 30 × 33 km, 35,764 nodes (edge count undocumented). Not in the current tables, extractable from the same OSM snapshot as new work: road names/refs, land use, POIs. Whether footways and cycleways are in the graph is unchecked (P9). Reverse Dijkstra per destination on 36k nodes: networkx ≈ 0.4 s, scipy `csgraph` ≈ 40 ms.
- **Public prior (S1):** OSM plus cited published constants only (diurnal profiles, route-choice coefficients, mode speeds, motif shares, capacities); frozen by commit hash before any 182-user run, because the designers have already seen Geolife S4 numbers. Anything tuned on Geolife is an unprotected release.
- **Geolife v1.3:** 182 users, one trajectory per `.plt`, GMT timestamps (Beijing = UTC+8), transport-mode labels unused. Cleaning: drop points implying > 200 km/h, keep points ≥ 5 s apart, need ≥ 20 points and ≥ 500 m. 17,313 clean trajectories (≈ 95 per user); only 1,770 pass match score 0.3; generators train on ≈ 809. Split once by user, seed 42: train 0.5 / test 0.2 / shadow 0.2 / attack 0.1. Per-user trip counts and time spans are undocumented. Porto (LDPTrace `.dat`): 367,008 one-trajectory users, no map, no real timestamps.
- **MIA (LiRA-lite), synthetic only:** candidate = one trajectory; members = train trajectories, non-members = test trajectories of other users; 16 shadows each fit a random half of the shadow split plus candidates; score = likelihood ratio of the candidate's `sequence_log_prob` under shadows with vs without it; AUC, TPR at FPR 0.001/0.01/0.1. Members/non-members 90/15 (u20), 130/33 (u50), 809/296 (u182); FPR 0.001 is NaN below 1,000 non-members. Whether candidates enter shadows as one-trajectory users or under their own `user_id` is unverified (P0). The proposed pass mark TPR ≤ 0.02 at FPR 0.01 is guaranteed by user-level ε only for ε ≤ ln 2.
- **Reference numbers:** non-private `markov` ceiling u182 AUC 0.776, TPR@0.01 0.027; u50 AUC 0.542. `rn_ldp_synth` u182 AUC 0.513 / 0.517 / 0.472 at ε 0.5 / 2 / 8. Runtime: u182 membership run 101–161 s per seed; per arm at u20 `rn_ldp_synth` 20 s, `ldptrace` 10 s, `privtrace` 8 s.
- **Reidentification** (k = 3/5/10 known points, DTW) runs on perturbation mechanisms only, never on synthetic data.
- **Utility:** today only paired raw-vs-release metrics for perturbation (`cell_js_divergence` on 20 × 20 cells; length, duration and speed W1). P2 adds unpaired synthetic metrics: cell JSD, length W1, duration W1, mean-speed W1, circular departure-hour W1, 3 × 3 OD JSD, a user-weighted option, held-out test users as reference, bootstrap intervals. Gain = share of the prior-to-oracle gap recovered; metrics where even the oracle does not beat the prior are dropped.
- **Large-n evidence (S2, P6):** parameter-recovery simulation with known parameters, n = 10, 25, 91, 300, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000, ε ∈ {0.5, 2, 8}, ≥ 20 seeds per cell, same `encode_user`/`server_fit` code; measures per-parameter error, interval coverage, parameters leaving the prior, gap share on the utility metrics; plus one misspecified run (simulate C3, fit C1).
- **Existing mechanisms (units not comparable):** `geo_indistinguishability` (ε per 100 m, per point), `point_ldp` (per point, RR over 20 × 20 cells), `ldptrace` (per trajectory, 12 × 12 cells), `privtrace` (central, per trajectory, adaptive 6 × 6), `rn_ldp_synth` (per trajectory: start zone, end zone, transition count ≤ 24, one zone transition; ε shares 0.15/0.15/0.2/0.5; 12 × 12 zones; no time), `markov` (none). None protects whole users.
- **Engineering:** Python 3.11+, numpy with seeded `np.random.Generator` only, Parquet + DuckDB, PyYAML, ruff, mypy; every new dependency needs a one-line justification; no PyTorch/GPU. Tests offline in seconds on committed fixtures (20 synthetic Geolife-format trajectories from 5 users, 183-node / 388-edge map).
- **Privacy kit (P4) every new mechanism inherits:** `encode_user`/`server_fit` split; tests: (1) params unchanged when views change but reports are fixed; (2) randomizer audit, empirical log-ratio ≤ ε; (3) canary user with an extreme trip; (4) `sequence_log_prob` reads only params and map/config-keyed caches; plus one deliberately leaky arm as a positive control.

## 8. Direction map for the round-2 lenses (baseline's reading; "open" = not covered in round 1, novelty unchecked)
- **R2-A Time-first: touched only as marginals on an independently drawn route.**
  - Round 1: departure harmonics, von Mises or circular SW densities (A.1, A.4, C.1, D.1, D.2, E.1); period shares (B.2, E.3, C2); period × road-class speed factors (C.1, E.3, B's public δ[class, period]); hour-bin cordons with a time-dependent logit (A.3); leave/return times, commute daypart, away duration, commute days per week as C4 slots (B.3, D.4, E.4); per-regime departure profiles (C3, D.3). Chosen design: departure prior + free-flow time × factor × AR(1) jitter.
  - Open: dwell and stay durations as released quantities (D.4 used stays only to find anchors); trip frequency and gaps between trips (C3 assumes a public trips-per-user law); weekly rhythm beyond one weekday share; joint departure × duration × distance coupling; space derived from time (space–time prisms on the public network); departure-time-conditioned routing; time-dependent segment times from cited constants.
  - Neighbours: TimeGeo 2016 (non-private, per-person temporal parameters; seen only in C4's novelty check), DP-WHERE 2013 (central, calling-time distributions), MTNet 2022 and PUTS 2023 (central, travel times). The MIA sees no time: gains must show on P2's duration, speed and departure-hour W1, and an edge-sequence likelihood is still required (e.g. time marginalised out).
- **R2-B Whole synthetic users: partly covered by C4 and blocked by Cond-C4.**
  - Round 1: B.3 (home→work→home tours), D.4 (relation classes on public land-use densities; legs emitted unlinked, "a linked variant is one flag"), E.4 (programs executed as a synthetic week). Deferred because unlinked-trip metrics cannot see coherent users, anchors are undefined at ≈ 9 matched trips and Geolife is not a commuter population.
  - Open: anchor-free user structure (habitual repetition of OD pairs or routes, number of distinct places, exploration vs return); per-user heterogeneity as an object (C.2's per-user regime weights w_u stayed on the device); tours or chains without home/work semantics; a user-level coherence metric (needed for Cond-C4); how a whole-user model yields a per-trip `sequence_log_prob` (the MIA candidate is one trajectory).
  - Neighbours: DP-WHERE, OnTheMap, Badu-Marfo et al. 2020, Berke et al. 2022, TimeGeo, MATSim/ActivitySim, LoPub.
- **R2-C One trip per user: the sampling argument is everywhere, the payload was always tiny.**
  - Round 1: "one trip chosen with data-independent local randomness keeps user-level ε" is used by B.2/C2 (one trip end's zone, one trip's band/period/regime), D.1 (the archetype of one whole timed trip; rejected as C5), A.4 (one length-uniform point's node + speed bit), E.2 (one move's bigram), B.1/E.3 (one trip's period, regime, O/D zone). Trip weighting: lens-A rule (report min(m_u, M)/M × value, ratio estimator) and B.2's padding-and-sampling with a public cap; user- vs trip-weighted reference left as an open decision. No round-1 idea releases or copies a route: all routes come from public routing between sampled endpoints.
  - Open: one sampled trip carrying its route, departure and duration through one randomizer over a public route space (candidate paths per OD, route encodings, randomized time bins); explicit, variance-accounted correction for heavy vs light users; sampling a day or a sub-path; the joint (O, D, departure, duration) of one trip instead of one marginal.
  - Hazard: per-trajectory LDP is the most crowded area (LDPTrace, RetraSyn, L-SRR, Cunningham et al., TraCS, DPMM, GeoPM-DMEIRL), so "sample one trip, then a known per-trip mechanism" invites a CLOSE VARIANT verdict; at n ≈ 91 one ≤ 9-way item is the most one question resolves.
- **R2-D Interactive multi-round: only large-n add-ons so far.**
  - Round 1: D.3 (later windows vote on the most uncertain factor or duel incumbent vs public challenger), C.4 → C7 (later cohorts near θ̂; LDP Bayesian optimization, CLOSE VARIANT), A.4 → C8 (AHEAD-style batch descent, never runs at n ≈ 91), C.3 → C5 (refined libraries), B.1 (optional re-scoring at θ̂), E.4 (PEM prefix groups at n ≈ 10k), A.1 (adaptive centre frozen before its batch), plan §4.8 (adaptive module allocation at large n). Consensus: n ≈ 91 is one batch.
  - Open: a protocol built for small n with 2–3 cohorts (≈ 45 or 30 users, every SD × √2 or × √3) whose adaptive second question (time bins, candidate route or OD sets, a centring reference, a codebook) gains more than the √c cost; sequential design with exactly known LDP noise; adaptive centring (cohort 1 estimates the reference, cohort 2 reports residuals).
  - Neighbours: AHEAD 2021, PEM, Gopi et al. 2020 (interactive rounds), Kamath et al. 2025, Zhou & Tan 2021, Ding et al. 2018, PE/PrE-Text (multi-round, central or distributed), Yang et al. 2020 (hot-path trie). Lens-D warning: cross-window adaptivity holds only if each window is a new user; a linked device pays T·ε.
- **R2-E Local synthesis with a compressed descriptor: the most covered lens.**
  - Round 1: one label from the posterior over a public catalogue (C3: B.4, C.2, D.3); the nearest public archetype or library-tree node (C5: D.1, C.3); program slots or prefixes (E.4 → C4); one clipped likelihood-gain scalar (C.4 → C7); score coordinates as compressed sufficient statistics (B.1, C.1 → C1). All codebooks were public (map-only simulated libraries, frozen and hashed).
  - Open: fitting a small model on the device and releasing its code or a few parameters with a road-valid decoder and an exact likelihood; one synthetic route per user through an LDP randomizer over route space; latent codes obtained by inverting a public simulator on the device; sketches of personal transition structure; codebook size set by a rate–distortion rule in n·ε².
  - Hazards: Private Evolution / Sim-PE / PrE-Text (vote for the nearest public sample) and C5's CLOSE VARIANT verdict; one-round LDP k-means (Chang et al. 2021); Gopi et al. 2020.
- **Summary.** Most covered: R2-E (C3 chosen, C5 rejected, E.4) and R2-B (all of C4, deferred). Partly: R2-C (sampling used everywhere, never with a whole-trip payload). Most open: R2-A (time as the primary object) and R2-D (adaptive cohorts designed for n ≈ 91).

## 9. Lessons from the round-1 evaluation: what sank candidates
1. Novelty is a gate: a CLOSE VARIANT (published work differs in one named detail) cannot be chosen whatever its score. "Noise moved onto the device", "one round instead of many", "a new alphabet" each counted as one detail (C5, C7, C9, C10; C3's duel form).
2. Five-way convergence is a warning, not a strength: C1 was the obvious construction, had to be searched hardest, and still hinges on one unread paper.
3. Noise at n ≈ 91 decides gain: novelty that only appears at n ≥ 5,000 (C8's descent, C10's flows, C9's bigrams) scored 1–2; authors who computed with n ≈ 180 were √2 too optimistic.
4. Route tastes at n ≈ 91 partly describe the map matcher (which favours shortest paths), so route-moment gains are a large-n claim only.
5. The benchmark is blind to user coherence (C4 deferred) and to time (MIA sees edges only); privacy does not rank candidates (all at chance; TPR ≤ e^ε·FPR). Evidence = gain over the candidate's own prior arm on time-aware metrics + structural privacy tests.
6. Fit penalties: no natural finite likelihood (C8's pool resampling, E.4's surrogate) and private-parameter factorizations × 17 (exact RL) that threaten the 300 s budget.
7. Prior contamination: any constant, zone, band, catalogue member or decay chosen after seeing Geolife is an unprotected release; freeze by commit hash.
8. Membership leaks enter through the one non-public factor of a public likelihood: train-derived within-zone node mass (C2), familiarity costs or a cost-to-go cache filled only for training destinations (C1), a train-fitted catalogue member (C3), a data-derived support floor (C6).
9. Fragile primitives cost points: PCKV budget split (C8), exact ℓ2-ball sphere sampling at dimension 69–2,000 (C10), fringe-localized eigenvectors (C6); duplicating C1's few numbers with a weaker generator (C9) adds no axis.
10. Winning profile: a small public question family that moves 1–2 numbers or one ≤ 9-way categorical at ε 2 where the public prior is badly wrong (speed level / mode mix, trip-length scale, 3 × 3 trip ends, maybe the departure profile).
