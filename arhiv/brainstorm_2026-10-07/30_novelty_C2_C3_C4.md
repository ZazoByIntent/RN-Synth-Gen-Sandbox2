# Novelty check: C2, C3, C4

Searched on 7 Oct 2026. The candidates are as defined in `20_candidates.md`.

**How the search went.** I ran about 40 web searches, more than the ~8 per candidate the task
allows: about 19 for C2 (several of them cover the shared direction "OD matrix plus public
routing", which also bears on C4), 9 for C3 and 12 for C4, plus page fetches. No PDF could be
opened in this environment. The MDPI page returned "403 Forbidden". Two university thesis pages
(Padova, Sabancı) redirected to a network access-check page, which I did not follow. Several
details therefore rest on abstracts and search snippets, and every such case is flagged. Details
marked "recalled" come from my prior knowledge of the paper and were not re-checked today.

**Abbreviations.** DP = differential privacy with a trusted curator who adds the noise ("central
DP"). LDP = local differential privacy (each user randomizes on the device). OD =
origin–destination. RR = randomized response; GRR / k-RR = its k-ary version; OLH = optimized
local hashing. KL = Kullback–Leibler divergence. GLS = generalized least squares. PE = Private
Evolution. EM = expectation–maximization.

**Cross-cutting note.** The theory of user-level LDP (each user holds many observations and
protects all of them at once) is developed in A. Kent, T. B. Berrett, Y. Yu, "Rate optimality and
phase transition for user-level local differential privacy", arXiv 2405.11923 (2024, revised
2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11923 . None of the closest works below is user-level LDP:
L-SRR, LDPTrace and DPMM protect one trip or one trajectory.

## C2: Gravity-regularized OD inversion from LDP margins or cordon counts

### Closest published works

1. **H. Wang, H. Hong, L. Xiong, Z. Qin, Y. Hong — L-SRR, ACM CCS 2022.**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15091
   - *What it does:* LDP collection of locations over a grid with a "staircase" randomized
     response. Its OD extension has each user perturb the origin and the destination of a trip
     with ε/2 each, and the server estimates the frequencies of all d² OD pairs with a
     Lasso-regression-based "private matching" step (known from the search snippet; full text not
     read).
   - *How C2 differs:* this is the colliding piece: LDP OD estimation with a regularized inversion
     already exists. C2 differs in what it sends and what it regularizes toward. Each user sends one
     margin item (trip-end zone, cost band, period or speed regime) at full ε instead of both
     endpoints at ε/2. The server pulls the estimate toward a road-network gravity prior with 2–4
     free parameters (KL penalty) instead of applying a sparsity (L1) penalty over d² pairs.
     L-SRR has no road network, no time and no user-level unit.

2. **Y. Du, Y. Hu, Z. Zhang, Z. Fang, L. Chen, B. Zheng, Y. Gao — LDPTrace, PVLDB 2023.**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06180
   - *What it does:* per-trajectory LDP synthesis on an adaptive grid. Two of its three locally
     collected patterns are the start-cell and end-cell distributions; the third is transitions,
     and a length distribution is also used (named from the paper body; the abstract does not list
     them). Trajectories are generated by a walk on the grid.
   - *How C2 differs:* LDPTrace's start and end distributions are separate margins with no
     coupling. C2 couples them through a distance-decay (gravity) model on a free-flow travel-cost
     matrix of the road graph, and generates paths by public routing with timing.

3. **DistOD — *Electronics* 13(22):4545, MDPI 2024 (authors not verified, page returned 403).**
   https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/13/22/4545
   - *What it does:* hybrid OD-matrix computation across several parties without a trusted
     server: distributed DP (noise added jointly by the parties) in hotspot zones and LDP elsewhere.
   - *How C2 differs:* its utility comes from distributed DP among parties, which the pure-LDP rule
     excludes. It has no gravity prior and no trajectory synthesis.

4. **F. Boninsegna, F. Silvestri — Differentially private release of hierarchical
   origin/destination data with a TopDown approach, PoPETs 2025.**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09256 , https://petsymposium.org/popets/2025/popets-2025-0087.php
   - *What it does:* central-DP OD tables over a region → province → city hierarchy, built on the
     US Census TopDown algorithm. Noisy counts are post-processed by constrained optimization so
     that the levels agree.
   - *How C2 differs:* it shows that "noisy OD counts plus structured post-processing" is published
     under central DP. There, the structure is a geographic hierarchy, not a gravity model, and
     nothing is routed. (Related, also central DP: S. Shaham, G. Ghinita, C. Shahabi, OD matrices
     with intermediate stops, arXiv 2202.12342, 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12342 .)

5. **J. Sakong, A. K. Zentefis — A simulation-based method to estimating economic models with
   privacy-protected data, NBER book chapter.**
   https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/data-privacy-protection-and-conduct-applied-research-methods-approaches-and-new-findings/simulation-based-method-estimating-economic-models-privacy-protected-data
   - *What it does:* fits a gravity model of consumer visits to DP-protected mobile-device data.
     It uses the method of simulated moments (matching simulated and observed summary statistics)
     to correct the bias that the DP noise causes.
   - *How C2 differs:* this is the nearest published "gravity model fitted to privatized flows".
     However, its inputs are a central-DP release and it synthesizes nothing. C2 does the
     analogous fit on LDP margins, whose noise covariance is known exactly.

6. **A. Haydari, C.-N. Chuah, M. Zhang, J. Macfarlane, S. Peisert — Differentially private map
   matching (DPMM), ACSAC 2022.**
   https://its.berkeley.edu/node/5589 , https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~peisert/research/2022-DPMM-ACSAC.pdf
   - *What it does:* adds two-dimensional (planar) Laplace noise to each trip's origin and
     destination GPS points. It then builds the travel path with an exponential mechanism (a DP
     way of picking one option with probability rising with its score) and outputs link-level road
     trajectories.
   - *How C2 differs:* this is the closest "private OD plus road routing" synthesis, but it works
     per trajectory (noise on each trip's points), builds no aggregated OD model and has no time
     component. C2 aggregates one user-level LDP report per user into a model and routes with a
     public logit.

*Also seen (context, not closer):*
- G. Qin, S. Deng, Q. Luo, J. Sun, Multimodal traffic assignment from privacy-protected OD data,
  *Communications in Transportation Research* 5:100223 (2025): trip-level DP noise inside a
  chance-constrained traffic assignment.
  https://www.sciopen.com/article/10.1016/j.commtr.2025.100223
- R. Dong, W. Krichene, A. Bayen et al. (2015): DP of the map from OD flows to link flows in
  routing games. https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04041
- Z. Zhang, T. Wang, N. Li, S. He, J. Chen, CALM, CCS 2018: LDP marginal release. Users are split
  across marginals, and the server makes the marginals consistent and reconstructs the rest (the
  maximum-entropy reconstruction step is recalled, not verified). https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10098306
- A central-DP mobility-matrix release from call records for humanitarian response (Scientific
  Reports 2024; authors recalled as Kohli, Aiken, Blumenstock).
  https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10555864-privacy-guarantees-personal-mobility-data-humanitarian-response
- The server-side estimators C2 uses are classical and non-private: Wilson (1967) entropy
  gravity, Evans and Kirby (1974) tri-proportional fitting, Van Zuylen and Willumsen (1980)
  entropy OD estimation from counts with a prior matrix, and Cascetta (1984) GLS from counts. I
  checked no URLs for these.

### Verdict: NOVEL COMBINATION

All the pieces are published:
- LDP OD collection with a regularized inversion (L-SRR);
- LDP start and end distributions feeding a synthesizer (LDPTrace);
- gravity fitting to privatized data (Sakong and Zentefis, central DP);
- private OD plus road routing (DPMM, per trajectory);
- the inversion itself (Van Zuylen and Willumsen; Cascetta).

I found no work that inverts user-level pure-LDP margins, or LDP cordon bits, through a gravity or
entropy prior. I also found none that uses such an OD to drive road-network timed synthesis. The
combination solves a problem neither side solves. L-SRR-style estimation of d² OD pairs is
hopeless at n ≈ 91 users, while a gravity prior alone uses no private data. Together they give
2–4 measurable departures from the prior plus road-valid timed paths.

Judged alone, the estimation layer is a close variant of L-SRR. The differing detail is a KL pull
toward a gravity prior over margins in place of an L1 penalty over joint pairs. The contribution
is therefore thin, as `20_candidates.md` already says.

### Confidence: medium

Not verified:
- the full OD section of L-SRR (the Lasso step is known only from the snippet);
- DistOD's authors and design;
- DPMM details beyond the abstract.

Transport journals (Transportation Research B/C) may hold privacy-preserving OD-from-counts work
that my queries did not surface.

The cordon variant (A.3) partly overlaps a family of ε-DP "point-to-point traffic measurement"
schemes. In those schemes, vehicles randomize their encounters with roadside units into bit arrays,
which is close to LDP crossing counts (e.g. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10177934 ; title
and authors not verified). Check this family before claiming LDP cordon counts as new.

### Must-cite list
- L-SRR (Wang et al., CCS 2022).
- LDPTrace (Du et al., PVLDB 2023).
- Van Zuylen and Willumsen (1980), with Wilson (1967), for the entropy / gravity inversion.
- DPMM (Haydari et al., ACSAC 2022), for private OD plus road routing.

## C3: Label vote over a public behaviour catalogue

### Closest published works

1. **S. Gopi, G. Kamath, J. Kulkarni, A. Nikolov, Z. S. Wu, H. Zhang — Locally private
   hypothesis selection, COLT 2020.**
   https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/gopi20a.html , https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09465
   - *What it does:* the input is k public candidate distributions and LDP-randomized samples
     from an unknown distribution. The output is one candidate whose total-variation distance (a
     standard distance between distributions) to the truth is close to the best possible. Under
     LDP it needs about k samples, against about log k centrally. It runs in a few interactive
     rounds, or non-interactively for k-wise testing. It is built on pairwise Scheffé
     comparisons: each comparison asks on which side of the region where one candidate's density
     exceeds the other's a sample falls.
   - *How C3 differs:* this collides with D.3's pairwise duel. A user answering "which of two
     public generators explains my trips better" through binary RR is an LDP Scheffé comparison.
     The differing detail is that the duel bit is the sign of the user's summed log-likelihood
     ratio over all their trips (user-level), not whether one sample lies in a set. B.4 and C.2
     differ more: they estimate soft mixture weights over the catalogue and synthesize from the
     mixture, while hypothesis selection returns a single winner.

2. **G. Kamath, A. F. Pour, M. Regehr, D. P. Woodruff — Query-efficient locally private
   hypothesis selection via the Scheffé graph, arXiv 2509.16180, 2025.**
   https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16180v1
   - *What it does:* non-interactive LDP hypothesis selection with about k^(3/2) queries. It still
     outputs one nearly-closest candidate and explicitly estimates no mixture.
   - *How C3 differs:* as for the entry above. It confirms that the LDP selection line is active
     and has not moved to mixture weights over generative models.

3. **Z. Lin, S. Gopi, J. Kulkarni, H. Nori, S. Yekhanin — Private Evolution (DP synthetic data via
   foundation-model APIs), ICLR 2024; Z. Lin, T. Baltrusaitis, W. Wang, S. Yekhanin — Sim-PE (APIs
   3: using simulators instead of foundation models), ICLR 2025 workshop.**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15560 , https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05505
   - *What it does:* each private record votes for its nearest sample from a public generator. The
     vote histogram receives Gaussian noise (central DP) and drives the next generation. Sim-PE
     uses parametric simulators as the public generator.
   - *How C3 differs:* this is the closest "vote over public generators" scheme. However, it uses
     central DP with a trusted aggregator, reuses the private data over many iterations, and votes
     for samples rather than for a generator. C3 is one-shot and pure LDP, and each user votes for
     a generator (or a factor level) using all their trips.

4. **A. Chang, B. Ghazi, R. Kumar, P. Manurangsi — Locally private k-means in one round, ICML 2021.**
   https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/chang21a.html
   - *What it does:* one-round LDP clustering. Users report through frequency oracles over a
     public bucketing structure, and the server finds the centres (mechanism recalled; the
     abstract confirms only one-round LDP k-means).
   - *How C3 differs:* "report one label from a public codebook" is the same primitive. However,
     the codebook is geometric, no mixture likelihood is released and nothing is synthesized.

5. **W. H. Greene, D. A. Hensher — A latent class model for discrete choice analysis,
   Transportation Research Part B, 2003 (non-private; no URL checked).**
   - *What it does:* treats a population as a finite mixture of behavioural classes and estimates
     their shares and coefficients jointly.
   - *How C3 differs:* C3 is a privatized latent-class model in which the classes are fixed in
     advance from public knowledge and only the shares are estimated. Its behavioural basis is
     therefore standard.

*Also seen:*
- DP-Hype (arXiv 2510.04902, 2025): federated hyperparameter search in which clients vote under
  DP. The abstract does not make the trust model clear (local randomization or secure
  aggregation). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04902
- "Canaries in the bank: auditing user-level privacy in Private Evolution" (arXiv 2609.13499,
  2026) audits user-level PE; it is still central DP. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13499

### Verdict: NOVEL COMBINATION

The primitives are textbook: one label through GRR, debiasing, and EM deconvolution of a known
confusion matrix. Choosing among public hypotheses under LDP is also published (Gopi et al. 2020;
Kamath et al. 2025). I found no work that estimates mixture weights over a public catalogue of
complete generative trajectory models from one user-level LDP label. I also found none that uses
the result as a road-network timed synthesizer with a closed-form mixture likelihood.

Taken alone, the D.3 duel sub-variant is a CLOSE VARIANT of LDP hypothesis selection. The
differing detail is the sign of a user-level log-likelihood ratio instead of whether one sample
lies in a Scheffé set.

### Confidence: medium

Not verified:
- whether the statistics literature has LDP estimation of mixing proportions with known
  components under another name (one targeted query found nothing);
- whether federated Private Evolution (PrE-Text, Hou et al., ICML 2024) has a pure-LDP variant
  (none surfaced);
- DP-Hype's trust model.

### Must-cite list
- Gopi et al., Locally private hypothesis selection (COLT 2020), with Kamath et al. (2025).
- Lin et al., Private Evolution (ICLR 2024), with Sim-PE (2025).
- Greene and Hensher (2003), latent-class choice.

## C4: Activity anchors and schedules

### Closest published works

1. **D. J. Mir, S. Isaacman, R. Cáceres, M. Martonosi, R. N. Wright — DP-WHERE: differentially
   private modeling of human mobility, IEEE BigData 2013.**
   https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/dp-where-differentially-private-modeling-of-human-mobility/
   - *What it does:* adds DP noise to the distributions of the WHERE model, which is built from
     call detail records, and generates synthetic call records for a synthetic population. The
     components (home location, commute distance, work location at that distance, call-time
     profiles) are recalled from the paper; the abstract does not list them.
   - *How C4 differs:* this is the closest structural counterpart (anchor-based synthetic people
     under DP). However, DP is central at the phone operator, locations are cell towers rather than
     road paths, and there is no per-segment timing. C4 moves collection to user-level pure LDP
     with one slot per user and routes every leg on the road graph.

2. **A. Machanavajjhala, D. Kifer, J. Abowd, J. Gehrke, L. Vilhuber — Privacy: theory meets
   practice on the map (OnTheMap), ICDE 2008.**
   https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/privacy-theory-meets-practice-on-the-map/
   - *What it does:* formally private synthetic commuting data for the US Census mapping tool.
     Synthetic home blocks are drawn for each workplace block from a prior-smoothed model
     (mechanism details recalled).
   - *How C4 differs:* the guarantee is central and covers administrative records, and only
     home–work pairs are released, with no schedules or routes. C4's D.4 "relation classes
     attached to public densities" is a local analogue of prior-smoothed commute synthesis.

3. **G. Badu-Marfo, B. Farooq, Z. Patterson — A differentially private multi-output deep
   generative networks approach for activity diary synthesis, arXiv 2012.14574, 2020 (journal
   version not verified).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14574
   - *What it does:* a generative adversarial network (GAN) that synthesizes socio-economic
     attributes plus spatially and temporally explicit activity sequences from a travel survey. The
     trusted data holder applies DP during training.
   - *How C4 differs:* it uses central DP and a deep model, with no road routing. C4 collects a
     low-dimensional parametric schedule under LDP.

4. **S. Jiang, Y. Yang, S. Gupta, D. Veneziano, S. Athavale, M. C. González — The TimeGeo modeling
   framework for urban mobility without travel surveys, PNAS 2016.**
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5027456 , https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/108728
   - *What it does:* a non-private mechanistic generator built on inferred home and work anchors.
     It uses three temporal parameters per person (weekly home-based tour number, dwell rate,
     burst rate) and rank-based exploration and return for other activities.
   - *How C4 differs:* it shows that "anchors plus a few per-person parameters" is a standard
     generator. C4 privatizes such parameters per user under LDP and adds road-graph routing with
     timed edges.

5. **X. Ren, C.-M. Yu, W. Yu, S. Yang, X. Yang, J. A. McCann, P. S. Yu — LoPub, IEEE TIFS 2018.**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.04350
   - *What it does:* LDP publication of high-dimensional records. Attributes are collected under
     LDP, their correlations are estimated, and synthetic records are generated from the estimated
     model.
   - *How C4 differs:* this is the generic LDP pattern C4 relies on: one publicly assigned slot per
     user, debiased marginals, then recombination. C4 applies it to activity attributes with public
     spatial priors, so its privacy layer is not new.

*Also seen:*
- A. Berke, R. Doorley, K. Larson, E. Moro, ACM SAC 2022: RNN-generated stay sequences for a
  census-seeded synthetic population. https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01139 . According to its
  abstract it gives no formal DP guarantee, which corrects the "central-DP" label in
  `20_candidates.md`.
- Cunningham et al. (VLDB 2021, a listed neighbour) perturbs time-annotated place n-grams with
  public points-of-interest knowledge. It works per trajectory and is place-based, not
  anchor-based.
- Apart from L-SRR and DistOD (see C2), I found no LDP collection of commute-like data, and none
  of home or work anchors or of schedules.

### Verdict: NOVEL COMBINATION

Each piece is published:
- anchor-based synthetic populations under central DP (DP-WHERE, OnTheMap);
- central-DP activity-diary synthesis (Badu-Marfo et al.);
- anchor-based mechanistic generators (TimeGeo);
- the LDP pattern of collecting one attribute per user and recombining (LoPub; CALM).

I found no work that collects activity anchors or schedule parameters under user-level pure LDP.
I also found none that turns them into road-routed legs with per-segment timing. The combination
solves a problem neither side solves: central anchor models need a trusted operator or a census,
and generic LDP tabular synthesis knows nothing about anchors, routes or time. The most distinctive
detail is D.4's relation-only reports, in which no coordinates are ever sent; I found it nowhere.

### Confidence: medium

Not verified:
- the exact mechanisms of DP-WHERE and OnTheMap (recalled; abstracts only);
- the journal version of Badu-Marfo et al.;
- whether transport venues contain randomized-response travel-survey work on home/work or trip
  purposes (two queries found none);
- whether industry LDP deployments that touch "significant locations" are documented in papers.

### Must-cite list
- DP-WHERE (Mir et al., IEEE BigData 2013).
- OnTheMap (Machanavajjhala et al., ICDE 2008).
- Badu-Marfo, Farooq and Patterson (2020).
- TimeGeo (Jiang et al., PNAS 2016).
